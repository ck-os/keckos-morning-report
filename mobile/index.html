THIS MORNING · Premarket

Chips Rebound, Oil Slides on Russia Sanctions Waiver

Tech is bouncing back after last week's chip rout -- Micron (+2.83%), Broadcom (+2.71%) and Intel (+4.13%) are leading -- while a Trump sanctions waiver on Russian oil is pulling crude down nearly 3%. Cheaper oil is helping cool the inflation outlook even as the 10-year yield ticks up slightly, and Tech's 0.558-point contribution is doing more for the S&P than any other sector today.

Top Story Chip Stocks Rebound After Last Week's Selloff Why Micron, Broadcom and Intel are bouncing after a rough stretch -- Micron's forecast had been dragged down by Taiwan strike-authorization risk and Chinese rival CXMT's memory ramp, per TradingKey, but CXMT itself fell nearly 8% this morning even as it posted a profit turnaround, easing some of that competitive overhang, per CNBC. Morningstar notes investors are also rotating into value as a counterweight ahead of this week's Big Tech earnings. Technology is 40.5% of the S&P and up 1.38% -- the single biggest contributor at +0.558 points, explaining more of today's move than the index's entire +0.51% gain.
Also Moving Oil Drops Nearly 3% on Russia Sanctions Waiver Why Crude is sliding after Reuters and AP reported Trump granted a waiver on Russian oil sanctions, easing supply-tightness fears tied to the conflict. Energy is only 3.4% of the S&P, so oil's slide barely dents the index (-0.017 pts) -- but cheaper crude feeds into a softer inflation read, which is part of why the 10-year yield rose just 0.18% instead of more.
Worth Knowing French Unrest Deepens a Debt-Crisis Worry Why Over 500,000 protested in France over the weekend, turning violent, as CNBC reports an analyst framed it bluntly: "either the bond markets riot, or the people riot, or both." MarketWatch separately draws a parallel between French and U.S. debt dynamics. Not in U.S. prices yet -- France's CAC is down over 1% and the DAX and FTSE are both lower, but it hasn't shown up in S&P futures; worth watching if French bond yields keep climbing.
Why It’s Happening

Why are chip stocks bouncing back today?

The trigger
Micron (+2.83%), Broadcom (+2.71%), Intel (+4.13%) and Teradyne (+3.78%) are all rebounding after last week's slide, per Morningstar's "Markets Brief: Chip Stocks Sell Off, Investors Rotate Into Value." Separately, China's CXMT -- the Chinese memory-chip maker whose ramp had pressured Micron -- saw its own shares fall nearly 8% this morning despite posting a profit turnaround, per CNBC.
Why it hits these stocks
Micron's slide had been driven by two named threats -- CXMT's expanding server-memory output competing for the same customers, and a Taiwan union voting 99% to authorize a strike, per The Motley Fool and TradingKey. CXMT's own stock stumbling on its earnings outlook, even with profits improving, suggests the market isn't pricing Chinese memory competition as cleanly dominant as feared last week, giving Micron and peers room to recover.
Who’s saying what
  • TradingKey Called out CXMT and Taiwan labor risk as the two forces dragging Micron toward $856 support despite 'record AI demand.'
  • The Motley Fool Flagged the Taiwan union's 99% strike-authorization vote as a fresh headwind for Micron.
  • CNBC Reported CXMT shares fell nearly 8% even after the company's profit turnaround, as investors 'look past' the improving numbers.
  • Morningstar Framed today's move as a rotation into value names alongside the chip bounce, with Big Tech earnings this week as the next test.
The other side
Micron's stock is still down a reported 29% from its peak, per ad-hoc-news.de, so today's bounce could be a reprieve within an ongoing slide rather than a reversal.
What settles it
Intel reports results October 29 and Teradyne on October 27 -- those prints, plus Big Tech earnings later this week, will show whether today's rebound has real legs.
