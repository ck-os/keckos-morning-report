Micron and Broadcom Lead as AI Trade Steadies; Microsoft Slips Ahead of Earnings

Memory and networking chip names are climbing on fresh AI-infrastructure spending news, while Microsoft's pre-earnings dip is the single biggest drag on the index -- a reminder that one stock's 5.8% weight can outmuscle an otherwise calm tape. The S&P is barely moving (+0.06%) because these forces are mostly canceling out, not because nothing's happening.

Top Story Micron, Broadcom Gain as Anthropic's $450B Compute Deal Lands Why TipRanks and Stocktwits report Micron, SanDisk and SK Hynix are rising premarket after a filing showed Anthropic's compute commitments reaching $450 billion, fueling demand expectations for memory and AI chips; KeyBanc separately reiterated an Overweight on Broadcom citing AI chip wins. Micron alone is adding 0.032 pts to the S&P (53% of the index's entire move) and Broadcom another 0.029 pts (48%) -- two chip names are doing more work than the whole Energy sector combined.
Also Moving Microsoft Slides Into Earnings on Buyout Offer to 60,000 Staff Why Microsoft is down 0.68% premarket after reports (Coinpaper, BeInCrypto) that it offered voluntary severance to 60,000 employees just days before its earnings report, even as AI spending climbs -- Yahoo Finance frames tomorrow's print as the market's next real test. Worth Knowing Morgan Stanley Warns Chip Stocks Could Fall Another 15% Why BigGo Finance reports Morgan Stanley sees the semiconductor correction continuing into mid-November before bottoming, even as Micron and Broadcom rally this morning -- a reminder the AI-chip story has real disagreement behind it. Not showing up in prices yet -- Technology is still 40.5% of the S&P, so a further chip slide would hit far harder than today's modest +0.01% sector move suggests.
Why It’s Happening

Why are memory and AI chip stocks rising today?

The trigger
TipRanks and Stocktwits report Micron, SanDisk and SK Hynix are rising premarket after a filing revealed Anthropic's compute commitments total $450 billion; separately, KeyBanc reiterated an Overweight rating on Broadcom citing AI chip wins, per Investing.com.
Why it hits these stocks
Micron's highest-margin business is server memory sold into AI data centers -- a massive compute commitment from a major AI lab signals sustained demand for that memory, while Broadcom's custom AI chips and networking gear sell directly into the same build-out.
Who’s saying what
  • KeyBanc (per Investing.com) Reiterated an Overweight rating on Broadcom, citing recent AI chip wins.
  • Seeking Alpha Argues the market has already priced in a potential OpenAI pullback into Broadcom's stock, but not the upside from its Anthropic relationship.
  • Bank of America (per GuruFocus) Gave a bullish outlook on Micron that helped the stock rise 2.5% Monday, ahead of today's further gain.
  • Morgan Stanley (per BigGo Finance) Warns chip stocks broadly could still fall another 15%, seeing the correction wrapping up around mid-November -- the strongest counter-view in today's headlines.
The other side
Morgan Stanley's own research says chip stocks could still fall further before this correction is over, suggesting today's bounce in Micron and Broadcom may not be the start of a sustained recovery.
What settles it
Microsoft reports earnings this week, and its guidance on AI infrastructure spending will be a key test of whether the demand story behind today's chip rally holds up.

Broadcom Rides the AI Chip Wave, Still 31% Off Its High

Why it’s in focus Broadcom is up 1.16% premarket after KeyBanc reiterated an Overweight rating citing AI chip wins, per Investing.com, and a Seeking Alpha piece argues the market has already priced in an OpenAI setback without pricing in Anthropic's upside -- the stock is also getting a lift from the same Anthropic compute-spending story moving memory chips this morning.

Broadcom makes the specialized chips that power AI data centers and the software that runs large companies' private computing systems. Revenue grew 23% last year to nearly $64 billion, and the company keeps 54 cents of every sales dollar as operating profit -- an unusually high margin. The stock is still down almost 31% from its peak even after years of strong growth, reflecting how volatile the AI trade has been. The one thing that matters most now: whether AI infrastructure spending (from companies like Anthropic, Meta and OpenAI) keeps growing fast enough to justify Broadcom's premium valuation.

THE BUSINESS

Chips for AI Data Centers, Software for Big Business

Broadcom's biggest business, Semiconductor Solutions, made $36.9 billion last year (58% of revenue) selling custom AI chips and networking gear, and it's growing fast at 23%. Its second business, Infrastructure Software (cloud and mainframe software for large companies), made $27.0 billion and grew even faster, at 26%.
