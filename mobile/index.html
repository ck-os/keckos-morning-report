Crude Slumps 6% as Oil Supply Fears Ease and Risk Appetite Firms Ahead of Trump-Xi Summit

Futures are broadly higher this morning with WTI crude ($94.11) sinking 6.17% as cooling Mideast supply fears and easing Treasury yields globally set a risk-friendly tone ahead of Thursday's Trump-Xi summit and Bessent's 'successful' pre-summit meeting with China's Vice Premier. Nasdaq futures are leading the move (+1.14%) versus the Dow's more modest 0.61% gain, consistent with tech's outsized weight benefiting most from lower yields, while bitcoin's 4.4% jump and a still-low VIX (14.95) point to a broad, not narrow, risk-on tilt in the tape despite gold easing 0.89%. The one contradiction: the CNN Fear & Greed Index still reads 'fear' (29.1), and this morning's KPI-based sentiment regime is Risk-Off on structural grounds even as the actual price action looks constructive.

Within the notable-companies watchlist, energy majors Exxon Mobil (XOM, $162.48, -0.43%) and Chevron (CVX, $208.50, -0.43%) are both seeing outsized activity this morning, tracking directly with crude's slide even as both companies used the Gastech conference in Bangkok to publicly double down on long-term LNG demand, per Insider Monkey -- a case of a shared macro headwind (falling oil) outweighing company-specific bullish messaging. No major U.S. economic release lands today; the next scheduled prints are PCE and GDP on September 30 and the jobs report on October 2, so the tape between now and the close is more likely to be driven by continued positioning ahead of Thursday's Trump-Xi summit than by a data surprise.

Energy and Megacap Tech Names See Elevated Activity

Energy majors and a handful of megacap names are seeing the day's most elevated activity, tracking both the crude slide and pre-summit positioning.

Chevron (CVX) $208.50-0.43%

Chevron used the Gastech conference in Bangkok this week to reaffirm its long-term bet on rising global LNG demand, appearing alongside Exxon Mobil in making the same case on the same stage. That message lands against a backdrop of crude prices sliding sharply this morning on easing Middle East supply concerns, a headwind for the stock that the LNG framing doesn't directly offset.

Exxon Mobil (XOM) $162.48-0.43%

Exxon Mobil joined Chevron at the Gastech conference in Bangkok this week doubling down on long-term LNG demand growth, even as separate reporting notes Big Oil's production has kept climbing despite years of spending cuts industry-wide. The stock is trading lower this morning alongside the broader energy complex as crude prices fall.

Johnson & Johnson (JNJ) $270.35+0.13%

Johnson & Johnson is in focus after CEO Joaquin Duato was named an independent director and Audit Committee member at PepsiCo, a governance move drawing attention to board oversight practices at both companies. No J&J-specific earnings or regulatory news accompanied the move.

Netflix (NFLX) $71.73-0.08%

Netflix is drawing attention after joining Amazon and Alphabet's YouTube -- normally fierce streaming rivals -- in forming a new lobbying coalition aimed at representing the streaming industry as a unified voice in Washington. Separately, Netflix's content chief pushed back this week on concerns that YouTube's short-form content is drawing younger viewers away from traditional streaming.

Alphabet (Google) (GOOGL) $353.29+1.07%

Alphabet is named alongside Netflix and Amazon as a founding member of the new streaming-industry lobbying coalition formed in Washington, with YouTube identified as a direct competitor-turned-ally in that effort. No Alphabet-specific earnings or regulatory catalyst was identified in today's pulled headlines beyond that story.
