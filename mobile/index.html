Yields Spike Past 5.18% on Iran Standoff, but Tech Shrugs It Off

Trump's rejection of Iran's ceasefire proposal is pushing the 10-year yield to 5.18% and oil down 2.33% at the same time -- a combination that usually hurts growth stocks, but Microsoft's 3.66% premarket pop is doing more index work than the yield spike is doing damage, keeping the S&P's implied move positive.

VIX, 1-month. The volatility index has fallen 5.11% to 14.87, within a point of its 6-month low of 14.21, even as the 10-year yield pushes to a fresh high on the Iran ceasefire rejection.

CNBC's own reporting this morning ties the yield move directly to a second, related thread: debt-funded AI infrastructure spending is facing "increased risk" as borrowing costs climb alongside the 10-year's push to 5.18% -- a mechanism worth watching even though it isn't showing up as a discrete stock move yet. There's no scheduled US economic data release today (the calendar's next items are PCE and GDP on Wednesday, September 30, then the jobs report on Friday, October 2), so today's premarket action is being driven almost entirely by the geopolitical headline rather than any data print. The VIX, down 5.11% to 14.87, is sitting within a point of its 6-month low of 14.21 -- a genuinely low level that suggests the options market isn't pricing much near-term stress from the Iran story despite the yield and oil moves.
