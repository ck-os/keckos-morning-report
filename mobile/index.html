THIS MORNING · Premarket

Futures Rally After Fed Hikes Rates, VIX Craters

The Fed unanimously raised rates a quarter point on Wednesday under Chair Kevin Warsh, and rather than spooking markets, the move has cleared the air: S&P futures are up 0.80% and the VIX has collapsed 9.49% to 16.03, suggesting the hike was fully priced in and the bigger relief is that the uncertainty is behind it. Nasdaq futures are leading at +1.04%, and the reaction looks genuinely broad rather than narrow -- crude is off 1.25% and gold down 0.90%, both consistent with reduced hedging demand rather than a flight to safety. The 10-year yield ticked up modestly to 5.01%, a reminder rates are still moving higher even as equities cheer the certainty of the decision itself.

The Standout The VIX's 9.49% plunge to 16.03 is the clearest signal on the tape -- a post-Fed-decision unwind of hedging demand now that Wednesday's quarter-point hike is a known quantity.
The Outlier Oil is down 1.25% to $101.15 even as equity futures rally, a combination more consistent with easing risk premia than with a shared growth story across assets.
The Rest of the Tape Gold's 0.90% pullback and the dollar's modest 0.13% softening round out a morning where nearly every asset class is quietly repricing lower volatility rather than reacting to a fresh shock.
CBOE Volatility Index (VIX), 1-month. The VIX's 9.49% drop to 16.03 marks its steepest single-session decline this month, unwinding much of the hedging built ahead of Wednesday's Fed decision.
The VIX's near-double-digit drop is the clearest tell that Wednesday's hike removed uncertainty rather than added to it.

Beyond the Fed reaction itself, the tape is also digesting a wave of AI-infrastructure dealmaking that touches several of the same mega-cap names: CNBC reports Salesforce raised its fiscal 2030 revenue target to over $63 billion at its Dreamforce conference, Snap is partnering with Nvidia (NVDA, $217.23, +1.56% premarket), AWS and Salesforce to push its AR glasses into the enterprise market, and Arm's CEO said he's more confident in hitting a $2 billion AI-chip revenue goal -- a cluster of announcements that helps explain why chip and platform names are among today's premarket gainers even without a single dominant earnings catalyst. There's no scheduled US economic data release today per the calendar (the next is PCE and GDP on September 30), so the rate decision itself is the dominant event driving positioning into the open.
