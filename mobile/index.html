Chip Rebound Fades, CPI Looms as the Real Test

Yesterday's chip-stock rebound is running out of steam -- Technology is the biggest drag on the index this morning even as the broader tape sits flat, with the CPI print due today likely to decide which way it breaks. Oil's slide below $90 a barrel is easing one inflation worry just as the hard data arrives.

Top Story Chip Rebound Stalls, CPI Due Today Technology is 38.2% of the S&P and its -0.51% move is pulling -0.195 pts off the index -- the single biggest sector drag this morning, with MSFT's -0.46% alone worth 22% of that sector's contribution.
Also Moving Oil Drops Below $90 on Iran De-escalation Hopes Why Brent crude fell below $90 for the first time since August after President Trump said Monday he expects the Iran conflict is "going to be ending fairly soon," per CNBC; WTI crude is down 1.70% to $85.37 this morning. Worth Knowing Anthropic Pulls Back Accidental IPO Filing Why Anthropic reportedly sent bankers a copy of an IPO filing seeking up to $69.3 billion before retracting it as a mistake, per MarketWatch -- a stumble that's feeding broader jitters over AI-sector valuations and spending. Not in prices yet -- no public AI-infrastructure name moved on this specifically, but it adds to the same nervousness already showing up in Technology's drag on the index today.
Why It’s Happening

Why are chip stocks giving back yesterday's rebound?

The trigger
Monday's chip rebound -- which sent AMD up 13% on an Oppenheimer upgrade and a Google Cloud chip deal, and Coherent up nearly 19% -- is fading this morning, with Coherent (-2.71%) and Monolithic Power (-2.52%) among Technology's biggest decliners, per Nacionale, TIKR.com and GuruFocus coverage of Monday's moves.
Why it hits these stocks
These AI-infrastructure and chip-equipment names rallied hard on company-specific news (upgrades, deals) rather than a sector-wide catalyst, so some of that move is unwinding as the market refocuses on the still-unresolved Nvidia CEO investigation (Bloomberg, CNBC) and today's CPI print, which could reset the Fed-rate-path conversation that underlies AI-capex valuations.
Who’s saying what
  • Morgan Stanley's Mike Wilson Argued the market correction is in its "final innings" and called the chip selloff an entry point, per BigGo Finance.
  • UBS Sees two more Fed rate hikes but says the US stock bull market is unlikely to end, per BigGo Finance.
  • Yahoo Finance Reported that AMD's own earnings delivered "a reality check" suggesting the AI trade looks like it's falling apart, a more cautious read than the upgrade-driven rally implies.
The other side
The ongoing Nvidia board investigation into CEO Jensen Huang's reported relationship with an alleged Chinese intelligence asset (24/7 Wall St., CNBC) is a company-specific overhang that could keep weighing on sentiment regardless of how today's CPI print comes in.
What settles it
Today's CPI release is the next scheduled data point that could move the rate-hike debate UBS and others are already having -- a cooler print would ease some of the pressure feeding this morning's pullback.

Microsoft Slips as AI Spending Tests Cash Flow

Why it’s in focus Microsoft is down 0.46% premarket amid a wave of coverage on its $25 billion debt sale to fund data centers, per ad-hoc-news.de, with analysts weighing whether free cash flow pressure from AI buildout is catching up with the stock even as cloud growth stays strong.

Microsoft sells software and cloud computing to businesses and consumers worldwide, and most of its money now comes from cloud services rather than traditional software licenses. Revenue hit $331.8 billion in its last fiscal year, up almost 18%, with the cloud business (Intelligent Cloud) growing nearly 30%. The stock trades about 2.4% below its 52-week high after a strong run over the past year, but a recent $25 billion bond sale to pay for AI data centers has investors asking whether spending is outrunning cash generation. The one thing that matters most now: whether Azure's growth can keep justifying the scale of AI investment before free cash flow strain becomes a bigger story.

THE BUSINESS

Cloud Is Now Microsoft's Main Engine

Microsoft's biggest business line, Productivity and Business Processes (Office, Teams, LinkedIn), brought in $140.0 billion, 42% of revenue, and grew 15.9%. But Intelligent Cloud (Azure and server products), at $137.8 billion, grew faster at 29.7% and is closing the gap fast. The older Windows and devices business (More Personal Computing) actually shrank 1.1% to $54.1 billion.

FINANCIAL HEALTH

Hugely Profitable, Cash Flow Is the Watch Item

Microsoft keeps about 68 cents of every sales dollar after the direct cost of its products (a 67.7% gross margin) and about 50 cents after running the business (a 49.8% operating margin) -- both very high for any company. Revenue grew 18.8% and earnings grew 32.3% over the past year. It holds $64.5 billion in cash against $133.9 billion in debt, a manageable load for a company this size, though free cash flow was reported at a notably thin $3.0 billion, the figure drawing scrutiny alongside its new debt sale.

HISTORY & ODDS

Historically Strong After Setups Like This

In 73 past instances where Microsoft traded within 10% of its 52-week high and above its 200-day average (going back to 1986), the stock was higher three months later 67% of the time (average gain 6.3%) and higher a year later in 76% of cases (average gain 26.2%) -- though outcomes ranged widely, from a 43.9% loss to a 140% gain over a year.

THE BULL CASE
  • Intelligent Cloud revenue grew 29.7% last year, Microsoft's fastest-growing and largest-margin segment, showing AI demand is translating into real sales.
  • A 49.8% operating margin and 34.2% return on equity are exceptional for a company of this scale.
  • History favors it: in similar past setups (near highs, above its 200-day average) the stock rose over the next year 76% of the time.
THE BEAR CASE
  • Free cash flow of just $3.0 billion is thin next to a $4 trillion-plus market value, and a new $25 billion debt sale to fund data centers is drawing direct questions about whether AI spending is outrunning returns.
  • The older Windows/devices business shrank 1.1% last year, meaning growth now depends almost entirely on cloud execution.
  • Earnings-day reactions have been volatile and sometimes negative even after beats (a 9.8% drop in January 2026 despite a 5.7% surprise), showing the market is pricing AI spending plans as closely as the numbers themselves.
