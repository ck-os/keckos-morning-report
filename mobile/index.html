Small Caps Lead a Broad Futures Pullback as Yields and Oil Both Push Higher

A risk-off tape in equities is trading alongside rising yields, rising crude, and a surging bitcoin -- a cross-asset picture that resists a single tidy explanation.

Equity futures are pointing to a lower open across the board, with the Russell 2000 (RTY=F) down 1.56%, the Nasdaq (NQ=F) off 1.07%, the Dow (YM=F) down 1.05%, and the S&P (ES=F) down a comparatively milder 0.72%. The VIX has jumped 6.45% to 15.85, still a low absolute level historically but a notable one-day move, while the 10-year Treasury yield (^TNX) has climbed to 4.70%, up 1.10% on the day -- a move Treasury Secretary Bessent addressed directly, telling CNBC his department's buyback operation targeting longer-dated securities, where yields have been surging, could total more than $4 billion. Crude oil (CL=F) is up 2.56% to $86.65, crossing today's chart threshold, while gold (GC=F) is modestly higher at $4,573.50 (+0.39%) and the dollar index (DX-Y.NYB) is roughly flat at 98.91.

Small caps are underperforming large caps by more than two-to-one, a classic rising-rate signature, even as bitcoin and oil both push higher alongside the selloff.

Overseas, the picture is mixed rather than uniformly risk-off: Japan's Nikkei 225 rose 1.2% and Hong Kong's Hang Seng added 0.8%, while Germany's DAX slipped 0.42% and London's FTSE 100 was essentially flat at +0.04%. Bitcoin's 5.03% jump to $72,784 stands out against the equity retreat, extending a two-day, roughly 12% surge tied to a last-ditch push by President Trump and crypto industry executives to advance the Clarity Act market-structure legislation. Rising yields, a higher VIX, and small-cap underperformance together suggest the futures weakness is more of a genuine rate-driven repricing than a single-headline reaction -- worth watching whether it holds once real volume arrives at the open.

Walmart Slips Despite Raised Guidance and a Tariff Refund

The retail giant's stock is falling premarket even after lifting its full-year outlook, as investors focus on underlying sales trends.

Walmart (WMT) --

Walmart raised its full-year outlook and disclosed a large tariff refund ahead of its fiscal second-quarter earnings report Thursday, but shares are falling as investors focus on sales concerns rather than the guidance hike.
