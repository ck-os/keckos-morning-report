THIS MORNING · Premarket

Bond Rout Deepens, Fed's Williams Floats Another Hike, Stocks Extend Slide

The 30-year Treasury yield pushed to its highest level since 2004 this morning as the bond rout that began yesterday continues, and New York Fed President John Williams told a London audience it's "reasonable" to expect another rate hike by year-end -- a hawkish combination now weighing more heavily on stocks than it did a few hours ago, with Nasdaq futures down 0.98% and the VIX up 5.20% to 15.97. Traders are also watching the Trump-Xi summit underway in Washington, with China confirming AI talks have taken place and hinting at a trade-truce extension, but the market's dominant story remains the yields side, not the geopolitics.

The Standout Fed's Williams says another rate hike by year-end is 'reasonable' just as the 30-year Treasury yield hits its highest level since 2004, and equity futures are sliding in response -- Nasdaq futures -0.98%, S&P futures (ES=F) -0.52%.
The Outlier Oil is bucking the risk-off tone, up 1.74% to $93.76, even as gold slips 0.33% -- a combination more consistent with a rates/growth story than a classic flight-to-safety trade.
The Rest of the Tape The VIX's jump to 15.97 (+5.20%) and a broad futures decline across S&P, Nasdaq, Dow, and Russell point to a genuinely tape-wide reaction to higher yields rather than an isolated sector story, even as the Trump-Xi summit and China's AI-talks confirmation add a second, so-far-secondary source of uncertainty.
CBOE Volatility Index (VIX), 1-month. The VIX's jump to 15.97 this morning, up 5.20%, marks a genuine pickup in hedging demand tied to the yields-driven equity slide, though it remains well within a calm historical range rather than signaling panic.
Higher long-term yields disproportionately pressure the rate-sensitive, longer-duration growth names that dominate the Nasdaq's weighting.

The cross-asset picture largely confirms this is a rates story rather than a broad flight-to-safety: the VIX is up 5.20% to 15.97, a real but orderly increase in hedging demand, while gold is actually down 0.33% alongside stocks rather than climbing as a haven asset would in a genuine risk-off episode -- a pattern that reads as investors repricing the rate path, not fleeing risk outright. Oil stands out as the true outlier, up 1.74% to $93.76, a move that doesn't fit the yields narrative cleanly and is worth flagging as unusual against an otherwise coherent tape. There's no scheduled US economic data release today -- the next major prints (PCE and GDP) aren't due until September 30 -- so between now and the close, the tape's direction likely hinges on further Fed commentary and headlines out of the Trump-Xi summit underway in Washington, where China has already confirmed AI-related talks have taken place and hinted at extending the trade truce.
