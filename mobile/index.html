Why It’s Happening

Why are chip and PC-hardware stocks falling so hard today?

The trigger
Intel warned of prolonged PC-market weakness in its earnings and shares plunged as much as 26%, on pace for one of its worst days since 1974, per Yahoo Finance and Business Insider -- Morningstar frames it as the AI boom's chip demand colliding with a weak PC cycle.
Why it hits these stocks
Intel's own warning is about consumer PC demand specifically, but it's landing on a market already nervous about AI-spending payoff after Amazon's cautious outlook yesterday -- so it's reinforcing rather than starting the worry, and it's dragging PC-exposed names like Dell (-12.41%) and HP (-11.14%) down with it as the same demand story.
Who’s saying what
  • An analyst quoted by Benzinga Called Intel's situation 'Houston, we have a problem,' pointing to the scale of the one-day drop.
  • Morningstar Says Intel's healthy AI chip growth is being offset by a genuine 'PC winter' in consumer demand.
  • Business Insider Frames the warning as evidence the AI boom's chip demand is itself hurting Intel's broader business.
  • TD Cowen (on Amazon, the adjacent story) Called Amazon's own post-earnings selloff 'overdone,' a dissenting view on how much the AI-spending worry should weigh on these stocks.
The other side
TipRanks and other outlets note Micron, SanDisk and SK Hynix are also falling on a separate memory-market story -- Samsung's capacity and softer hyperscaler capex -- so some of today's chip weakness may be a supply/pricing issue rather than purely an AI-spending verdict.
What settles it
November 10's CPI report and the next round of mega-cap earnings/capex commentary are the scheduled tests for whether the AI-spending worry or the PC-demand story proves more durable.
The Briefing
Intel's own AI chips are growing just fine -- it's the PCs sitting next to them that are freezing up, and that's enough to drag the whole chip trade down with it.

Mondelez Pops 7.7% on an Earnings Beat

Why it’s in focus Mondelez reported Q3 profit that beat expectations, even though revenue came in slightly short, and Goldman Sachs raised its price target this morning, according to Investing.com -- a combination that's lifted shares to a fresh 52-week high.

Mondelez makes the snacks found in nearly every pantry -- Oreo cookies, Ritz crackers, Cadbury chocolate, Toblerone -- sold in over 150 countries. The business is steady and global: nearly 76% of its $38.5 billion in annual sales comes from outside the United States. The stock is up 7.68% this morning after beating profit expectations, even though revenue fell just short of forecasts, and it now sits within 3.3% of its 52-week high. The one thing that matters most: can Mondelez keep growing profit faster than sales while costs for cocoa and other inputs stay elevated?

THE BUSINESS

A Global Snacking Company, Not Just Cookies

Biscuits and baked snacks (Oreo, Ritz) are the biggest line at $18.4 billion, nearly half of sales, growing a modest 3.3%. Chocolate (Cadbury, Milka, Toblerone) is the fastest grower at 12.9%, now $12.7 billion. Smaller lines -- gum and candy, cheese and grocery, beverages -- round out the rest.

FINANCIAL HEALTH

Profits Growing Much Faster Than Sales

Revenue grew about 4.1% over the past year, but earnings grew a striking 144.9% -- the company is extracting far more profit from each dollar of sales than it was. It keeps about 22 cents of operating profit on every sales dollar (a 22% operating margin) and generated $2.25 billion in free cash flow, though it carries $22.1 billion in debt against just $1.7 billion in cash.
