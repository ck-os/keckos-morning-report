AI Doubts and Oil Spike on Iran Risk Send Tech Lower

A fresh wave of AI-spending doubts is hitting the most expensive corner of the index right out of the gate, and it's colliding with an oil spike from Gulf tensions that's pushing yields the wrong way for growth stocks. Technology is pulling the S&P 500 down about -0.90 points on its own, which explains most of the S&P 500's -1.1% premarket move.

Top Story AI-Spending Doubts Hammer Chip and Server Names Why Lam Research (LRCX, $240.23, -7.89% premarket) beat on revenue and earnings but gave guidance investors read as capped by supply-chain limits, and per TIKR.com the reaction was 'a guide that beat' but 'would have been a bit higher' -- not a miss, a disappointment on the margin of the beat. That's landing alongside Bloomberg's report that China's CSI 300 confirmed a bear market on fading AI-trade hopes, and a wider Reuters/Yahoo narrative of 'AI worries resurface.' Technology is 40.5% of the S&P and is down -2.23% today, contributing -0.902 points -- 82% of the S&P's entire -1.1% move traces to this one sector.
Also Moving Gulf Tensions Push Oil Toward $100, Yields Higher Why CNBC reports Brent crude topped $112 on worries the US and Israel are weighing fresh Iran strikes, with Houthi strikes already hitting a Saudi airport and oil field (Reuters) and the US pulling non-emergency diplomats from Saudi Arabia. WTI crude (CL=F) is up 2.58% to $99.66 on the supply-risk premium. Energy is only 3.4% of the S&P, so its +1.26% move adds back just +0.043 points -- nowhere near enough to offset Tech; the bigger channel is the 10-year yield near 5.15%, which pressures the same long-duration growth names already selling off.
Worth Knowing VIX Jumps 11% But Still Mid-Range Why The VIX rose to 18.57 (+11.33%) as the tape sold off broadly across tech and Asian equities overnight, with Nikkei -2.65% and Hang Seng -3.03%. At 18.57, VIX sits well inside its 6-month range of 14.21-22.22 -- a real jump in fear, but not yet a reading near either extreme, and the curve remains in contango (18.72 on the 3-month), meaning options markets aren't pricing sustained stress.
Why It’s Happening

Why are tech and chip-equipment stocks falling today?

The trigger
A cluster of AI-trade doubts landed together: Lam Research (LRCX) dropped 7.89% despite beating on revenue and earnings, after flagging supply-chain constraints capping next-quarter sales (TIKR.com, Economic Times); Lumentum fell 5.58% and Marvell 5.42%; and Bloomberg reported China's CSI 300 confirmed a bear market as 'AI trade, policy hopes fade.'
Why it hits these stocks
Lam, Lumentum and Marvell all sell into the AI/data-center buildout -- when a bellwether like Lam says it can't fully meet demand due to supply constraints, it raises doubts about near-term order fulfillment across the whole chip-equipment and optical-networking supply chain, not just Lam itself. Technology carries a 40.5% weight in the S&P, so any broad doubt about the AI capex cycle moves the index disproportionately.
Who’s saying what
  • TIKR.com Framed Lam's drop as a reaction to guidance that beat expectations but 'would have been a bit higher' without supply-chain limits -- a disappointment on the margin of a beat, not an outright miss.
  • Bloomberg Reported China's CSI 300 confirmed a bear market as AI-trade enthusiasm and policy-stimulus hopes both faded, framing this as part of a broader regional AI pullback.
  • A JPMorgan strategist (per MarketWatch) Argued the stock market's recent tailspin may have 'run its course,' pointing to hedge fund positioning that has already lightened considerably since its September peak.
  • Maxx Kettner, HSBC chief multiasset strategist (per MarketWatch) Warned retail investors chasing the AI rally that the goalposts for what counts as AI-trade success keep moving, a caution against assuming further easy gains.
The other side
JPMorgan's positioning read suggests this could be a controlled de-risking rather than a new deterioration in the AI growth story, since hedge funds are already lighter than they were in September.
What settles it
Whether chip-equipment peers confirm or ease the supply-chain concern Lam raised, and whether the AI-trade selloff in China's CSI 300 stabilizes or spreads further into US tech names.

Lam Research builds the specialized machines that chipmakers use to deposit and etch the microscopic layers inside computer chips -- essentially the equipment behind the equipment. Business has been strong: revenue grew 30% this past year to $23.2 billion and operating margin sits near 37 cents of every sales dollar. The stock has still gained 82% over the past year even after today's drop, but it's now 44% below its 52-week high set earlier this year. The one thing that matters most right now: today's selloff isn't about weak demand, it's about how fast Lam can actually deliver what customers already want to buy.

THE BUSINESS

Lam Sells the Machines That Make Chips

Lam Research makes deposition and etch systems -- machines that add and carve microscopic material layers onto silicon wafers -- sold to the world's major chipmakers in the US, China, Korea, Taiwan, Japan and Europe. It also services and refurbishes that equipment, a steadier, recurring revenue stream alongside new-machine sales.
