What do these mean?
1. Breadth
Compares the S&P 500 (SPY, cap-weighted — the biggest companies count the most) against RSP, an equal-weight version of the same 500 stocks where every company counts the same. When RSP keeps pace with or beats SPY, gains are broad-based across the average stock, not just the largest names; when SPY meaningfully outpaces RSP, the rally is concentrated in a handful of mega-caps.
2. Yield Curve
The interest rate the U.S. government pays to borrow money for 3 months, 2 years, and 10 years. Subtracting the shorter rate from the 10-year rate gives the “10s2y” and “10s3m” spreads — when a spread goes negative (the curve “inverts”), short-term borrowing costs more than long-term borrowing, an unusual condition that has historically preceded economic slowdowns. This is a slow-moving macro signal, not something that reacts to a single day's news — read it over weeks and months, not as a daily trading cue.
3. Volatility Term Structure
VIX measures how much volatility options traders expect in the S&P 500 over the next 30 days; VIX3M measures the same expectation 3 months out. When VIX3M is higher than VIX (“contango”), that's the normal, calm state — more uncertainty priced further into the future than right now. When VIX is higher than VIX3M (“backwardation”), options traders are pricing MORE stress in the near term than the long term — a sign of acute, immediate concern rather than steady-state uncertainty.
4. Cross-Asset Correlation
How closely the S&P 500's daily moves have tracked the 10-year Treasury yield, gold, and the U.S. dollar over the past 20 trading days. Stocks and gold, and stocks and the dollar, normally move independently or even opposite each other. When those normally-inverse pairs start moving together — or a normally-independent pair starts moving in lockstep — it can signal a broad de-risking or re-risking move across all assets at once, rather than a stock-specific story.
5. Sector Rotation
1-day and 5-day performance of the 11 S&P 500 sector groups (via their SPDR ETFs) against the S&P 500 itself, as of the most recent completed session(s). Which sectors lead can indicate whether the market favors growth and risk-taking (Technology, Consumer Discretionary) or caution (Utilities, Consumer Staples, Health Care).
