Oil Near $100, Yields Rising as Wholesale Inflation Data Looms

Treasury yields are climbing into this morning's PPI release, with CNBC reporting traders bracing for the wholesale inflation print, while crude has pushed to $97.43 (+1.44%) amid the ongoing Iran-U.S. tension near the Strait of Hormuz that CNBC also flagged overnight. The reaction is showing up broadly rather than in isolation: the 10-year yield is up 0.65% to 4.84%, the VIX is up 1.22% to 16.66, and Nasdaq futures are the weakest of the four major contracts (-0.37%) as rate-sensitive tech lags. Today's PPI print is the next real test of whether that inflation anxiety is justified.

The Standout Crude oil's push to $97.43 (+1.44%) alongside a rising 10-year yield (4.84%, +0.65%) is the morning's clearest cross-asset signature, with CNBC citing both oil and yields as the drag on equity futures ahead of today's PPI data.
The Outlier Gold is up only modestly (+0.20%) even as yields and the dollar both rise -- a combination that doesn't fit the usual flight-to-safety script, suggesting today's move is inflation-driven rather than a pure risk-off shift.
The Rest of the Tape Dow futures are holding a small gain (+0.18%) while Nasdaq futures lag (-0.37%), a split that lines up with a sector tape where most S&P sectors are red this morning except energy.
WTI Crude Oil, 1-month. Crude's push toward $100 a barrel is the session's largest single-asset move, up 1.44% this morning amid Mideast tensions near the Strait of Hormuz.
Gold's muted 0.20% gain alongside rising yields and oil is the tell: this reads as inflation anxiety, not a flight to safety.

Inside the S&P sector basket, energy (XLE) is the day's standout at +0.83%, tracking crude's climb, while industrials (XLI, -1.51%) and consumer discretionary (XLY, -1.34%) are leading the laggards -- a pattern consistent with higher input costs and higher financing rates squeezing cyclical names first. Today's PPI release, due later this morning, is the next scheduled catalyst that could move the tape materially before the close: a hot print would likely reinforce the yield move already underway, while a soft one could unwind some of this morning's defensiveness ahead of tomorrow's CPI data.
