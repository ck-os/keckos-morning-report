THIS MORNING · Market Open

Hot PPI Sends the 10-Year Above 5%, Nasdaq Leads a Broad Pullback

A hotter-than-expected wholesale inflation print pushed the 10-year yield to 4.98% and spooked a market already nursing a bruised chip sector -- the Nasdaq's 1.67% slide is nearly double the S&P's 0.95% drop because Technology, at 38.2% of the index, is carrying the weight of both the inflation scare and Nvidia's ongoing leadership turmoil. VIX jumping to 20.72 confirms this is broad risk-off, not an isolated stock story.

Top Story Wholesale Inflation Jumps 6.5%, Yields Push Past 5% Higher yields hit long-duration growth stocks hardest -- Technology is 38.2% of the S&P, which is why the Nasdaq's 1.67% drop is nearly double the S&P's 0.95% decline.
Also Moving Nvidia's Board Faces Fresh Criticism After Huang's Exit Why A day after Jensen Huang's resignation, former Medtronic CEO Bill George told Yahoo Finance he'd grade Nvidia's board "about an F" on succession planning, and CNBC reports Treasury Secretary Bessent is pointing to Commerce's handling of chip "leakage" to China -- keeping the leadership story, not just the stock price, in the headlines. Nvidia (NVDA, $159.36, -4.11%) and Broadcom (-6.48% on the day's watchlist) are dragging the chip complex; at 7.6% index weight, NVDA's move alone is a meaningful chunk of today's Technology weakness even before PPI is factored in.
Worth Knowing Bitcoin's 4.2% Drop Is the Day's Biggest Mover Why Bitcoin fell 4.23% to $68,324, the sharpest move on today's board, amid the same broader risk-off tape as stocks -- no single crypto-specific headline explains it in the data provided, suggesting it's riding the same rate-driven, risk-off wave hitting equities. Not an S&P mover directly, but it's a useful read on risk appetite: when yields spike and VIX jumps (today +10.8% to 20.72, though still inside its 6-month range of 14.21-28.18), the most speculative assets tend to fall hardest first.
Why It’s Happening

Why did a single inflation report send yields above 5% and stocks lower?

The trigger
The Producer Price Index (a measure of prices businesses charge each other) rose 6.5% year-over-year, its highest reading in nearly four years, per Zacks -- even as AP reported the monthly pace of wholesale inflation actually declined.
Why it hits these stocks
Hotter annual inflation data reduces the odds the Federal Reserve cuts rates and raises the odds it holds steady or hikes again -- the Fed's last move, in September, was already a hike to 4.0%. That repricing pushed the 10-year Treasury yield to 4.98%, per 24/7 Wall St, and higher yields make long-duration growth stocks -- concentrated in Technology, 38.2% of the S&P -- less attractive relative to safer bonds.
Who’s saying what
  • 24/7 Wall St. Framed the day around Treasury yields topping 5% and an inflation report due Friday as the twin threats to the week's best stretch in months.
  • U.S. Treasury Secretary Argued per Reuters that the Fed should not hike rates over an energy shock tied to the Iran war, pushing back against a hawkish reading of the inflation data.
  • AP Noted wholesale inflation actually declined last month even though it remains 'stubbornly elevated' on an annual basis -- a more nuanced read than the headline number alone.
The other side
The monthly PPI pace cooled per AP, and Treasury's own public comments argue against reading this as hike-justifying, suggesting the yield spike may be reacting to the annual figure's optics more than a clear change in the Fed's actual path.
What settles it
Retail Sales on Tuesday, November 17 and the PCE/GDP releases on Wednesday, November 25 will show whether this inflation pressure is broadening or was a one-off annual-base effect.

Apollo Global Management runs money for institutions and manages retirement-style insurance products -- think of it as part asset manager, part insurer. The business is growing fast (revenue up from $26 billion to $32 billion over the past year) but earnings have been bumpy, including a rough earnings reaction last week. The stock had fallen more than 15% from its 52-week high and dipped below its long-term trend line before today's jump. The one thing that matters most now: whether today's reassurance on private credit demand marks a real turn or just a one-day bounce in a stock that's been shaky around credit-market headlines all year.

THE BUSINESS

Apollo Is Mostly an Insurance-and-Lending Machine

Apollo's biggest line by far is Retirement Services -- essentially annuities and retirement products -- bringing in $27 billion of the firm's $32 billion in revenue and growing 23% over the past year. Asset Management, the traditional private-equity business most people associate with Apollo's name, is actually the smaller piece at $5 billion, though it's growing nearly as fast. A small Principal Investing arm (the firm's own money alongside clients') rounds it out and is growing fastest of all, at nearly 30%.

FINANCIAL HEALTH

Revenue Is Growing, But Profits Are Uneven

Total revenue rose to $32 billion last year, but the fundamentals data shows revenue actually fell 52% more recently -- a sign results here can swing sharply from one period to the next, typical for an insurer whose results move with markets. Operating margin is about 9% (9 cents of operating profit per revenue dollar), while the bottom-line profit margin is actually negative (-3%), meaning the company lost money after all costs in the most recent stretch counted. It holds about $41 billion in cash against $45 billion in debt -- a close balance, not a fortress.

HISTORY & ODDS

Past Dips Like This One Usually Recovered

In 20 past instances when Apollo traded this far below its 52-week high and below its 200-day average, the stock was higher three months later 53% of the time (average gain 4.8%), and higher a year later nearly 65% of the time, with an average 12-month gain of 27% -- though outcomes ranged widely, from a 27% loss to a 129% gain, so this is a wide spread of outcomes, not a reliable pattern.
