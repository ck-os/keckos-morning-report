THIS MORNING · Premarket

Treasury Yields Keep Spiking, Rattling Rate-Sensitive Trade

The 10-year yield has pushed to 5.11% and CNBC reports the surge is now posing a fresh policy headache for Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, with markets betting on a firmer inflation response even as the same yield spike just pushed the 30-year mortgage rate to 7.45%, its highest since April 2024. Futures are barely moved by comparison -- S&P futures -0.04%, Dow futures -0.01% -- but the VIX is up 3.23% and gold is bid, a classic sign the tape is nervier under the surface than the index-level numbers let on. Oil's -1.63% drop stands out as the outlier move of the morning, running against the inflation-anxiety narrative elsewhere in the data.

The Standout Treasury yields keep climbing -- the 10-year at 5.16% and 2-year at 4.85% per this morning's data -- with CNBC framing it as a genuine new problem for the Fed under Kevin Warsh, and the same move already spiking the 30-year mortgage rate to 7.45%.
The Outlier WTI crude is down 1.63% even as yields spike on inflation concern, an unusual divergence given oil and rate-driven inflation fears often move together.
The Rest of the Tape Equity futures are flat to slightly mixed (S&P -0.04%, Nasdaq +0.08%), but the VIX's 3.23% jump and a firmer gold price suggest more caution under the hood than the index numbers alone show.
Hang Seng Index, 1-month. Hong Kong's benchmark fell 1.69% overnight, the largest single-day move across the international indices tracked this morning.
The 10-year at 5.16% is now a real problem for the Fed, not just a bond-market curiosity -- it's already pushed the 30-year mortgage to 7.45%.

With no scheduled US economic release landing today -- the calendar's next entries are PCE and GDP on September 30 and the jobs report on October 2 -- today's tape is being driven entirely by the ongoing yield story rather than a fresh data print. Oil is the notable outlier against that backdrop, down 1.63% to $93.07, a divergence worth flagging since rising yields on inflation concern would typically travel with firmer, not softer, crude. Elsewhere, the Trump-Xi summit and state dinner are dominating the geopolitical wires but haven't produced a clear market reaction in the data pulled this morning -- worth watching for spillover into trade- or tech-policy-sensitive names given Xi's on-record push for U.S.-China cooperation on AI, but nothing sized yet.
