Futures Extend Gains Into Fed Decision as Yields and Oil Ease Back

The Fed's rate decision and Retail Sales both land today, and futures have added to premarket gains since the overnight edition (S&P futures +0.34%, Nasdaq futures +0.59%) even as CNBC reports traders still see a 92% chance of a hike rather than a cut -- a reversal of the market's usual instinct that shows how unsettled the setup already was after yesterday's across-the-board close lower. The move is showing up broadly, not just in futures: the 10-year yield has slipped to 4.98%, VIX has eased to 16.80 (-2.33%), and crude has dropped 2.02% to $103.69 on reported rising U.S. inventories, even as gold keeps climbing (+1.11% to $4,380.90) on lingering Iran-conflict tension -- a mixed but calmer cross-asset picture heading into this afternoon's announcement.

The Standout VIX has fallen 2.33% to 16.80 and the 10-year yield has eased to 4.98%, both retreating even as traders still price a 92% chance the Fed hikes rates this afternoon.
The Outlier Crude is down 2.02% to $103.69 on reports of a surprise rise in U.S. inventories, even as gold pushes higher (+1.11% to $4,380.90) on Iran-conflict supply risk -- oil and gold pulling in opposite directions on the same geopolitical story.
The Rest of the Tape All four major futures are modestly higher (Nasdaq futures leading at +0.59%), and overseas markets are following suit, with the Nikkei up 0.69% and the FTSE up 0.64%.
CBOE Volatility Index (VIX), 1-month. VIX has eased 2.33% to 16.80 this morning, its calmest premarket level in over a week, as traders await this afternoon's Fed decision.
Yields, VIX, and oil have all eased together into the Fed decision -- a broad, not narrow, pullback in risk pricing, even as gold keeps climbing on the same Iran-conflict backdrop.

Retail Sales prints today alongside the Fed's decision, giving the market a second data point to react to before the close -- a weak or strong read could easily reinforce or complicate whatever the Fed does this afternoon. Away from the macro story, chip-sector M&A chatter is also moving individual names: CNBC reports Intel and SK Hynix shares jumped on a report the two are discussing U.S. memory-chip manufacturing, a headline sitting alongside separate reporting that Micron's blowout quarter and $100 billion in committed revenue contracts left its stock sliding anyway -- a reminder that even strong AI-memory results are being read skeptically this week.
