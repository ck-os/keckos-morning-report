Chips Rebound as Nvidia Bounces Back From Huang Scrutiny

Nvidia (NVDA, $177.44, +5.12%) is clawing back a chunk of Tuesday's near-$5 trillion chip rout, and since it's 7.6% of the S&P on its own, that one stock is worth half the index's entire 0.77% move this morning. Yields are still elevated after Tuesday's hot CPI reading, which keeps pressure on Health Care and Staples, but Tech's bounce is simply bigger.

Top Story Nvidia Bounces Back From Huang Scandal Selloff NVDA alone is 7.6% of the S&P and its +5.04% move (per Index attribution) is worth 50% of the index's entire move today -- more than the whole Technology sector's other names combined.
Also Moving Hot CPI Has Goldman Penciling In More Fed Tightening Why MarketWatch reports Goldman Sachs is revising its Fed forecast toward additional tightening after Tuesday's inflation data came in hotter than expected, extending beyond gas prices per the AP -- hotter inflation means less room for the Fed to cut and more hike risk, not less. The 10-year sits at 4.88% and 3-month bills at 4.24% -- a curve that's pricing policy staying tight, which is why rate-sensitive Health Care (-0.73%) and Staples (-0.93%) are lagging even as the headline index rises.
Worth Knowing Optics and AI-Infrastructure Suppliers Are Jumping Too Why Coherent (+8.59%) and Lumentum (+8.44%) are both riding Lumentum's upbeat results and reports that Microsoft is pushing to halt a ban on Chinese optics -- TIKR.com reports Coherent is 'basically booked out' -- while a pending Marvell-Lumentum deal adds its own speculative lift, per Barron's and ad-hoc-news.de. Neither name is a megacap, so their double-digit-adjacent moves barely register in index points -- but they show the AI-infrastructure bounce is broader than just Nvidia and Broadcom.
Why It’s Happening

Why are chip stocks bouncing back today?

The trigger
Nvidia shares are rebounding per CNBC as investors weigh whether CEO Jensen Huang's personal scandal and a China-related scrutiny probe will meaningfully hurt the business, following Tuesday's roughly $430 billion selloff tied to the controversy, per TipRanks.
Why it hits these stocks
Chip stocks fell hard on fears the Huang controversy could disrupt Nvidia's leadership or its China dealings right before earnings; the bounce reflects investors concluding the selloff was overdone relative to the company's actual fundamentals, since the scrutiny is personal/legal rather than a demand or competitive problem.
Who’s saying what
  • Barron's Argues the scrutiny around Jensen Huang might actually be a buying opportunity rather than a lasting threat to Nvidia's business.
  • Bloomberg Frames Nvidia's bull case as hinging specifically on Huang surviving the China spy probe, keeping the outcome genuinely uncertain.
  • Goldman Sachs Per Yahoo Finance, Goldman now calls chip stocks broadly 'oversold' and has published names it says investors should consider from within the selloff.
  • The New York Times Reports a trader had raised alarms about Nvidia's China entanglements months before this week's selloff, suggesting the risk wasn't entirely a surprise.
The other side
Goldman Sachs separately warned, per MarketWatch, that it's now penciling in additional Fed tightening after Tuesday's hot CPI print -- a hawkish backdrop that could cap any risk-on bounce in rate-sensitive growth names like chips if yields keep climbing.
What settles it
Nvidia's upcoming earnings report is the next concrete test of whether the Huang controversy has actually dented demand or bookings.

Stryker makes medical equipment hospitals and surgeons use every day -- things like joint replacements, surgical tools, and hospital beds. The business is still growing (revenue up 7.6% over the past year to $25.1 billion), but investors just got a guidance cut after slower growth in some product lines, and the stock has fallen alongside it. Shares sit 17% below their 52-week high of $384.17, even though they're still trading above their 200-day average price. Wall Street's consensus rating remains 'buy' with a wide range of future price targets, reflecting real disagreement about how much the slowdown matters.

THE BUSINESS

Stryker Is Mostly a Surgical-Tools Company

Its biggest unit, MedSurg and Neurotechnology, sold $15.6 billion of surgical equipment, hospital beds, and stroke-treatment devices last year -- 62% of all revenue -- and grew nearly 16%. The smaller Orthopaedics and Spine unit, which makes hip, knee, and shoulder implants, brought in $9.5 billion and grew more slowly, at 4.3%.

HISTORY & ODDS

Past Dips Like This Usually Recovered

In 45 past instances where Stryker traded at least 15% below its 52-week high while still above its 200-day average, the stock was higher three months later 61% of the time (average +5.1%) and higher a year later in 82% of cases (average +30.7%) -- though outcomes varied widely, from a 38% loss to a 167% gain.

THE BULL CASE
  • The larger MedSurg and Neurotechnology segment grew nearly 16% last year and now makes up 62% of revenue.
  • After similar past pullbacks, the stock has historically recovered -- up 82% of the time a year later.
  • Analysts still rate it a 'buy' on average, with targets well above the current price.
THE BEAR CASE
  • The guidance cut followed a Q3 growth miss, and earnings growth has actually turned negative recently.
  • The stock has fallen on five of its last eight earnings reactions, a pattern of disappointing the market.
  • Debt ($15.4B) is more than four times cash on hand ($3.3B), limiting flexibility if growth keeps slowing.
