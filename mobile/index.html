Yields at 2007 Highs as Oil-Bond Link Tightens Ahead of Fed

The 10-year Treasury yield has pushed to its highest level since 2007, CNBC reports, as investors increasingly price in a rate hike at Wednesday's FOMC decision -- a fresh CNBC survey now shows respondents expecting at least two more hikes over the next year, with most citing an inflation problem broader than just energy. Oil is up 0.99% to $102.39 and CNBC notes crude and the 10-year yield are moving in their tightest lockstep since 2019, a combination it flags as bad news for equities. Stock futures, gold, and bitcoin are all lower together -- a broad, cross-asset retreat that reads as the market bracing for a hawkish surprise rather than an isolated dip in any one asset.

There's no scheduled US economic release today (Retail Sales and the FOMC decision itself both land tomorrow, September 16), so today's premarket move looks driven entirely by yesterday's yield move and this morning's CNBC hike survey rather than a fresh data print -- worth watching whether that yield climb extends into tomorrow's Fed decision itself.
