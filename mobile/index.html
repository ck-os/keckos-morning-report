Chip Stocks Rebound as Software Splits From Hardware in AI Trade

Memory and optics names like Sandisk and Coherent are leading Tech's bounce while software gets singled out as the AI trade's safer half -- that split, not a blanket rebound, is what's lifting the S&P. Falling yields and a cooling VIX are giving the whole tape room to breathe, but the move is still concentrated in a handful of chip-adjacent names rather than broad participation.

Top Story Chip Stocks Bounce, But Software Splits From Hardware Technology is 38.2% of the S&P and contributes +0.279 pts today, 39% of the index's move -- Nvidia alone adds +0.098 pts (14% of the S&P's move) on its +1.29% bounce, even with Apple (-0.13%) a drag.
Also Moving Tariff Threats on 14 Nations Land With a Shrug Why CNBC and Reuters report the White House unveiled fresh tariff hikes on 14 trading partners, with reduced rates dangled for countries that sign trade deals -- but global markets are rallying anyway, with investors described as choosing to 'fish for bargains' rather than price in trade risk. The dollar index is barely moved, +0.04% to 103.71 -- a real tariff risk-off typically shows up as dollar strength, so the market isn't treating this as today's story yet.
Worth Knowing VIX Eases but Stays Well Above Its 6-Month Low Why The VIX fell 4.91% to 26.90 as the tech rebound takes hold, but it remains far from its 6-month low of 14.21 and close to backwardation territory against its 3-month contract (26.29). Backwardation in the vol curve means options markets still expect more near-term turbulence than calm -- not a crisis signal, but a reminder the rebound hasn't fully erased the week's scare.
Why It’s Happening

Why are chip stocks rebounding, and why is software being treated differently?

The trigger
The Financial Times reports 'software stocks spared in tech rout as AI narrative shifts,' while CNBC and Deutsche Bank highlight memory and optics names -- Sandisk, Coherent, Lumentum -- leading today's chip bounce; TipRanks reports memory peers Micron and SK Hynix also surged overnight on what it calls an 'Nvidia flash test.'
Why it hits these stocks
When a sector-wide selloff pauses, the market often re-sorts winners from losers rather than rallying uniformly -- memory and optics suppliers tied directly to AI-server buildouts (Sandisk, Coherent) are rebounding hardest because their earnings are a narrower, more immediate read on hyperscaler capex, while software names were framed as less exposed to the capex-spending anxiety that drove the selloff in the first place.
Who’s saying what
  • Financial Times Reports software stocks were largely spared in the broader tech rout as the AI narrative shifts away from pure infrastructure spending.
  • Deutsche Bank Argues semiconductor exports have overtaken oil and gas for the first time, framing chips as a structurally bigger story than the day's bounce alone.
  • TipRanks Reports memory stocks Micron, SanDisk and SK Hynix surged overnight on an 'Nvidia flash test,' raising the question of whether buybacks follow.
  • Barron's Reports Nvidia reportedly halted AI server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries amid tighter US export controls -- a headwind even as the stock bounces.
The other side
Nvidia itself is still down -3.91% on the notable-companies watchlist even as it's the S&P's top mega-cap contributor this morning (+1.29% in the index-attribution data), and Seeking Alpha describes an analyst upgrade to just 'Hold,' calling it 'too early to buy' -- a sign the rebound in chip-adjacent names hasn't fully extended to Nvidia's own shares on this data cut.
What settles it
No scheduled data today; the next real test for the AI trade and rates is the December 4 jobs report, followed by the December 9 Fed decision and December 10 CPI.

Amazon runs three businesses in one: a massive online store, a cloud-computing arm called AWS that rents out computing power to other companies, and a fast-growing advertising business. The core retail business is steady but slow-growing, while AWS and advertising are growing faster and make up a growing share of profit. The stock is down about 13% from its 52-week high and has round-tripped through some of the sharpest earnings-day swings of any mega-cap this year. The one thing that matters most now: whether AWS's growth, prized by investors for carrying Amazon's profit margin, is slowing as some analysts warn, or whether this week's wobble is just part of a broader AI-stock pullback.

THE BUSINESS

Online Stores Still Amazon's Biggest Line

Online Stores brought in $269.3 billion last year, 38% of Amazon's $716.9 billion in total revenue, but grew a modest 9%. Amazon Web Services (cloud computing) is the profit engine -- $128.7 billion in revenue, up nearly 20% -- and Advertising is the fastest grower, up 22.1% to $68.6 billion, as Amazon sells ad space to brands trying to reach shoppers already on its site.

FINANCIAL HEALTH

Profitable and Growing, but Cash Flow Went Negative

Revenue grew 12.1% over the past year and the company keeps about 13 cents of operating profit on every sales dollar (operating margin 13.1%), with operating income nearly doubling from $36.9 billion in 2023 to $80.0 billion in 2025. Free cash flow, however, is currently negative (-$34.7 billion) -- Amazon is spending heavily, likely on AWS data centers -- while it holds $114.2 billion in cash against $304.8 billion in debt.

HISTORY & ODDS

Pullbacks Like This One Usually Faded Within a Year

Amazon has traded at least 10% below its 52-week high and below its 200-day average 38 separate times since 1998. Three months later, it was higher 67.6% of the time, averaging a 9.0% gain; a year later, it was higher 71.4% of the time, averaging a 26.0% gain -- though outcomes ranged widely, from a 77.8% loss to a 233% gain, so this is a historical pattern, not a prediction.

THE BULL CASE
  • AWS and Advertising, the two fastest-growing and most profitable lines, now make up a rising share of revenue -- 27.6% combined -- and operating income nearly doubled in two years.
  • Return on equity of 27.4% is strong, and the historical base rate for a pullback like this one shows gains 3 and 12 months out most of the time.
THE BEAR CASE
  • Free cash flow is currently negative ($-34.7 billion), meaning heavy spending (likely AWS infrastructure) is outrunning the cash the business brings in right now.
  • Bernstein has flagged cloud-growth concerns per TradingKey, and the stock's last earnings reaction (-5.6% on October 29) came with a negative surprise -- a reminder the market punishes misses sharply.
