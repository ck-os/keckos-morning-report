Also Moving Health Care and Bank Stocks Quietly Lead the Tape Why Health Care is broad-based -- Quest Diagnostics (+3.26%) and Eli Lilly (LLY, $1,385.93, +2.32% premarket) are leading -- while Financials get a lift from KKR's (+4.25%) earnings-day pop and a WSJ piece arguing bank-regulation threats from Capitol Hill are 'worse than its bite.' Worth Knowing VIX Still Elevated Despite a Sharp Overnight Drop Why VIX fell 7.41% overnight, but at 23.74 it's still well above its 6-month low of 14.21 and sits in contango (VIX3M at 24.6) -- a sign the options market still wants more protection further out than right now, consistent with 24/7 Wall St.'s report that downside-protection buying just hit a record last week. Not a crisis level, but nowhere near calm either -- worth watching whether it keeps fading toward its 6-month range or stalls out near 24.
Why It’s Happening

Why is Nvidia falling again today?

The trigger
CNBC reports Nvidia's board has launched an independent investigation into CEO Jensen Huang after stories surfaced alleging he was the target of a 'foreign intelligence effort' connected to an alleged Chinese spy; Business Insider frames it as 'bombshell revelations about CEO Jensen Huang's affair,' and Stocktwits reports Beijing has called the allegations 'smears against China.'
Why it hits these stocks
Nvidia is the face of the AI trade and carries the heaviest single-stock weight in the S&P (7.6%), so any headline risk around its CEO -- on top of last week's broader AI-spending selloff -- gets amplified into real index-level points; the story also lands directly on an already-jittery sector, with Citi telling clients per MarketWatch that it's 'better to sit out the global tech trade' until AI positioning 'gets cleaner.'
Who’s saying what
  • CNBC Reports Nvidia's board launched an independent probe following claims Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort.
  • Citi's global strategy team (via MarketWatch) Argues investors should stay out of the global tech trade until AI positioning is cleaner, rather than resuming their prior overweight stance.
  • Reported Chinese government response (per Stocktwits) Beijing has reportedly rejected the spy allegations as 'smears against China.'
  • Morgan Stanley's chief strategist (via MarketWatch) Argues the broader market correction is 'entering its final phase' but suggests waiting before buying the dip, expecting a potential further leg down first.
The other side
AMD (+2.33%) and Broadcom (+1.26%) are both rising this morning, suggesting investors are treating this as an Nvidia-specific headline risk rather than a read-through on AI/chip demand broadly.
What settles it
Nvidia's own public response to the board investigation, and whether the story develops further detail or fades, will likely determine if this stays an isolated single-stock move or reignites the broader AI-trade selloff from last week.

KKR Is Now an Insurance Company With a PE Arm

KKR's insurance segment brought in $11.6 billion last year, 60% of total revenue, though that line actually shrank 20.7% from the year before. Its asset-management and 'strategic holdings' business brought in $7.8 billion and grew 8.6%, making it the faster-growing of the two.

FINANCIAL HEALTH

Profits Growing Fast, but Margins Still Thin

Earnings grew 40% over the past year even as revenue grew a more modest 7.8% -- a sign more of each new dollar is reaching the bottom line. The company keeps 20.7 cents of operating profit on every revenue dollar (operating margin) and 12.2 cents after all costs (net margin). It holds $47.6 billion in cash against $56.2 billion in debt, and pays shareholders a 0.81% dividend yield.

HISTORY & ODDS

Beaten-Down KKR Has Usually Bounced Back

In 13 past instances when KKR traded at least 30% below its 52-week high and below its 200-day average (going back to 2011), the stock was higher three months later 67% of the time, with an average gain of 7.4%. A year out, it was higher 70% of the time, averaging a 36% gain -- though outcomes ranged widely, from a 21% loss to a 123% gain, so this is a historical pattern, not a prediction.

THE BULL CASE
  • Earnings grew 40% over the past year, faster than revenue, suggesting improving efficiency.
  • In 13 similar past setups (30%+ below high, below the 200-day average), the stock was higher a year later 70% of the time.
  • Analyst consensus is 'buy' with a mean target roughly 20% above the current price.
THE BEAR CASE
  • The insurance segment, 60% of revenue, shrank 20.7% last year.
  • The stock is down 18.3% over the past year and has had several sharp double-digit drops tied to deal and clawback news.
  • Debt ($56.2 billion) exceeds cash ($47.6 billion), and return on equity is a modest 7.3%.
