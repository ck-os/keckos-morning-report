THIS MORNING · Premarket

Chip Selloff Deepens on China Threat; UnitedHealth and Banks Hold the Line

Tech is dragging the S&P on its own -- Nvidia (NVDA, $224.25, -0.91% premarket), Micron and Broadcom are all down on China's chip advances and AI-spending doubts -- but UnitedHealth's earnings beat and strength in Financials ahead of bank earnings are offsetting almost all of it, leaving the index down just 0.1%.

Top Story China's CXMT Advance Deepens the Chip Selloff Why Forbes and others report China's CXMT has advanced its memory-chip technology just as Broadcom's CEO compared AI investing to a 'casino,' per The Guardian -- together stoking fears that AI capex won't pay off and that Chinese competition is catching up faster than expected. Wedbush's Dan Ives called the selloff 'way overdone,' per TradingView, but the names most exposed to memory and China competition -- Micron, SanDisk, Seagate, Western Digital -- are leading the slide. Technology is 40.5% of the S&P and is down 0.88%, contributing -0.356 points -- the single biggest driver of today's move, more than explaining the index's -0.1% decline on its own.
Also Moving UnitedHealth's Earnings Beat Lifts Health Care Why UnitedHealth raised its 2026 profit forecast to at least $20.50 a share, up from $19.50-$20.00, after beating third-quarter estimates, per Reuters -- a rare bright spot that's pulling Health Care higher even as Moderna and J&J slide on separate pressures (J&J flagged Middle East conflict weighing on medical device sales). UnitedHealth itself is up 2.47% and Health Care's 0.5% gain adds back 0.046 points -- a small but real offset to Tech's drag.
Worth Knowing Big Bank Earnings Loom, Financials Already Rallying Why Goldman Sachs (+2.69%) and Morgan Stanley (+1.81%) are leading Financials higher ahead of today's bank earnings, per Barchart and Saxo, as traders position for results that could confirm whether higher rates are still boosting bank profitability. Financials are 11.2% of the index and contributing +0.082 points -- not enough to offset Tech, but the clearest sign money is rotating toward banks while chips sell off.
Why It’s Happening

Why are chip stocks falling again today?

The trigger
China's CXMT has advanced its server-memory chip technology, raising competitive concerns for SanDisk, Micron and other memory makers, per Yahoo Finance and Forbes; separately, Broadcom's CEO likened AI investing to a 'casino,' per The Guardian, and Micron's own headlines cite Taiwan strike risk alongside the China competition threat.
Why it hits these stocks
Memory chips are a commoditized, margin-sensitive business -- new Chinese supply threatens pricing power precisely where names like Micron, SanDisk, Seagate and Western Digital earn their margins, while the Broadcom CEO's comment feeds a broader doubt about whether AI infrastructure spending will generate the returns priced into the group.
Who’s saying what
  • Dan Ives, Wedbush Called the semiconductor selloff 'way overdone' given underlying AI demand, per TradingView.
  • Broadcom's CEO Compared current AI investing to a 'casino,' per The Guardian, stoking doubts about capital discipline in the space.
  • An unnamed top economist Warned that 'high concentration' of semiconductor stocks isn't a real bull market and investors should 'beware,' per Benzinga.
  • Reuters Reported chip weakness is weighing on S&P 500 futures broadly as investors also await big bank earnings.
The other side
Micron's own headlines note its revenue and profits are surging even as the stock sinks deeper into bear territory, per Invezz -- suggesting the selloff may be sentiment- and positioning-driven rather than a reflection of actual fundamentals.
What settles it
Big bank earnings landing this week and Wednesday's CPI print are the next scheduled events that could either validate the AI-spending worry or give the group room to stabilize.
