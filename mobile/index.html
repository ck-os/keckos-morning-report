Why It’s Happening

Why are chip and tech stocks rebounding again this morning?

The trigger
Memory chipmakers Sandisk, Micron and Western Digital led a broad rally in AI-adjacent names overnight, per Yahoo Finance, with Intel up 5% before the open and chip stocks described as rising "across the board" by TradingKey ahead of the shortened Black Friday session.
Why it hits these stocks
These are the same names that sold off hardest over the past month -- Technology's 5-day return is still -4.9% even after today's bounce -- so a round of bargain-hunting and short-covering in oversold AI-infrastructure names can produce an outsized one-day pop without a single new fundamental catalyst.
Who’s saying what
  • Mizuho (via ad-hoc-news.de) Kept a Buy rating and $237.50 price target on Sandisk even as the stock bounced sharply.
  • MarketWatch Reported Nvidia's stock is 'still paying the price for the Jensen Huang CEO drama' and has tumbled 27% over the past month.
  • Barron's Framed Nvidia as 'finding a floor' with Jensen Huang back in focus, naming Sandisk, Broadcom, AMD and Nvidia among the stocks explaining today's market.
  • Benzinga Noted Sandisk's stock hit a 'death cross' technical pattern despite the Nvidia-linked AI rally, a note of caution inside the bounce.
The other side
Benzinga's point that Sandisk is forming a bearish 'death cross' even as it rallies suggests the technical damage from the prior sell-off hasn't fully healed, and MarketWatch's framing of Nvidia 'still paying the price' for recent turmoil implies today's move is a bounce within a larger drawdown, not a reversal of it.
What settles it
Whether today's thin, shortened Black Friday session (half-day trading) actually holds through next week's full sessions will show if this rebound has real follow-through or fades like the overnight futures spike some outlets flagged as running ahead of the cash market.

Fast Growth, Very High Margins

Revenue grew 51% from 2024 to 2025 ($2.95 billion to $4.47 billion), and operating income nearly doubled to $2.09 billion -- meaning the company keeps about 46 cents of operating profit on every sales dollar. It holds $24.8 billion in cash against $22.6 billion in debt, a solid cushion, though earnings growth over the past year was actually negative (-16.4%) even as revenue kept climbing.

HISTORY & ODDS

Mixed Record After Similar Deep Declines

In the past, when Robinhood traded at least 50% below its 52-week high and below its 200-day average, it bounced in the following 3 months every time (4 occurrences, averaging a 16.3% gain) -- but a year later the record was split 50/50, with an average 12-month return of -8.8%. Both samples are small, so this is suggestive at best, not a reliable pattern.

THE BULL CASE
  • Revenue grew 51% year over year, with options and crypto -- its two biggest lines -- both growing nearly 50% or more.
  • Operating margin is a strong 45.6%, and the company carries more cash ($24.8B) than debt ($22.6B).
  • After similarly steep declines in the past, the stock rallied an average of 16.3% over the following 3 months in 4 of 4 cases.
THE BEAR CASE
  • The stock is down 42.8% over the past year and has missed or disappointed on its last several earnings reports, each triggering a selloff.
  • Earnings growth over the trailing year was actually negative (-16.4%) despite rising revenue, a warning sign on cost or margin pressure.
  • A year after similar past declines, the stock was down 8.8% on average, with only a 50/50 chance of being higher.
