THIS MORNING · Premarket

Hot CPI Hikes Fear; Chip Rout Deepens on AI-Spending Doubts

Wednesday's hotter-than-expected 3.7% CPI pushed the 10-year yield to 5.21% and revived hike talk, and that's landing squarely on the most rate-sensitive, priciest corner of the market: AI chips. Technology is down 1.4% and alone is pulling about 0.57 points off the S&P, while Micron (-3.73%) and Nvidia (-1.84%) lead a broader storage-and-AI-hardware slide that's dragging the Nasdaq futures down over 1% even as defensive Health Care and Staples hold the Dow and Russell in the green.

Top Story Chip Rout Deepens as AI-Spending Doubts Spread Why Memory and storage names are sliding on reports of China's CXMT advancing in server-memory chips and a Taiwan strike risk at Micron, per TradingKey and Benzinga, while Barron's reports Anthropic's slowing revenue growth is adding to fears that AI spending can't keep outrunning its returns -- TradingView and The Guardian both tie the move to investors 'questioning the sustainability of AI spending.' Technology is 40.5% of the S&P and down 1.4% -- it's contributing about -0.57 points on its own, more than explaining the index's -0.26% move; Nvidia alone accounts for 58% of the S&P's total decline.
Also Moving Hot CPI Pushes 10-Year Yield to 5.21%, Revives Hike Talk Why Wednesday's CPI print came in at 3.7% annually, hotter than expected, and AP reports it's the worst equity day since July; Reuters says UBS has now extended its forecast to include a March Fed hike. The Fed's last move was a hike in September to 4.0%, so this isn't cut-timeline talk -- it's whether the Fed has to go again. A 5.21% 10-year raises the discount rate on long-duration growth earnings -- exactly what's pressuring the 40%-weighted Tech sector; CNBC frames $2 trillion deficits at 5% yields as a structural headwind, not a one-day story.
Worth Knowing TSMC's Record Profit Can't Stop the Chip Slide Why TSMC posted a sixth straight record quarter and its CEO said he sees 'no sign of a slowdown' in AI chip demand, per MarketWatch -- the opposite signal from the stocks selling off around it, suggesting the selloff is about valuation and financing worries, not near-term demand. Not resolving the rout yet -- TSMC's own strong numbers didn't stop Micron (-3.73%) or Western Digital (-4.37%) from falling, a sign the market is pricing a future spending slowdown rather than today's results.
Why It’s Happening

Why are chip and AI-infrastructure stocks falling so hard today?

The trigger
A deepening global tech selloff -- Nikkei down nearly 5%, SK Hynix down 57% from its peak per Moneycontrol -- is colliding with Wednesday's hotter 3.7% CPI print, and Barron's reports Anthropic's slowing revenue growth (ahead of a planned public listing) is adding a fresh demand-side worry to the AI trade.
Why it hits these stocks
Chipmakers and storage names (Micron, Western Digital, Sandisk) carry some of the market's highest growth-expectation valuations, so they're hit twice: higher yields compress what investors will pay for future earnings, and reports of China's CXMT progressing in server-memory chips plus a Taiwan strike risk at Micron (per TradingKey) raise real doubts about pricing power and supply.
Who’s saying what
  • JPMorgan (via MarketWatch) A strategist argues this AI-trade pullback isn't set up for a rebound like July's -- fewer shorts, buybacks ending, no fresh inflows, and positioning already too long.
  • TSMC CEO (via MarketWatch) Says he sees no sign of a slowdown in AI chip demand, even as TSMC reports a sixth straight record profit quarter.
  • UBS (via Reuters) Has extended its Fed tightening forecast to include a possible March hike after the inflation surprise, reinforcing the higher-for-longer rate backdrop pressuring growth stocks.
  • Citi (via Reuters) A Citi banker says governments globally may shift toward non-dollar bonds amid what's described as an AI-driven liquidity crunch -- a sign the funding-strain worry extends beyond equities.
The other side
TSMC's own record quarter and CEO commentary directly contradict the demand-slowdown narrative, suggesting the selloff may be more about stretched valuations and financing than actual order books.
What settles it
Nvidia and TSMC-linked earnings commentary into month-end, plus the October 28 FOMC decision, will show whether this is a valuation reset or a real crack in AI demand.
The Briefing
TSMC just posted a record quarter and said it sees no slowdown -- and the chip stocks around it are falling anyway, which tells you this selloff is about valuation and rates, not today's demand.
