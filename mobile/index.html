Fed's Rate Move Digested as Oil Slide Eases Inflation Worry; VIX Slumps 13%

Wednesday's Fed rate hike -- the first since 2023 -- is being read calmly rather than as a fresh shock: the S&P 500 and Nasdaq posted their best day in six weeks, helped along by a sharp drop in crude (WTI now $101.17, -1.23%) that eased inflation worries just as the Fed itself is being watched for its next move. Jensen Huang's comment that Nvidia (NVDA, $219.24, -0.05%) will sell twice as many chips next year kept the AI-infrastructure story alive even as NVDA itself sat roughly flat. The calm is showing up broadly, not just in one index: the VIX cratered 12.82% to 15.44 and the 10-year yield eased 1.18%, a combination that reads as confirmed risk appetite rather than a narrow bounce.

The Standout The VIX's 12.82% collapse to 15.44 is today's single loudest signal, arriving alongside a falling 10-year yield (4.95%, -1.18%) in the wake of Wednesday's Fed rate decision -- a classic de-risking-in-reverse move as markets digest the hike without panic.
The Outlier Crude's 1.23% drop to $101.17 is doing real work behind the scenes, easing the inflation-pass-through worry that a rate hike alone would otherwise sharpen.
The Rest of the Tape Overnight futures are mixed-to-flat (S&P futures -0.09%, Nasdaq futures -0.17%) even as gold, the dollar, and bitcoin all sit little-changed -- a quiet, directionless overnight session after Wednesday's cash-market rally.
VIX, 1-month. The VIX's 12.82% drop to 15.44 marks its steepest single-day decline in recent weeks, tracking the market's calm digestion of Wednesday's Fed rate hike.
A falling VIX and falling 10-year yield moving in the same direction as equities is the signature of a broad, confirmed reaction to the Fed decision, not stocks drifting alone.

The standout mover in the Glance basket by far is the VIX, down 12.82% to 15.44 -- a sharp de-risking that lines up with the 10-year yield's 1.18% decline to 4.95%, both moving the same direction as Wednesday's equity rally in what reads as textbook confirmation rather than contradiction: falling volatility and falling yields alongside gaining stocks all point the same way, toward markets treating the Fed decision as manageable rather than threatening. Overnight futures themselves are essentially flat to slightly negative (S&P futures -0.09%, Nasdaq futures -0.17%), consistent with a market that already did its reacting during Wednesday's cash session rather than building a new move into this morning. No major US economic release lands today -- the next scheduled prints (PCE and GDP) aren't due until September 30 -- so the next real catalyst for a shift in this calm is more likely to come from additional Fed commentary than from a data surprise.

Boeing, AMD, and GE Aerospace See Elevated Activity

A handful of names are seeing outsized activity today, tied to earnings-adjacent coverage, acquisition news, and analyst chatter rather than a single common thread.

Boeing (BA) $197.40+0.20%

Boeing shares slipped even as the broader market rallied, with Zacks noting the stock closed down roughly 2.5% versus its prior session -- a divergence from Wednesday's broad market gain that stood out enough to draw its own coverage.

GE Aerospace (GE) $313.52+0.02%

GE Aerospace remains in focus following its $11.75 billion acquisition of castings maker Consolidated Precision Products, the company's largest deal since becoming standalone in 2024, aimed at securing turbine-blade component supply that has been a persistent industry bottleneck. Separately, CFO Rahul Ghai told a Morgan Stanley conference that commercial services, original equipment, and defense demand all remain strong, with the company's services backlog now around $170 billion.

AMD (AMD) $545.40+0.06%

AMD drew fresh attention after Investor's Business Daily flagged it alongside Moderna and SpaceX as names showing bullish technical signals following the S&P 500 and Nasdaq's move back above their 50-day lines in the wake of this week's Fed decision. The name is also getting a secondary lift from the broader AI-chip demand narrative, including Intel's rumored SK Hynix talks covered the same day.
