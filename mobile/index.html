Crude's Geopolitical Bid Outweighs a Calmer Bond Market

Iran tensions push oil up more than 3% and futures modestly lower, even as yields ease and Asia rallies.

U.S. equity futures are pointing to a soft open, with S&P 500 futures (ES=F) off 0.29% to 7,716.25, Nasdaq futures (NQ=F) down 0.57% to 29,443.50, and the Dow (YM=F) and Russell (RTY=F) both lower by roughly 0.4-0.5%. The catalyst is crude: WTI (CL=F) has jumped 3.10% to $87.11 after President Trump threatened an "Economic D-Day" for Iran, reviving the pressure campaign the administration has waged since April. That's the standout cross-asset move of the morning. The VIX is up nearly 3% to 15.33 — still a low absolute level, but a sign of some fresh caution. Working the other direction, the 10-year yield (^TNX) has eased 1.13% to 4.65% as traders digest a Treasury debt buyback plan, and gold (GC=F) is only modestly lower at $4,534.40 despite the generally risk-off tone, itself a bit of a tell that the move is oil-specific rather than a broad flight from risk.

Crude's 3% jump on Iran headlines is doing more to move futures this morning than the calmer bond market is doing to support them.

Overseas, the picture is split rather than uniformly risk-off. Japan's Nikkei 225 is up 1.2% and Hong Kong's Hang Seng is up 0.8%, while Germany's DAX is down 0.54% and London's FTSE 100 is off 0.29% — Asia catching a bid even as Europe leans lower alongside U.S. futures. Bitcoin (BTC-USD) is a notable outlier within the risk complex, up 3.91% to $72,012 after Trump urged Congress to pass the crypto Clarity Act, a regulatory-framework push that's giving digital assets their own tailwind independent of the broader tone. The dollar index (DX-Y.NYB) is essentially flat, down just 0.07% to 98.74, suggesting FX markets aren't reading this as a major regime shift yet.

Walmart Raises Its Outlook, Alibaba Takes an AI-Spending Hit

One retail giant leans on a tariff refund to defend its guidance; one Chinese tech giant shows what heavy AI investment can do to the bottom line.

Walmart (WMT) --

Walmart hiked its full-year outlook ahead of this morning's fiscal second-quarter report, saying it will use a large tariff refund to keep prices low for consumers. The move is being read as a real-time signal on the health of the consumer and the broader K-shaped economy — a retailer of Walmart's scale defending margins and guidance while explicitly citing tariff relief is a data point worth weighing against the softer risk tone elsewhere in futures this morning.

Alibaba (BABA) --

Alibaba's U.S.-listed shares fell roughly 3% in premarket trading after the company reported a 75% drop in net income for the June quarter, with the company attributing the hit to heavy AI spending. It's a concrete example of the theme flagged elsewhere in today's wire (the Barchart piece questioning whether AI-trade valuations are in bubble territory) — a company spending aggressively on AI infrastructure before that spending shows up in earnings, and the market marking the stock down for it.
