Why It’s Happening

Why are tech and chip stocks falling again today?

The trigger
Nvidia shares fell 5% premarket as the rally following Jensen Huang's return as CEO loses steam, per Yahoo Finance, while Investing.com reports renewed AI-spending concerns hitting U.S. chip stocks broadly; overnight, SK Hynix and Samsung plunged up to 11% in South Korea (Admirals) and China's A-shares dropped sharply with the Shanghai Composite below 3,500 (finance.biggo.com).
Why it hits these stocks
The move is about confidence in the AI capital-spending cycle itself: if hyperscalers and chipmakers are perceived to be overspending or if that spending slows, the memory, networking and AI-infrastructure names most levered to it -- from Nvidia to Korean memory makers to U.S. server builders like Dell -- all reprice together, since their earnings are tied to the same buildout.
Who’s saying what
  • Michael Burry (per Yahoo Finance) Reportedly sold his remaining Nasdaq-100 puts but is eyeing a fresh Nvidia short near $200, saying he is 'long undervalued common stocks' -- a skeptical read on AI valuations even after closing his broad bearish bet.
  • Bill Gunderson, GCG Advisory Partners (per Yahoo Finance) Argues Nvidia is 'the cheapest growth stock in the market at nine times forward earnings,' naming it a top pick for 2027 -- the bullish counter-view.
  • CNBC Reports that AI debt worries are growing, with Big Tech potentially finding it harder to secure financing from debt markets as lenders grow pickier on corporate bonds tied to the AI build-out.
  • Morgan Stanley / Wells Fargo (on Workday, per Investing.com) Morgan Stanley cut its price target citing slowing growth, while Wells Fargo reiterated its rating citing AI momentum and a 'solid Q3' -- analysts disagree on the same stock the same morning.
The other side
Dell beat earnings by $5.6 billion and raised its outlook to $202 billion with a $132 billion AI backlog, per TradingKey -- a sign AI infrastructure demand itself remains strong even as the stock fell on valuation reset.
What settles it
Today's PCE inflation data and GDP report could move the tape further; a cooler PCE print would ease rate pressure on these same richly-valued tech names, while a hot one adds another reason for the selloff to continue.

AppLovin runs an AI-powered advertising business that helps mobile-app developers and publishers find and monetize users, plus a connected-TV ad platform called Wurl. Revenue grew 70% last year to $5.48 billion, and the company turns about 78 cents of every sales dollar into operating profit -- a remarkably high margin for an ad-tech firm. The stock has still fallen 26.7% over the past year and sits 49.7% below its 52-week high, even after a sharp November rebound. The one thing that matters most now: whether this week's pullback is a routine cooldown after a 7x three-year run, or the start of another leg down like the ones that hit the stock twice already this year.

THE BUSINESS

An AI Ad Engine for Mobile Apps

AppLovin's core product runs real-time auctions that help app publishers sell their ad space and help advertisers find users likely to download or buy. The U.S. accounted for $2.83 billion of 2025 revenue and non-U.S. $2.65 billion, with non-U.S. revenue actually growing faster (77% vs. 64%). It also owns Wurl, a connected-TV distribution and ad platform, and Adjust, a marketing-analytics tool.

FINANCIAL HEALTH

Extremely High Margins, Modest Debt

Revenue grew 55.5% and earnings 75.5% over the past year. The company keeps 89 cents of every sales dollar as gross profit and 78 cents as operating profit -- exceptionally high for any company. It holds $3.17 billion in cash against $3.52 billion in debt, roughly balanced, and generates $3.72 billion in free cash flow a year.
