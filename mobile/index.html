Iran-Strait Tensions Push Oil Past $100, Futures Slip on Broad Risk-Off Tape

Brent crude topped $100 a barrel for the first time since July as U.S. and Iran traded strikes near the Strait of Hormuz, with WTI crude (CL=F) up 2.49% to $95.35 on supply-disruption fears -- and equities are showing a genuine flight-to-safety reaction to it, not just noise: S&P futures are down 0.38%, the VIX has jumped 4.45% to 16.42, and gold is up 1.20% alongside a modest bid for Treasurys. The move is broad rather than isolated to energy stocks, with all four major futures contracts in the red and the Dow leading the equity decline given its heavier industrial and financial exposure to a geopolitical supply shock. Meta is a rare bright spot, up over 5% premarket, though today's tape reads primarily as a geopolitics-and-oil story.
