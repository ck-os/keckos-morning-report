THIS MORNING · Premarket

Chip Rout Stabilizes, but Apple and Micron Still Weigh on Tech

Technology is the whole story this morning: it's -0.73% and worth -0.295 points off the S&P on its own, more than explaining the index's -0.09% dip. Apple (-0.89%, -0.065 pts) and Micron (-2.48%, -0.045 pts) are the two biggest single-stock drags, while Health Care's calmer +0.67% (+0.062 pts) is partly offsetting them.

Top Story Memory-Chip Fears Still Pressuring Apple, Micron Why Per Yahoo Finance and Opening Bell Daily, chip stocks just snapped their longest losing streak since 2008 after shedding roughly $2 trillion in value over eight sessions -- but the bounce is partial and uneven. Micron (-2.48% today) remains caught in the memory-pricing and AI-capex-sustainability debate even as Elon Musk reportedly backed its pricing stance, per ad-hoc-news.de, and Loop Capital downgraded Apple (-0.89%) on iPhone shipment concerns. Tech is 40.5% of the S&P and its -0.73% move is costing the index -0.295 points -- more than the index's entire -0.09% move -- while Apple alone accounts for 72% of today's net S&P move and Micron another 50%, per the attribution data.
Also Moving French Curfews Hold as Unrest Lingers, Macron Under Fire Why Per CNBC, French Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu said some areas will stay under curfew after a weekend of rioting, with Macron facing criticism over a Gulf trip taken during the unrest -- part of a broader wave of European cost-of-living protests CNBC also flagged this morning. Not showing up in US futures yet, but European cash markets are feeling it directly -- Germany's DAX is down 0.89% and the UK's FTSE 0.61%; worth watching if it spreads into eurozone sovereign-debt sentiment.
Worth Knowing AI-Infrastructure IPOs Keep Getting Pulled Why MarketWatch reports a South Korean IPO flop is the latest in a string of disappointments for the global AI trade -- three separate data-center flotations backed by Nvidia, Blackstone and SoftBank have been shelved this month alone, as investors question whether AI capex spending is sustainable. It's a sentiment data point, not a price mover yet -- Nvidia itself is only down 0.23% today (-0.019 pts) -- but it's the same capex-doubt theme running underneath this week's chip selloff, worth tracking into Thursday's GDP print.
Why It’s Happening

Why is Tech still dragging on the S&P even as chip stocks try to bounce?

The trigger
Chip stocks snapped their longest losing streak since 2008 overnight, per Opening Bell Daily and Yahoo Finance, after losing roughly $2 trillion in value over the prior eight sessions -- but Korean chipmakers (Samsung, SK Hynix) and European chip names fell again overnight on renewed AI-investment jitters, and Loop Capital downgraded Apple on iPhone shipment concerns this morning.
Why it hits these stocks
The selloff centers on doubts about AI infrastructure spending outpacing returns -- MarketWatch notes three AI-backed data-center IPOs have been pulled this month -- which hits the memory and chip-equipment names (Micron, Coherent, Dell, Sandisk, all down 2.5%-3.2% today) hardest since their earnings are most directly levered to that capex cycle, while Apple's weakness is a separate, company-specific shipment concern.
Who’s saying what
  • Loop Capital Downgraded Apple's stock rating specifically on iPhone shipment concerns, per Investing.com.
  • Elon Musk Reportedly backed Micron's pricing stance amid a Taiwan strike vote and a $600 million patent dispute, per ad-hoc-news.de.
  • Opening Bell Daily / Business Insider Framed the move as chip stocks snapping their longest losing streak since 2008, with Wall Street analysts said to still favor the sector despite the AI-fear-driven stumble.
  • JPMorgan Raised its Apple price target to $360, a counterpoint to Loop Capital's downgrade, per ad-hoc-news.de.
The other side
JPMorgan's raised Apple target and reports that Wall Street analysts still favor the chip sector suggest the move may be more of a pause after a sharp eight-day, $2 trillion drawdown than a lasting reassessment of AI demand.
What settles it
GDP and the Fed's preferred PCE inflation gauge both land Thursday, October 29, alongside the FOMC decision on Wednesday, October 28 -- any of the three could reset the growth and rate backdrop the AI-capex debate is being argued against.
