Oil Shock Escalates as Brent Tops $109; AI Slowdown Call Still Weighing on Tech

Overnight, Saudi Arabia shut down the East-West pipeline that bypasses the Strait of Hormuz and Iran said it downed a U.S. drone over the strait, pushing Brent above $109 and WTI crude (CL=F, $104.21, +4.16%) sharply higher as the Mideast conflict intensifies -- CNBC reports U.S. diesel has now hit a record above $6/gallon. That's compounding with the AI-safety backlash from Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and OpenAI's Sam Altman, both publicly urging an industry slowdown, which is still hammering chip and AI-infrastructure names and dragging Nasdaq futures (NQ=F, -1.71%) far harder than the Dow (YM=F, +0.41%) or Russell (RTY=F, +0.20%). The VIX has jumped another 11.74% to 17.70 and gold is down 1.13% even as equities slide, a combination that doesn't fit a clean flight-to-safety script -- this reads more like an energy-cost shock and a single-sector selloff than a broad panic.

The Standout WTI crude (CL=F) is up 4.16% to $104.21 as Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline shutdown and an escalating Iran-U.S. standoff near the Strait of Hormuz threaten global oil flows, with Brent above $109 and U.S. diesel at a record.
The Outlier Nasdaq futures (NQ=F, -1.71%) are falling far harder than the Dow (YM=F, +0.41%) or Russell (RTY=F, +0.20%), as the AI-slowdown warnings from Anthropic's Dario Amodei and OpenAI's Sam Altman concentrate the damage in the tech-heavy index while industrials and small caps hold up.
The Rest of the Tape The VIX has jumped 11.74% to 17.70 alongside falling gold (-1.13%), a mismatched signature that suggests this is an energy-cost and single-sector shock rather than a broad flight-to-safety move.
CBOE Volatility Index (VIX), 1-month. The VIX has now jumped more than 11% in back-to-back premarket sessions, a second straight day of outsized moves as the oil shock and AI-slowdown story compound.
A story that's really about one sector's valuation reset is showing up almost entirely in one index.

The VIX is the clearest confirmation that today's move is broad enough to matter -- up 11.74% to 17.70, its second straight double-digit jump, while gold is falling 1.13% rather than rising, a combination that breaks the classic flight-to-safety pattern (falling equities usually pair with rising, not falling, gold). That split -- fear gauge up, safe-haven metal down -- reads less like systemic panic and more like a rotation: money moving out of AI-adjacent tech and into energy-linked and value names, consistent with Russell futures (+0.20%) and Dow futures (+0.41%) holding up while Nasdaq futures lag. There's no major scheduled U.S. data release today to compete for attention -- the next big prints are Wednesday's FOMC decision and retail sales report -- so today's tape is being driven almost entirely by the two live news threads above rather than a data surprise.
