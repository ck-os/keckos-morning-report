Yields Hit 2007 Highs, Oil Jumps on Saudi Pipeline Risk as Fed Decision Looms

The 10-year Treasury yield has pushed to its highest level since 2007, CNBC reports, as markets increasingly price in a rate hike at Wednesday's FOMC decision -- a CNBC survey out this morning shows respondents now expecting at least two hikes over the next year, with most citing inflation concerns broader than energy alone. Oil is adding to the pressure, up 1.59% to $103.00 on CNBC reporting that Saudi Arabia is racing to restart a key pipeline before an estimated five-to-seven-day inventory cushion runs out. Futures, gold, and bitcoin are all lower together while the dollar firms -- a broad, same-direction retreat consistent with a market bracing for a hawkish surprise rather than an isolated move in any one asset.

The Standout The 10-year yield's climb to its highest since 2007 is the load-bearing story this morning, with CNBC linking it directly to rising odds of a rate hike at Wednesday's FOMC meeting.
The Outlier Crude is up 1.59% to $103.00 even as equities and other risk assets fall, a divergence CNBC ties to fears that Saudi Arabia's key pipeline could stay offline past its inventory cushion.
The Rest of the Tape Equity futures, gold (-0.81%), and bitcoin (-1.61%) are all lower together while the dollar firms, a broad risk-off tilt across asset classes rather than a single sector's problem.
Bitcoin (BTC-USD), 1-month. Bitcoin's 1.61% drop today, alongside falling gold, underscores the broad-based nature of today's pullback across both traditional and digital risk assets.
Rising yields on a hawkish Fed repricing and rising oil on a Saudi pipeline scare are squeezing risk assets from two directions at once.

S&P futures are down a modest 0.13% and Nasdaq futures off 0.11%, but the mover with real breadth this morning is cross-asset rather than confined to one index: gold is down 0.81% to $4,316.50 and bitcoin has dropped 1.61% to $76,901, both falling alongside equities rather than acting as the usual safe-haven offset -- a signature more consistent with real rates rising broadly than with a classic flight-to-safety rotation. The Fed's own rate decision lands tomorrow, Wednesday, September 16, alongside Retail Sales the same morning, so today's premarket move reads as positioning ahead of that pair of releases rather than a reaction to news already in hand; a hawkish surprise or a soft retail print could swing the tape meaningfully into and through the announcement.
