Why It’s Happening

Why are chip and tech stocks bouncing back again today?

The trigger
CNBC reports Nvidia shares are rising alongside a broader premarket rally after last week's Nasdaq correction, and Reuters reports Morgan Stanley's trading desk turned its most bullish on stocks since April following last week's selloff; MarketWatch also reports the Trump administration is pushing for Jensen Huang's reinstatement at Nvidia.
Why it hits these stocks
Last week's selloff was driven by positioning and valuation worries around AI infrastructure spending, not a change in underlying chip demand -- when a drop is sentiment-driven rather than fundamentals-driven, a sharp reversal is common once a major desk like Morgan Stanley signals the selling is overdone and buyers step back in.
Who’s saying what
  • Morgan Stanley (trading desk), via Reuters Turned most bullish on stocks since April after last week's selloff, signaling the firm sees the pullback as a buying opportunity rather than a trend change.
  • Yardeni Research, via Stocktwits Expects the AI investment boom to continue despite capacity constraints and says a recession remains unlikely.
  • Michael Burry, via GuruFocus Reportedly warned that Apple, Costco and Walmart could face a delayed selloff, drawing parallels to prior cycles where leading stocks stayed resilient for months before declining.
  • Goldman Sachs, via CEO Economy Estimates trend-following funds sold about $57 billion of stocks in the past month and could sell up to $80.6 billion more, a mechanical flow risk separate from the fundamental debate.
The other side
JPMorgan's trading desk, per Bloomberg, dropped its bullish view on stocks after the S&P's correction last week, showing the two biggest desks on the Street don't agree on whether the bounce is durable.
What settles it
Dell and HP Enterprise report earnings this week, and Wednesday's PCE and GDP data are the next concrete tests of whether AI-infrastructure demand and the broader economy support this rebound.

Why it’s in focus Dell shares are up 8.15% premarket after TradingKey reported a $95 billion AI infrastructure backlog pushing the stock above $500 last session, with Benzinga noting the move comes alongside rival HP Enterprise also rallying on signs of AI server price hikes -- all of it set against Dell's own Q3 earnings due Tuesday, November 24.

Dell sells computers and data-center equipment -- everything from laptops to the massive AI servers companies like OpenAI's rivals buy to train artificial intelligence. Its server and storage business (Infrastructure Solutions Group) has roughly doubled down on AI demand, growing revenue 39.5% last year and now making up more than half of Dell's $113.5 billion in total sales. The stock has been a wild ride: up 135% over the past year but still 49% below its 52-week high after a sharp pullback tied to broader worries that AI spending has outrun what it's earning back. Tuesday's earnings report is the next checkpoint for whether that AI server demand is translating into real profit growth.

THE BUSINESS

Dell Is Now Mostly an AI Server Company

Dell's Infrastructure Solutions Group -- servers, storage, and networking gear, much of it built for AI workloads -- brought in $60.8 billion last year, 54% of total revenue and growing 39.5%. Its older PC and laptop business (Client Solutions) brought in $51 billion, growing a much slower 5.4%. The AI server business has become the company's main growth engine.

FINANCIAL HEALTH

Revenue Surging, but Thin Profit Margins

Revenue grew 57.7% in the most recent period and earnings growth was especially strong, but Dell only keeps about 7.5 cents of profit for every sales dollar (a 7.5% profit margin) -- hardware is a low-margin business. The company holds $11.6 billion in cash against $35.3 billion in debt, and still generates $6 billion in free cash flow a year, the money left over after running the business and investing in it.

Analysts Rate It a Buy, but Price Targets Vary Widely

Of 25 analysts covering Dell, the consensus rating is "buy" (third-party opinion), with price targets ranging from $403 to $700, averaging $578.88 -- well above today's $300.69 price. Dell's reaction to earnings has swung wildly: a 37.86% jump in May 2026 came after results beat expectations by 63.8%, while a 13.72% drop in late 2024 came despite a smaller 4.2% beat, showing the stock's reaction depends heavily on the details beyond the headline number.

HISTORY & ODDS

Rare Setup Has Always Rebounded Before, But on Thin Data

Dell has only hit "down 50%+ from its high and below its 200-day average" 4 distinct times since 2017. In the 3 of those with complete data, Dell was higher 3 months later every time, averaging a 52.43% gain, and higher a year later every time too, averaging 121.96%. That is a very small sample -- just 3 instances -- so it's suggestive at best, not a reliable statistic.

THE BULL CASE
  • The AI server business (Infrastructure Solutions Group) grew revenue 39.5% last year and now drives more than half of total sales.
  • A reported $95 billion AI infrastructure backlog, per TradingKey, suggests revenue already booked for future quarters.
  • Wall Street's average price target of $578.88 is nearly double the current price, and the stock has beaten earnings expectations in 7 of its last 8 reports.
THE BEAR CASE
  • Profit margins are thin (7.5% net margin) -- hardware is a low-margin business even as revenue grows fast.
  • The stock is down 48.8% from its 52-week high, with SMBtech reporting insider selling and customer backlash tied to the broader AI-spending unwind.
  • Debt ($35.3 billion) is more than triple cash on hand ($11.6 billion), leaving less room for error if AI demand slows.
