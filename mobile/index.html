Why It’s Happening

Why are chip stocks falling again this morning?

The trigger
Nvidia shares are down 2.19% premarket as the fallout from CEO Jensen Huang's resignation continues -- CNBC reports growing calls from tech executives and major shareholders for Huang to return, while the Washington Post reports President Trump's personal Nvidia stake and Treasury Secretary Bessent's comments restricting advanced chip sales to China are keeping the company under political scrutiny, all two days ahead of Nvidia's earnings report and the Anthropic IPO.
Why it hits these stocks
Nvidia's leadership uncertainty and the unresolved China export question create doubt about near-term AI chip demand and governance, which spreads to suppliers and competitors (Broadcom, AMD) and optical/memory names (Lumentum -5.47%, Coherent -5.57%, SanDisk -5.41%) that depend on the same AI infrastructure buildout -- per TipRanks and Bloomberg, Asian chip stocks are falling for the same reason.
Who’s saying what
  • CNBC Reports that tech executives and major shareholders are increasingly calling for Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia CEO.
  • Senator Maria Cantwell Per CNBC, urged Commerce Secretary Lutnick to stop AI chip sales to China ahead of the Xi meeting, citing his close ties to Huang.
  • Scott Bessent Per Yahoo Finance, said Trump made it 'very clear' China gets no Nvidia chips above H100-level performance.
  • Seeking Alpha Argued the Lumentum selloff 'makes less sense' after the company's own earnings, suggesting some of the decline may be overdone sector-wide contagion rather than company-specific news.
The other side
Simply Wall St argues Coherent could be 44% undervalued following its $20 billion AI deal, and Morgan Stanley strategists are telling clients to go 'max overweight' on stocks in one of the most bullish year-ahead outlooks published so far -- suggesting some of today's weakness may be sentiment-driven rather than a reassessment of AI demand itself.
What settles it
Nvidia's earnings report, due Wednesday, November 18, is the next scheduled event that could confirm or ease these concerns.

Everpure sells data storage hardware and software that companies use to run their computer systems -- think of it as digital filing cabinets built for speed and reliability. The business is growing at a healthy clip, with revenue up 16% to $3.66 billion last year, but the stock has fallen 34% from its 52-week high even as the shares are still up almost 22% over the past year. The one thing that matters most right now: the stock reports earnings again on November 18, just two days from today, after a pattern of sharp post-earnings swings in both directions.

Everpure Sells Hardware and Subscriptions Equally

Product sales (storage hardware) made up $1.97 billion of revenue last year, growing 16%, while service and subscription revenue brought in $1.69 billion, growing 15.1% -- nearly an even split. The company also sells internationally, where revenue grew faster (24.7%) than in the U.S. (11.7%), showing where the newer growth is coming from.
