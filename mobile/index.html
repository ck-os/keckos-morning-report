THIS MORNING · Premarket

Communication Rally Holds as Tech Stabilizes, Chip Worry Lingers

The same Communication Services rally from earlier this morning is still carrying the tape -- up 2.96% and worth 184% of the S&P's +0.16% move -- while Tech has flipped flat (-0.09%) as Apple's iPhone Duo preorder pop offsets a continued Micron slide. That's the real story: yesterday's chip selloff is cooling, not reversing, and the index is up only because one sector is doing nearly all the work.

Top Story Communication Still Carrying the Index, Tech Flips Flat Why Fox jumped 3.63% after Bank of America double-upgraded it to Buy on Roku deal progress, and EchoStar added 1.86% on JPMorgan's Overweight resumption -- Bloomberg and WSJ both describe money rotating into telecom/media names as a 'defensive tilt' following Wednesday's AI-stock rout, even as Netflix fell 3.59% on its own. Communication is 10% of the S&P but its 2.96% move accounts for 184% of the index's entire +0.16% gain today -- Tech, at 40.5% of the index, is basically flat (-0.09%, -0.036 pts), so one smaller sector is doing all the lifting.
Also Moving Micron Keeps Sliding on Chinese Memory Competition Why Micron fell another 2.1% this morning; Yahoo Finance flagged that Chinese memory competition is 'altering the investment case' for the stock, and The Motley Fool noted it's now 25% below its June record -- the same chip-specific worry from Wednesday's selloff that the broader market has mostly moved past. Micron alone is pulling 106% of Technology's sector contribution negative (-0.038 pts) -- its drag is nearly the whole reason Tech, 40.5% of the index, isn't adding to today's gain despite Apple's strength.
Worth Knowing Tesla's Roadster Reveal Adds a Second Lift to Discretionary Why Tesla unveiled its long-delayed, thruster-powered Roadster on Thursday, and shares are up 3.19% this morning -- CNBC and multiple outlets call it a flashy engineering demo, though the vehicle has missed every prior delivery target since 2017. Tesla is responsible for 66% of Consumer Discretionary's sector contribution and 30% of the S&P's total move today -- a single stock's reveal is moving the index more than most of the other ten sectors combined.
Why It’s Happening

Why is Communication Services still leading the market, and has the chip selloff actually ended?

The trigger
Fox Corporation jumped after Bank of America upgraded it to Buy on Roku deal progress, and EchoStar rose after JPMorgan resumed coverage at Overweight with a $144 target citing a spectrum-sale value opportunity -- both concrete, named catalysts distinct from Wednesday's broader chip-sector rout.
Why it hits these stocks
With chip-sector sentiment still shaky (Micron down 2.1% this morning on Chinese memory competition, per Yahoo Finance), money is rotating into telecom/media names seen as less exposed to AI-capex anxiety -- Bloomberg describes this directly as a 'defensive tilt from AI.' It's a rotation within equities, not a broad risk-off move, since Netflix (-3.59%) shows the rally is selective by name, not sector-wide.
Who’s saying what
  • JPMorgan Resumed EchoStar coverage at Overweight with a $144 price target, citing its spectrum sale and a value opportunity.
  • Bank of America Double-upgraded Fox to Buy, saying its Roku deal 'changes the equation' for the stock.
  • Yahoo Finance (reporting) Reported that Chinese memory competition is altering the investment case for Micron, the clearest named cause of its continued decline.
  • Dan Ives, Wedbush Argued Apple's new Meta AI partnership could unlock $75 per share in AI value, a bullish counterpoint on the Tech side even as Micron drags the sector.
The other side
Netflix fell 3.59% the same morning Communication Services rallied, and Micron and AMD (-1.77%) are still falling -- the rotation into Communication is name-specific (Fox, EchoStar) rather than a sign the underlying chip-sector worry has resolved.
What settles it
Next week's FOMC decision (Wednesday, October 28) and PCE/GDP the following day will show whether this rotation holds or chip-sector pressure reasserts itself across Tech.
