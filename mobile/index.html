SpaceX and Supermicro Reignite the AI Trade

SpaceX's earnings beat and Supermicro's raised sales outlook are pulling chip and server names back up after last week's slide, and Tech's 0.73% move is the single biggest reason the S&P is green at all. Oil's 2.5% drop and a cooler 10-year are easing the pressure on those same growth names, even as French bond stress is a separate, contained story for now.

Top Story SpaceX Earnings Beat Revives the AI Trade Why SpaceX reported surging quarterly revenue and Elon Musk floated a possible SpaceX-Tesla tie-up, while Supermicro raised its own sales outlook roughly 27% above its prior forecast on AI server demand, per CNBC and Investor's Business Daily -- together reversing last week's chip-stock slide. Technology is 40.5% of the S&P and up 0.73% premarket, contributing +0.295 points -- 128% of the index's entire 0.23% move, with Nvidia (NVDA, $219.40, +1.35% premarket) alone worth 47% of that.
Also Moving Netflix Slides on Weak Ad Revenue, Profit Outlook Why Netflix shares are down as Oppenheimer cut its price target following what WTVB and Investing.com describe as a disappointing ad-revenue showing and softer profit outlook, intensifying investor worries about its ad-tier ramp. Netflix's -6.07% move drags Communication Services to -0.22%, but the sector is only 10% of the index -- a -0.022 point hit, nowhere near enough to offset Tech's gain.
Worth Knowing French 10-Year Yield Hits 5%, Highest Since 2002 Why Reuters and CNBC report French 10-year yields crossed 5% as political unrest and protests deepen fiscal concerns, with Carrefour separately cutting guidance and citing the same unrest. Not showing up in US futures yet -- DX-Y.NYB is flat at 101.76 and the 10-year Treasury yield is actually down to 5.12% this morning, but a widening transatlantic yield gap is worth watching if French stress spreads.
Why It’s Happening

Why are chip and AI-server stocks rallying again today?

The trigger
SpaceX reported a quarterly revenue beat and Elon Musk raised the idea of a SpaceX-Tesla combination, per CNBC, while Supermicro raised its own revenue outlook roughly 27% above its prior forecast citing AI demand, lifting shares about 12%.
Why it hits these stocks
Both are read as fresh evidence that AI infrastructure spending -- data centers, servers, and the chips that power them -- is still accelerating rather than slowing, which is exactly the growth story that had been in doubt after last week's chip selloff; Supermicro and Dell (+4.54%) sit directly in the AI-server supply chain Nvidia and Broadcom depend on for demand.
Who’s saying what
  • CNBC SpaceX shares jumped on surging quarterly revenue growth, with Musk hinting at a possible SpaceX-Tesla merger.
  • Investor's Business Daily Called Supermicro's blowout sales guidance a key driver reviving AI stocks broadly, alongside SpaceX's results.
  • Ken Griffin, Citadel (via MarketWatch) Said AI stocks are probably in a bubble but argued they might still be worth holding, acknowledging the risk without calling for an exit.
  • Morgan Stanley (via Stocktwits) Warned that momentum in chip stocks specifically is fading and favors rotating into hyperscalers instead.
The other side
Morgan Stanley's caution on chip-stock momentum, plus Microsoft's (-0.75%) and Apple's (-0.57%) declines even as the broader Tech sector rallies, suggest the enthusiasm isn't universal across the AI complex.
What settles it
Nvidia's own earnings next week is the next real test of whether the AI-demand story these headlines describe actually holds up in hard numbers.
The Briefing
Tech is carrying the index again -- but it's Supermicro and SpaceX doing the lifting, not the usual mega-caps, with Microsoft and Apple both actually in the red.
