Why is Technology leading the S&P again today, and who's actually driving it?

The trigger

Why it hits these stocks
Both stories funnel fresh capital and bullish sentiment specifically into chip and AI-infrastructure names -- BofA's call directly repriced memory-chip demand expectations, while Meta's raise is a literal cash commitment from one of the largest AI spenders into the chipmakers' order books, which is why Micron, Nvidia, Broadcom and AMD are all higher even as Apple drags on separate bearish pre-earnings analyst notes.

The other side
Apple, the single biggest mega-cap drag today at -1.4% (-0.103 pts), is falling on bearish analyst notes ahead of next week's earnings, per TipRanks -- a reminder the chip/AI rally isn't lifting every Technology name.
What settles it

THE BUSINESS

Ross Sells Discounted Name Brands, at Scale

Ross Stores operates off-price retail stores that sell designer and brand-name clothing, shoes, accessories and home goods at a discount, through its Ross Dress for Less and dd's DISCOUNTS chains. It targets middle-income and lower-to-moderate-income shoppers across the US. A detailed revenue breakdown by business line wasn't available this edition, but the company operates as essentially a single retail format at scale, with 111,000 employees.

FINANCIAL HEALTH

Sales and Profit Both Growing Faster Than Last Year

Revenue grew 13.3% over the past year and earnings grew a much faster 70.5%, meaning profit is growing far quicker than sales -- a sign of improving efficiency. The company turns 33 cents of every sales dollar into gross profit and keeps 13.6 cents as operating profit before taxes. It holds $4.29 billion in cash against $4.74 billion in debt -- a roughly even balance -- and generated $2.0 billion in free cash flow (cash left over after running and investing in the business) over the last year.

Week in Review · Oct 19-23

AI Leaders Leak as Money Runs for Shelter

The quick-rescue reflex is weakening. In the latest four weeks the AI basket had five drops of 1.5% or more (two in the four weeks before), and only two of the five recovered even half within three days. On the two days the report led with someone spending more, the basket still fell on average (-1.2%); doubts about spending led five days. Small samples, but the pattern of spending news reliably lifting AI stocks has not shown up this month.

What the trend suggests

The trend points to a market rotating rather than retreating, but rotating toward shelter: if yields keep easing, rate-sensitive and defensive groups have room to keep doing the lifting while AI leaders work off their excess. The other path is the old reflex returning: a convincing spending or earnings beat that the AI basket holds for days, not hours, would put the leaders back in charge. Market State has read Losing Footing for two Fridays, so the burden of proof sits with the bulls for now.

What would change it: Wednesday's Fed decision and Thursday's PCE inflation print: a hawkish surprise that sends the 10-year back above 5.2% would undercut the rotation, while an AI-basket rally that holds three straight days after spending news would revive the rescue pattern.
