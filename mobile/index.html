THIS MORNING · Premarket

Yields Keep Spiking on Fed Regime-Change Fears, But Stocks Shrug It Off

The 10-year Treasury yield has pushed to 5.11% (+0.94%) as CNBC reports Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh's 'regime change' at the central bank is meeting internal resistance, with his emerging framework suggesting further rate hikes remain possible -- a genuinely unusual dynamic given equity futures are still higher (S&P futures +0.35%, Nasdaq futures +0.68%) rather than selling off with rates. Gold is up 0.99% to $4,340.50, which alongside a softer dollar (-0.31%) looks more like inflation-hedging than classic risk-off positioning, while VIX has dropped to 15.03, within a point of its 6-month low of 14.25 -- a level that doesn't square with a market genuinely worried about tighter policy. Separately, easing Middle East tensions (Iran offering to reopen the Strait of Hormuz) has crude down 2.37% to $92.37, providing some offsetting relief on the inflation front even as yields climb.

The Standout CNBC reports Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's push for a policy 'regime change' is hitting resistance internally, with his framework leaving further rate hikes on the table -- the 10-year yield has responded by climbing to 5.11%.

The Rest of the Tape Equity futures are broadly higher (Nasdaq futures +0.68% leading, VIX down to 15.03 near 6-month lows) even as yields spike, a mismatch worth watching given the CNN Fear & Greed Index still reads 'fear' at 36.
VIX, 1-month. VIX has fallen 4.08% to 15.03, within a point of its 6-month low of 14.25, even as Treasury yields climb on Fed policy uncertainty.
Rising real yields on hawkish Fed rhetoric would typically pressure equity futures and lift volatility, not the reverse -- today they aren't.

The cross-asset picture offers a partial explanation for the disconnect: gold is up 0.99% to $4,340.50 and the dollar is down 0.31%, a combination more consistent with inflation-hedging than outright risk-off positioning, while crude has dropped 2.37% to $92.37 after CNBC reported Iran has offered to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days and restart nuclear talks -- an easing of a separate geopolitical risk that's giving the tape some room to absorb the yield spike without panicking. No major US economic data is scheduled today; the next releases are PCE and GDP on September 30, giving the market five more sessions to digest the Warsh story before a fresh inflation read arrives.
