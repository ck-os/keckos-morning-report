Crude Slides 4.5% on Iran De-Escalation Signals as AI Rally Extends

Crude oil is down 4.5% to $90.34 as Zelenskyy's call for an 'energy ceasefire' and softer rhetoric around the Strait of Hormuz ease some of the geopolitical risk premium that's been built into energy markets. At the same time, fresh bullish momentum in AI names -- Meta's new Muse model and continued Anthropic/OpenAI competition among the headlines -- is pushing Nasdaq-sensitive sectors higher even as broader futures sit roughly flat to slightly negative. Fed officials remain split, with Boston Fed's Collins warning inflation could stay 'notably' above target after backing a rate hike, keeping the 10-year yield elevated near 4.97%.

The Standout Crude oil is sliding 4.5% to $90.34 as Zelenskyy's push for an 'energy ceasefire' and Iran's floated conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz cool the geopolitical risk premium built into oil prices.

Oil's 4.5% slide reads as a geopolitical relief signal, not a growth scare -- gold is softer too, not spiking, alongside it.

The cross-current holding equity futures in check is the Fed: Boston Fed President Susan Collins warned this morning that there's an 'increased likelihood' inflation stays 'notably' above the 2% target, a notably hawkish tone that's keeping the 10-year yield near 4.97% (+0.10 points) even as oil eases. That's a genuine tension for the tape -- lower energy prices would normally ease inflation pressure, but a Fed official flagging persistent inflation risk anyway is keeping bond yields firm and small caps (Russell futures -0.42%) under more pressure than the large-cap indices. No major scheduled US economic release lands today -- the next data points on the calendar are GDP and the Fed's preferred PCE gauge on September 30, followed by the jobs report on October 2 -- so today's tape is being driven by the Iran/Ukraine de-escalation story and Fed commentary rather than a data print.
