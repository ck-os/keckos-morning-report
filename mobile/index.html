Crude Jumps, Yields Ease, Futures Tread Water

A firmer oil market and a cooler bond market are pulling in different directions this morning, while equity futures sit little changed ahead of the open.

U.S. equity futures are modestly softer heading into Thursday's session, with S&P futures (ES=F) off 0.14% to 7,728.00, Nasdaq futures (NQ=F) down 0.23% to 29,545.75, Dow futures (YM=F) down 0.19%, and small-cap Russell futures (RTY=F) lagging at -0.34%. The more notable move is in commodities and rates: WTI crude (CL=F) is up 2.59% to $86.68, while the 10-year Treasury yield (^TNX) has eased 1.13% to 4.65%, a move CNBC ties to traders digesting a new Treasury debt buyback plan after yields pulled back sharply Wednesday. The VIX is up 1.48% to 15.11 -- still a low absolute level, but a mild uptick in caution. Gold (GC=F) is down slightly to $4,545.30 and the dollar index (DX-Y.NYB) is little changed at 98.69.

Crude's 2.59% jump and a cooling 10-year yield are pulling the tape in different directions, while equity futures sit largely flat.

Overseas, Asia outperformed Europe overnight: Japan's Nikkei 225 gained 1.2% to 66,216.79 and Hong Kong's Hang Seng added 0.8% to 25,698.49, while Germany's DAX slipped 0.56% to 25,982.14 and London's FTSE 100 eased 0.35% to 10,709.71. Bitcoin (BTC-USD) is the standout mover in the risk-asset space, up 3.65% to $71,831 after President Trump urged Congress to pass the crypto Clarity Act, a regulatory framework bill for the sector. Elsewhere, rhetoric around Iran continues to escalate, with Trump threatening what he called an 'Economic D-Day,' though a former Iranian central bank adviser pushed back on claims the economy is collapsing -- a geopolitical thread to watch but not yet visibly priced into oil's move today, which CNBC and Yahoo coverage don't directly tie to it.

Walmart Lifts Outlook, Alibaba Slides on AI Spend

Two large-cap earnings reports are setting the tone in premarket trading, one on the U.S. consumer and one on AI infrastructure spending.

Walmart (WMT) --

Walmart raised its full-year outlook ahead of its fiscal second-quarter report Thursday, saying it will use a large tariff refund to keep prices low for consumers. CNBC frames the report as a read on both consumer health and the broader K-shaped economy -- a name to watch for how a value-oriented retailer is navigating tariff costs relative to peers.

Alibaba (BABA) --

Alibaba's U.S.-listed shares were volatile in premarket trading after the company reported a 75% drop in net income for the June quarter, which CNBC attributes to heavy AI and cloud infrastructure spending. The report adds a data point to the broader AI-capex debate -- a company spending aggressively on AI capacity now showing that cost weighing directly on near-term profitability.
