PPI Prints In-Line but Oil's Break Above $100 Drives Yields to Multi-Year Highs, Tech Leads Futures Lower

Producer prices rose 0.4% in August, exactly matching the Dow Jones consensus, but the actual market mover this morning is crude oil above $100/barrel as CNBC reports traders bracing for a prolonged Iran conflict -- WTI is up 4.13% to $100.02, and CNBC directly ties this morning's jump in Treasury yields (the 10-year now at 4.88%, +0.99%) to oil outweighing the tame inflation data. The ECB added to the unease by hiking to 2.5% on inflation and Iran-war risk, and the combination is pressuring the rate-sensitive Nasdaq futures (-0.92%) harder than the Dow (-0.13%), with the VIX up 3.89% to 17.10 confirming the risk-off tilt is broad rather than confined to one corner of the market.

The Standout WTI crude's 4.13% jump to $100.02 is today's biggest cross-asset mover, with CNBC explicitly attributing the rise in Treasury yields to oil outweighing a tame PPI print.
The Outlier PPI rose 0.4% in August exactly as expected -- a non-event on its own -- yet yields still climbed to multi-year highs, underscoring that oil, not inflation data, is driving the rate path this morning.
The Rest of the Tape Nasdaq futures (-0.92%) are underperforming the Dow (-0.13%) as the higher-yield, higher-oil backdrop weighs hardest on richly-valued tech, while gold's 0.48% dip and the dollar's 0.26% gain round out a broadly cautious, rates-led morning.

Oil breaking $100 on Iran-war fears is outweighing a tame PPI print -- and pushing the 10-year to a multi-year high in the process.

Nasdaq futures are the weakest of the four major contracts this morning, down 0.92% versus a comparatively modest 0.13% decline in Dow futures -- a gap that lines up with the yield story above, since the Nasdaq's heavier concentration in long-duration, richly-valued tech names makes it more sensitive to a rising discount rate than the more value-tilted Dow. That reaction is showing up broadly rather than in isolation: the VIX is up 3.89% to 17.10 and gold is down 0.48% even as yields rise, a combination consistent with a rates-led repricing rather than a pure flight-to-safety trade. Today's PPI print has already landed and come in as expected, so the next real test for this tape is tomorrow's CPI release, followed by the Fed's rate decision and Retail Sales both due September 16 -- with oil's trajectory now arguably the bigger swing factor for how those numbers get read.
