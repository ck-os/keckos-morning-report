Oil Slides a Third Day as Saudi Supply Eases Fears; Futures Edge Higher

Crude is down 5.4% to $96.42 as Saudi Arabia signals additional supply to offset Houthi-related disruption fears, per CNBC -- a real relief valve for an economy that's been bracing for record diesel prices to ripple through trucking and rail. That drop is showing up as risk-friendly across the board: futures are modestly higher (S&P futures +0.14%, Nasdaq futures +0.35%), the 10-year yield is down 1.18%, and the VIX is off 0.78% to 15.32, all consistent with an easing energy-cost worry rather than a fresh scare. Gold's still up slightly (+0.32%) and bitcoin is up 2.29%, so the move isn't a clean single-direction risk-on signal, but the oil-led relief is the clearest throughline in today's headlines.

The Standout WTI crude fell 5.39% to $96.42, its third straight down day, as CNBC reports Saudi Arabia is moving to add supply and ease fears of a Houthi-driven disruption to the Bab el-Mandeb Strait.

There's no major U.S. economic release scheduled today -- the calendar's next entries (PCE and GDP) don't land until September 30 -- so today's session is set up to trade mostly off the oil move and any further headlines out of Saudi Arabia/the Gulf rather than a scheduled data catalyst.
