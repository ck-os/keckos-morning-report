Why It’s Happening

Why is Nvidia dragging the whole market down again today?

The trigger
CNBC and the WSJ report Nvidia's board discussed removing CEO Jensen Huang after a second reported affair came to light, with Fox Business and Barron's saying the stock fell more than 7% premarket on the news.
Why it hits these stocks
Nvidia is the AI trade's dominant chipmaker and carries 7.6% of the S&P's weight -- more than any other single stock -- so leadership uncertainty at the top of that company doesn't just hit NVDA shares, it unsettles the entire AI-hardware chain investors have priced off Nvidia's continuity, including memory and server-infrastructure names mentioned alongside it.
Who’s saying what
  • Dan Ives, Wedbush (via TipRanks) Argues a Huang ouster could cut roughly 25% of Nvidia's value, framing him as inseparable from the company's AI-era success.
  • Bernstein (via Yahoo Finance) Cut its Nvidia price target in half to $200, citing the succession risk as a serious, not cosmetic, threat to the stock's valuation.
  • Barron's Notes AMD and Broadcom are rising specifically because of Nvidia's turmoil, suggesting money is rotating within the AI trade rather than leaving it.
  • TipRanks (via Anthropic claim) Reports Anthropic's claim that its Mythos model ran on AMD chips in about three days, raising a separate question about whether Nvidia's CUDA software advantage is more vulnerable than the market assumed.
The other side
AMD and Broadcom climbing rather than falling suggests markets see this as a Nvidia-specific governance shock, not a verdict on AI spending or demand broadly -- AP noted AI stocks had nearly erased last week's selloff just one day before this news broke.
What settles it
Any official Nvidia board statement confirming or denying a leadership change would settle whether this stays a contained, one-stock event or spreads further through the AI trade.

Why it’s in focus Entergy shares rose 3.5% premarket after a Q3 earnings beat (a 16.5% surprise over estimates) and a dividend increase, per Yahoo Finance, with coverage also pointing to a rate-plan extension and Entergy's growing role supplying power to data centers.

Entergy is a regulated electric utility serving 3.1 million customers across Arkansas, Louisiana, Mississippi and Texas, including New Orleans. The business is steady and predictable: 2025 revenue grew 9.2% to $12.9 billion, almost all of it from regulated utility sales. The stock sits near its 52-week high, up 18.2% over the past year, as investors bet that AI data centers will need a lot more electricity and Entergy is positioned to supply it. The one thing that matters most now: whether that AI-driven demand story can keep justifying a stock trading at 26 times earnings for a regulated utility.

THE BUSINESS

Entergy Is Almost Entirely a Power Utility

Entergy makes nearly all its money generating and selling electricity to households and businesses across four Southern states -- the Utility segment brought in $12.9 billion of its $12.95 billion total revenue in 2025, growing 9.2%. A small corporate/other segment makes up the rest and actually shrank 20.4%.

FINANCIAL HEALTH

Growing Fast for a Utility, But Spending Heavily

Revenue grew 14.5% and earnings grew 29.4% over the past year -- unusually fast for a regulated utility, tied to the AI power-demand story. It keeps a healthy 25% operating margin (25 cents of every sales dollar before interest and taxes) but free cash flow is negative $5.1 billion, meaning it's spending more building new power infrastructure than it's bringing in -- common for utilities investing in growth, but it adds to a debt load of $34.8 billion against $4.2 billion cash.

HISTORY & ODDS

Setups Like This Have Usually Paid Off

In 83 past instances when Entergy traded within 10% of its 52-week high and above its 200-day average, the stock was higher three months later 64.6% of the time (average +3.2%) and higher a year later 75.3% of the time (average +11.6%) -- though the range was wide, from -38.6% to +93.6%, so these setups haven't always worked out.

THE BULL CASE
  • Revenue and earnings growth (14.5% and 29.4%) are unusually strong for a regulated utility, driven by rising demand, including from data centers.
  • Analyst consensus is buy with 21 analysts covering it, and the stock has beaten earnings expectations in most recent quarters.
  • Historically, similar high-and-uptrend setups have led to gains three-quarters of the time over the following year.
THE BEAR CASE
  • Free cash flow is deeply negative (-$5.1 billion), meaning the company is financing heavy growth spending with debt rather than its own cash generation.
  • Total debt of $34.8 billion against just $4.2 billion cash is a large leverage load for a regulated, rate-capped business.
  • At roughly 26 times trailing earnings, the stock already prices in a lot of future growth -- a report flagged whether its valuation has outrun what its dividend can support.
