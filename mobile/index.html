Top Story Blue Wave Hits AI Trade; Chip Names Tumble Technology is 38.2% of the S&P and its -1.13% move costs the index -0.43 points -- the single biggest drag today, even with the index essentially flat.
Also Moving Financials and Health Care Catch the Rotation Why Money moving out of AI/tech names is landing in sectors seen as regulation-light winners from divided government -- Financials (+1.10%) and Health Care (+0.71%) are both among the day's top contributing sectors, per the index attribution data. Worth Knowing Oil's Slide Is Pulling the 10-Year Down With It Why Reuters reports oil fell as investors bet the election outcome changes energy policy; AP and CNBC both tie the post-election tape to tumbling crude. Not a big index mover yet (Energy is just 3.9% of the S&P), but lower yields take pressure off growth stocks broadly -- worth watching if it persists past today's noise.
Why It’s Happening

Why are chip and AI stocks falling this morning?

The trigger
Tuesday's U.S. midterm elections produced what Reuters and the WSJ are calling a 'blue wave' -- a Democratic sweep of Congress -- and investors are repricing AI and chip stocks on the assumption that divided government changes the regulatory and spending outlook for the sector. Qualcomm separately fell 10.36% after Investing.com and Traders Union reported weak handset chip sales overshadowed growth in its data-center business.
Why it hits these stocks
A shift in Washington's makeup raises questions about future AI infrastructure policy and spending support, per CNBC's reporting that 'the midterms could fuel the K-shaped AI trade' by slowing new data-center buildouts -- which hits chipmakers whose growth story depends on continued capex from hyperscalers. Qualcomm's miss adds a company-specific wound: its legacy smartphone-chip business is shrinking even as its newer data-center bet hasn't yet offset it.
Who’s saying what
  • Dan Ives, Wedbush (via Benzinga) Warns chip stocks aren't done falling and sees another 12% drop ahead.
  • Tom Lee (via Benzinga) Calls the AI selloff overdone, pushing back on Ives's bearish call.
  • Reuters Reports AI stocks and oil are falling together as investors bet the election outcome changes the policy backdrop for both sectors.
  • CNBC Frames the move as a 'K-shaped AI trade' -- existing AI capacity could become more valuable even as debt-funded new data-center buildouts face more pressure.
The other side
Tom Lee's view that the selloff is overdone, plus the fact that Technology's -1.13% move only explains a small slice of today's flat S&P -- the damage so far looks contained to specific chip names (Qualcomm, Broadcom, AVGO) rather than a broad tech rout.
What settles it
Whether the rotation out of chips persists past today's post-election noise, and how AI-infrastructure spending plans from hyperscalers (next due at upcoming earnings) respond to the new political backdrop.

The S&P looks calm at +0.12% only because two forces are canceling out -- Tech's drag and Financials' lift are nearly the same size, pulling in opposite directions.
