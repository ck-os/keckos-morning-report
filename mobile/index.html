Why It’s Happening

Why are chip and AI stocks falling again today?

The trigger
Per Bloomberg, Anthropic's stock is sliding closer to its $83 IPO price on day two, a reaction MarketWatch says is read as "bad for the rest of tech." Separately, the WSJ reports the U.S. is moving to tighten chip export curbs, and Yahoo Finance reports AMD is being hit by a fresh short-seller report alongside Nvidia's ongoing six-day slide.
Why it hits these stocks
Nvidia and Broadcom carry the heaviest AI exposure inside the S&P's Technology weighting, so a flat/failing AI-adjacent IPO reaction plus tighter export rules reads as evidence the AI-spending story is stalling -- and because Technology is 38.2% of the index, their declines translate directly into index-wide pressure.
Who’s saying what
  • MarketWatch Argues Anthropic's inability to hold its IPO price is being read as bad for the rest of the tech sector, not just the company itself.
  • Yahoo Finance Reports AMD stock was "drilled again" following a surprise short-seller report, adding a company-specific leg to the chip-sector decline.
  • 24/7 Wall St. Calls yesterday's reversal the S&P's biggest since March 2020, attributing it to a mix of AI jitters, a surprise CEO exit, and renewed Fed hike risk.
  • The Motley Fool Pushes back with a case that Nvidia could hit a new all-time high in 2027, arguing the selloff is overdone relative to the underlying growth metric.
The other side
The Motley Fool and Barron's both ran pieces this morning arguing Nvidia's slide looks overdone and the stock still has a path back to highs, a genuinely bullish counter-read inside the same news cycle.
What settles it
Wednesday's PCE inflation report and GDP data (Nov. 25) are the next scheduled catalysts that could reset the rate outlook weighing on these names; until then, watch whether chip names stabilize as the Anthropic IPO reaction fully settles.

Sandisk makes memory chips -- the NAND flash storage that goes into phones, laptops, and data centers. The business has been on a wild ride: revenue more than tripled this past fiscal year to $20.25 billion, with the fastest growth coming from data-center storage, up 437%. The stock is down 69.5% from its 52-week high even after a 208% gain over the past year, and it's dropping again this morning -- down 5.64% -- as worries about oversupply in the memory chip market spread across the sector.

THE BUSINESS

Sandisk Sells Memory, Not Just Drives

Sandisk's biggest business line is Edge storage (devices like phones, cars and gadgets), at $12.16 billion -- 60% of revenue -- up 195% from a year ago. Datacenter storage is smaller at $5.15 billion but growing fastest, up 437%, as cloud providers buy more flash memory. Consumer products (USB drives, memory cards) are the smallest and slowest-growing piece at $2.94 billion.

FINANCIAL HEALTH

Extremely Profitable, But Growth Is Slowing

Sandisk keeps about 73 cents of operating profit on every sales dollar -- an unusually high margin for a hardware maker -- and generated $11.2 billion in free cash flow (cash left after running the business) against just $201 million in debt. But revenue growth has cooled sharply to under 4% most recently after last year's huge jump, and the stock trades at 48 times trailing earnings, a rich price for a chip maker whose products can swing wildly in price.

Analysts Still Overwhelmingly Bullish

Of 24 analysts covering the stock, the consensus rating is "strong buy" (third-party opinion), with price targets ranging from $108.75 to $375 -- all above today's $89.07 price. HISTORY & ODDS

Too New a Setup to Have a Track Record

Sandisk has only spent one distinct stretch this far below its highs and moving averages in the past year, so there isn't enough history yet to say what typically happens next from here -- this is a genuinely new situation for the stock, not one with a reliable past pattern to lean on.

THE BULL CASE
  • Operating margins near 73% and $11.2 billion in free cash flow show a genuinely profitable business, not just a hyped story.
  • Datacenter revenue growing 437% points to real demand tied to AI and cloud infrastructure buildout.
  • Wall Street's "strong buy" consensus and targets well above the current price suggest analysts see the selloff as overdone.
THE BEAR CASE
  • The stock is down 69.5% from its high, and repeated headlines this morning cite "NAND market concerns" and oversupply fears -- a real demand/pricing risk for memory chips.
  • Earnings-day moves have been extremely volatile (swings from +11.5% to -18.1%), showing the market doesn't trust the numbers to hold.
  • Revenue growth has slowed sharply after last year's surge, raising the question of whether the best of the cycle has already passed.
