THIS MORNING · Premarket

Yields Surge to 19-Year High, Fed's Williams Floats Another Hike, Stocks Slide

The 30-year Treasury yield hit its highest level since 2004 overnight, and New York Fed President John Williams told a London audience it's "reasonable" to expect another rate hike by year-end -- a hawkish combination that's pushing Nasdaq futures down 1.26% and the S&P (ES=F) down 0.77% ahead of the open. The reaction is broad and orderly rather than panicked: the VIX is up 7.84% to 16.37, but gold is actually down 0.73% alongside stocks rather than rallying as a safe haven, suggesting this reads more as a rates repricing than a flight from risk. Traders are also bracing for today's Trump-Xi summit, though Treasury Secretary Bessent has already confirmed the U.S.-China trade truce is extended to January 10.

The Standout Fed's Williams says another rate hike by year-end is 'reasonable' just as the 30-year Treasury yield hits its highest level since 2004, sending the 10-year to 4.96% and Nasdaq futures down 1.26% premarket.

The Rest of the Tape Gold is falling alongside equities (-0.73%) rather than rallying as a hedge, and bitcoin is down 1.41% -- a pattern that looks like a rates-driven repricing rather than a classic flight-to-safety move.
VIX, 1-month. The VIX jumped 7.84% to 16.37 this morning as Fed's Williams raised the prospect of another year-end hike and long-end Treasury yields hit a 19-year high.
Nasdaq futures are down nearly twice as much as the Dow's, a gap that tracks tech's greater sensitivity to a higher discount rate on future earnings.

Beyond the rates story, oil is the day's clear outlier: WTI crude (CL=F) is up 1.97% to $93.98, with Reuters' own morning note flagging "surging oil prices cascading through markets and supply chains" as traders watch today's Trump-Xi summit -- Treasury Secretary Bessent has already confirmed the U.S.-China trade truce is extended to January 10, which should limit the downside tail risk from that meeting even as headline uncertainty keeps futures on edge. No major U.S. economic data lands today; the next scheduled releases are PCE and GDP on September 30, followed by the September jobs report on October 2, so today's tape is being driven entirely by the Williams comments and the yield move rather than a data surprise.
