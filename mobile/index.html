THIS MORNING · Premarket

CPI Comes In Hot, Bank of America Falls on Earnings and a Fine

September CPI rose 0.6% and the annual rate hit 3.7%, slightly ahead of the 3.6% consensus, which is nudging the 10-year back below 5.2% on rate-cut math even as it keeps pressure on bank and chip stocks reporting their own separate problems today. Technology is the single biggest drag on the S&P, down 1.0% and worth -0.405 points, while Financials add another -0.087 points as Bank of America and State Street sink on earnings -- together explaining more than the S&P's whole -0.17% move, with gainers in Communication and Health Care cushioning the rest.

Top Story CPI Rises 3.7% Annually, Slightly Hotter Than Expected Why CNBC reports the September CPI rose 0.6% month-over-month against a 0.6% consensus, but the annual rate of 3.7% came in above the 3.6% Dow Jones forecast; AP notes pricier gas and semiconductors were among the inputs that pushed the reading higher. The 10-year yield eased to 5.19% (-0.56%) on the print, which is why the move reads more mixed than hot -- not a clean 'yields spike' reaction.
Also Moving Bank of America Slides on Earnings Miss and $1.2B Fine Why CNBC and MarketWatch report Bank of America shares fell after weaker-than-expected trading revenue and a $1.2 billion Treasury fine compounded the disappointment; State Street (-8.05%) also missed revenue estimates despite beating on EPS, and Cantor Fitzgerald cut targets on Regions Financial and Citizens Financial the same morning. Financials are contributing -0.087 points to the S&P, the second-largest sector drag -- BAC alone is down 6.23% and State Street 8.05%, though BlackRock's +2.45% (on what Reuters calls resilient markets lifting fees) is a bright spot in the same sector.
Worth Knowing Chip Selloff Persists Ahead of Nvidia, TSMC Earnings Why Barron's and Reuters describe a continuing memory-chip rout -- Micron down 1.42%, HPE down 4.96%, NetApp down 4.08% -- even as MarketWatch reports JPMorgan and Citigroup still expect further revenue growth when TSMC reports; Jim Cramer is quoted calling the selloff 'nonsense' per Benzinga. Nvidia alone is -0.085 points of the S&P's move -- half the index's entire decline on its own -- while Apple's +0.81% move (+0.06 points) is one of the few things offsetting Tech's drag.
Why It’s Happening

Why did Technology and Financials drag the S&P lower this morning?

The trigger
September CPI printed hotter than expected (0.6% monthly, 3.7% annual vs. a 3.6% consensus per CNBC), landing the same morning Bank of America reported a trading-revenue miss plus a $1.2 billion Treasury fine, and State Street missed revenue estimates despite an EPS beat.
Why it hits these stocks
A hotter-than-expected CPI keeps the Fed's rate path less certain, which weighs on bank trading and lending economics just as BAC and STT's own earnings disappointed on revenue; in chips, the memory/storage complex (HPE, NetApp, Micron) remains under pressure from a broader rout that Barron's frames as deepening even as ASML's results stayed strong.
Who’s saying what
  • CNBC Reports Bank of America's revenue miss and the $1.2 billion fine together drove the stock's decline, separate from any CPI reaction.
  • Jim Cramer (per Benzinga) Argues the semiconductor selloff is 'nonsense' and the group remains, in his view, in a buy zone.
  • Fed Governor Michelle Bowman (per Reuters) Said there's time to 'wait and see' before any further Fed action, suggesting no rush to react to today's inflation data.
  • MarketWatch Frames BAC's slide as a profit miss compounding the misery of the government fine, rather than a market-wide rate story.
The other side
The selloff looks concentrated in specific names with their own earnings problems (BAC, STT, HPE) rather than a broad rate- or inflation-driven rotation -- Health Care, Communication, Energy and Consumer Staples are all higher this morning.
What settles it
Thursday's PPI and Retail Sales, plus Thursday's TSMC results, will show whether today's inflation read and the chip rout are connected or separate stories.
The Briefing
A hotter CPI eased yields instead of spiking them -- so today's real drag on the S&P is two separate earnings stories, not an inflation scare.
