AI-Spending Doubts Spread to Banks and Telecom as Yields Plunge

The AI-spending scare that hit chips this week is now bleeding into other sectors -- bank stocks are down on an "AI access scare" and Communication is the S&P's biggest drag (-1.26%, -0.125 pts) as SpaceX's tower ambitions keep pounding telecom. The flight to safety is showing up loudest in bonds: Treasury yields just logged their biggest drop since the 2023 banking crisis, which is why Real Estate's rate-sensitive cell-tower names are up double digits even as the broader market falls.

Top Story AI-Spending Fears Spread From Chips to Banks Why Protos and MarketWatch report bank stocks lost roughly $22 billion in value this morning on what's being called an "AI access scare," compounding a chip selloff that Al Jazeera and multiple outlets tie to investor doubt over whether AI infrastructure spending can keep paying off -- Bloomberg notes Chinese AI-model stocks alone have shed $150 billion with no sign of stabilizing. Financials is the S&P's second-biggest drag today (-0.56%, -0.063 pts, 15% of the index's move) -- a sector not usually caught up in an AI story now carrying real index weight in the selloff.
Also Moving Treasury Yields Post Biggest Drop Since 2023 Banking Crisis Why CNBC and MarketWatch report the 10-year yield reversed sharply lower this week as investors "seek cover from AI worries," with PGIM's Tipp calling it "a take-back trade" rather than outright recession fear. The flight to bonds is showing up as a flight to rate-sensitive stocks too -- Real Estate is the S&P's best sector today (+1.18%, +0.02 pts) on cell-tower names like Crown Castle (+12.98%) and American Tower (+10.15%), which also benefit directly from safety-seeking capital.
Worth Knowing Hedge Funds Start Shorting Stocks After Rough Month Why MarketWatch reports hedge funds, after a painful month of losses on popular trades, are now aggressively shorting U.S. stocks to try to claw back performance. Not visible in sector or index numbers yet, but it's a positioning signal worth watching -- added short interest can amplify moves in either direction if the AI-spending story keeps evolving.
Why It’s Happening

Why is the market falling broadly today, beyond just chip stocks?

The trigger
Al Jazeera and multiple outlets report U.S. tech stocks are tumbling on investor doubts over AI spending payoff, a theme that began with Wednesday's chip-equipment guidance scare and, per Protos, has now spread into bank stocks on what's being called an "AI access scare" that reportedly wiped roughly $22 billion from bank shares this morning.
Why it hits these stocks
Communication is being hit hardest and directly -- T-Mobile (-5.26%), AT&T (-5.01%) and Verizon (-4.49%) are falling on Forbes and CNBC reports that SpaceX is in talks for roughly 80,000 cell sites to build a rival wireless network via Starlink Mobile, a direct threat to carriers' core business; meanwhile Financials' selloff reflects broader worry about exposure to AI-related lending and spending risk rather than any one company's results.
Who’s saying what
  • PGIM's Tipp (via MarketWatch) Calls the bond rally "a take-back trade," not an all-out fear-of-recession move.
  • Protos Reports bank stocks crashed roughly $22 billion in value on what it terms an 'AI access scare.'
  • MarketWatch Frames the market as 'splitting in two' -- tech slumping even as over 300 S&P constituents rose Thursday and Treasurys rally, questioning whether that's a warning sign or just new leadership.
  • Forbes / CNBC Report AT&T's strong Q3 beat is being overshadowed by the SpaceX tower-buildout threat to the wireless business.
The other side
MarketWatch's own reporting notes more than 300 S&P 500 names actually rose Thursday even as tech slumped, suggesting this reads more like a rotation away from AI-exposed names than a broad market breakdown.
What settles it
Verizon reports earnings Monday, October 26 -- its reaction will show whether the SpaceX overhang and the broader AI-spending doubt are being priced into the whole sector or isolated to a few names.

AT&T is the country's largest wireless and broadband carrier, selling phone plans, internet, and fiber to consumers and businesses. The core business is steady and profitable -- it keeps about 25 cents of operating profit on every revenue dollar -- but growth is slow (revenue up just 5.3% this year) and the company carries a large pile of debt. The stock has fallen 22% from its 52-week high over the past two months on a mix of a bruising earnings reaction and new competitive fears, even though per-share earnings actually beat expectations last quarter.

THE BUSINESS

Almost Entirely a U.S. Phone and Internet Company

AT&T's Communications segment -- wireless plans, home internet/fiber, and business data services -- brings in $120.9 billion of its $125.6 billion in total revenue, or about 96 cents of every sales dollar, and grew 2.8% last year. A much smaller Mexico wireless business (Latin America) adds $4.4 billion and grew a bit faster, 3.5%.

FINANCIAL HEALTH

Profitable and Cash-Generative, But Loaded With Debt

Revenue grew 5.3% and the company kept about 25 cents of every sales dollar as operating profit (a 24.65% operating margin) and 10 cents as net profit. It generated roughly $12 billion in free cash flow (cash left after running and investing in the business) against $180.6 billion of total debt versus just $10.8 billion of cash on hand -- a heavy debt load that's standard for a capital-intensive phone network but leaves little room for error.

HISTORY & ODDS

Stocks This Beaten-Down Usually Recovered Some Ground

Looking back to 1984, AT&T has been this far below its highs and below its 200-day average 37 separate times. A year later, the stock was higher 58.8% of the time, with an average gain of 5.63% (median 3.9%), though outcomes ranged widely from -43.9% to +61.0% -- a reminder this is a historical pattern, not a prediction.

THE BULL CASE
  • Trades at just 8.8 times next year's expected earnings with a 4.88% dividend yield, a cheap valuation if the business stabilizes.

  • Analyst consensus remains 'buy' with an average target ($27.80) well above the current price, though this is third-party opinion.
THE BEAR CASE

  • The stock fell despite beating earnings estimates, a sign investors are focused on subscriber trends or long-term competitive risk rather than the quarter's numbers.
  • $180.6 billion in debt against $10.8 billion in cash limits financial flexibility if competition intensifies or rates stay elevated.
