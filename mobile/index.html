Oil Jumps, Yields Hit 2007 Highs as Geopolitics and Fed Bets Collide

Crude is up 2.71% to $104.14 on fresh reports of Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia and Iranian attacks on Gulf shipping, per CNBC, while the 10-year Treasury yield has climbed to its highest since 2007 as traders increasingly price in a Fed rate hike -- not a cut -- at Wednesday's decision. Despite that unusual combination, futures are broadly higher (S&P futures +0.48%, Nasdaq futures +0.55%) even as VIX jumps 3.45% to 17.69 and gold slips 0.94%, a sign the tape is shrugging off the risk headlines for now rather than confirming them. Bitcoin is down 1.5% to $76,990, and the dollar is firmer, rounding out a morning where energy and rates -- not equities -- are where the real stress is showing up.

The Standout Crude oil is up 2.71% to $104.14 after reports of Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia and Iranian attacks on Gulf shipping, the clearest single catalyst in this morning's tape.
The Outlier The 10-year Treasury yield has climbed to its highest level since 2007 on rising bets that the Fed hikes rather than cuts at Wednesday's decision, even as VIX jumps 3.45% to 17.69 -- a rates-and-vol story equity futures aren't yet reflecting.
The Rest of the Tape Equity futures are broadly green across the board (S&P, Nasdaq, Dow, Russell all higher) while gold slips 0.94% and bitcoin falls 1.5%, an unusual split that leaves the safe-haven complex looking more nervous than stocks this morning.
CBOE Volatility Index (VIX), 1-month. VIX is up 3.45% to 17.69 this morning, its sharpest premarket jump of the recent stretch, as oil's geopolitical spike and a repricing toward a Fed rate hike both add to hedging demand.
Oil is spiking on a real geopolitical shock while Treasury yields hit their highest since 2007 on rate-hike bets -- and equity futures haven't decided which story to believe yet.

Alphabet (GOOGL, $345.74, -1.04%) and Microsoft (MSFT, $500.32, -1.01%) are among the watchlist's bigger premarket decliners, continuing the AI-led tech pullback that Yahoo Finance's Ines Ferre and DA Davidson's Gil Luria described yesterday as investors respond to renewed calls to slow AI development, a theme Elon Musk also weighed in on publicly. That pullback lines up with today's sector KPI data showing XLK down 1.81% on the day, the weakest major sector, consistent with tech-specific rather than broad-market pressure. Nothing on today's date is scheduled on the economic calendar -- the next major catalysts are Wednesday's FOMC decision and retail sales report -- so between now and the open, the oil and yield moves described above, plus Scott Bessent's testimony before Congress today (per CNBC, on energy prices, Fed policy, and government debt), are the more immediate things capable of moving the tape.
