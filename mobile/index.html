Oil and Yields Climb on Mideast Tensions; Tape Reads Mixed

Oil is extending its advance -- WTI up 1.59% to $97.58 -- as Brent holds above $100 on worries that the standoff between the U.S. and Iran near the Strait of Hormuz, including Trump's warning to Tehran over activity at a suspected nuclear site, could disrupt supply. The 10-year yield is up 0.65% to 4.84%, a move CNBC ties to Treasury Secretary Bessent's expanded buyback plan and rising fiscal/political scrutiny of his bond-market credibility, and that yield pressure is showing up in a defensive tilt under the surface -- most S&P sectors are red even as headline futures sit little changed. Today's PPI print, due at 8:30 AM, is the next test of whether that inflation pressure is starting to broaden.

The Outlier The 10-year yield's 0.65% jump to 4.84% -- which CNBC links to Bessent's expanded Treasury buyback plan -- is pulling markets in the opposite direction from the classic flight-to-safety script, since gold (+0.46%) is rising alongside yields rather than against them.
The Rest of the Tape Equity futures are essentially flat and mixed (S&P +0.09%, Nasdaq -0.17%), while under the hood breadth is narrow and defensive-leaning, with only energy among sectors posting a clear one-day gain.

Oil's Mideast-driven bid and Bessent's buyback-driven yield jump are two separate stories pulling markets in different directions at once.

Under the calm-looking futures headline (S&P +0.09%, Nasdaq -0.17%), the KPI breadth data shows the equal-weight S&P (RSP) down 0.96% against the cap-weight SPY's -0.46% -- a half-point spread that points to a narrower, less broad-based tape than the index-level number alone would suggest, with only the energy sector (XLE +0.83%) posting a clear one-day gain while utilities, consumer discretionary, and consumer staples are all down more than 1%. The scheduled catalyst to watch: PPI lands today at 8:30 AM ET, a first read on producer-side inflation ahead of tomorrow's CPI print, and with yields already elevated on the Bessent buyback story, a hot PPI print would add a second, genuinely inflation-driven reason for yields to keep climbing rather than just the fiscal-supply one already in play.
