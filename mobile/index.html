Crude's Slide Deepens to 4.3% as AI Rally Broadens Into Software

Crude oil has extended its decline to -4.3% (now $90.53) as easing Iran/Hormuz rhetoric and Zelenskyy's call for an 'energy ceasefire' strip out geopolitical risk premium, dragging energy stocks lower (Sector heatmap: XLE -2.3% today) even as broader equity futures sit little-changed to slightly negative. Fed voices stay split -- Boston's Collins again warned inflation could run 'notably' hot after backing a hike -- keeping the 10-year near 4.96% and capping enthusiasm in cyclicals and small caps (Russell futures -0.56%).

Two geopolitical flashpoints -- Iran and Ukraine -- are cooling at the same time, and crude is pricing that out fast; equities, meanwhile, are absorbing a second, unrelated story as the AI trade broadens beyond chips into software and communications.

The broader tape is barely moving in comparison -- S&P futures are down just 0.11%, Nasdaq futures -0.27%, and Dow futures -0.29% -- because the two big stories today are pulling different sectors in different directions rather than moving the index as a whole: oil's slide is a drag on energy while the AI-driven rotation into tech and communications is offsetting it elsewhere. The one clear laggard is small caps, with Russell futures down 0.56%, likely reflecting Boston Fed President Collins' warning this morning that inflation could stay 'notably' above the Fed's 2% target after she backed a rate hike -- comments keeping the 10-year yield elevated near 4.96% and disproportionately weighing on rate-sensitive small-cap names. There's no scheduled US economic data release today; the next data of note isn't until PCE and GDP on September 30, so today's tape is being driven almost entirely by the geopolitical and Fed-commentary headlines above rather than a print.

Separately, the retailer is among several major chains, alongside Walmart and Lowe's, receiving billions of dollars in tariff refunds -- though research cited in today's coverage suggests shoppers have already absorbed part of that tariff cost themselves.

Meta (META) $738.43+0.25%

Meta's newly launched Muse AI model is being described as a serious step forward for the company's consumer AI ambitions, with commentary framing it as Meta catching up to OpenAI and Anthropic. The product's momentum is significant enough that traders are reportedly targeting Charles Schwab as a potential disruption casualty tied to the broader AI-driven shakeup in financial services, and the news is feeding into a broader debate over AI-industry concentration and valuation risk highlighted elsewhere in today's coverage.

Amazon (AMZN) $254.70-0.11%

Amazon is showing up in today's coverage mainly through the lens of AI-concentration risk rather than its own news -- commentary from Gabelli Funds and Wells Fargo Advisors warns of a 'concentration trap' catching retail investors exposed to mega-cap tech, while a separate piece covers the intensifying Anthropic-OpenAI model race (new Claude Opus 5.5 versus GPT-6 Sol and Luna) that bears on Amazon's own AI infrastructure bets.

Boeing (BA) $197.92+0.10%
