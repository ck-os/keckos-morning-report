CPI Prints In Line, Core a Touch Hot -- Stocks Shrug, Oil and VIX Do the Talking

August CPI rose 0.4% as expected, but core inflation came in a bit hotter than economists had forecast -- and instead of spooking futures, the reaction has been calm: the VIX is down sharply to 16.98 (-4.82%), oil is sliding -3.16% to $99.24 after snapping its multi-day rally, and equity futures are broadly higher (S&P futures +0.43%, Dow futures +0.48%). The 10-year yield is still edging up to 4.95%, so the bond market hasn't fully exhaled even as the options market has -- this reads like relief that the inflation print wasn't worse, not an all-clear on rates.

The Standout The VIX's -4.82% drop to 16.98 is the sharpest move on the board, suggesting the CPI print -- in line headline, slightly hot core -- removed enough uncertainty to unwind pre-data hedging.
The Outlier Oil's -3.16% slide to $99.24 breaks a multi-day winning streak that had pushed both Brent and WTI above $100, even as diesel prices hit a record high above $6/gallon on Ukraine- and Iran-related supply disruption.
The Rest of the Tape Equity futures are higher across the board and the dollar and yields are both firmer too, a mixed cross-asset picture that looks more like post-CPI repositioning than a single unified signal.
CBOE Volatility Index (VIX), 1-month. The VIX's -4.82% drop to 16.98 is today's largest single move in the Glance basket, consistent with options markets unwinding pre-CPI hedges once the print landed close to consensus.
The VIX's sharpest drop on the board and oil's steepest slide are telling two different relief stories at once -- one about inflation fear unwinding, the other about a supply rally running out of steam.

The two biggest movers in today's Glance basket are the VIX (-4.82% to 16.98) and crude oil (-3.16% to $99.24), and both trace to real, separate stories rather than one shared catalyst. The VIX's slide lines up with this morning's CPI print landing close enough to consensus to unwind pre-data hedging, even with core inflation running a touch hot. Oil's drop snaps a multi-day winning streak that had pushed both Brent and WTI above $100 amid Ukraine- and Iran-related supply disruption -- CNBC reports diesel prices hit a record above $6 a gallon on the same disruption, so today's pullback reads as a breather in that rally rather than a reversal of the underlying supply story. With CPI now behind the tape for this cycle, the next scheduled catalyst is the September 16 FOMC decision and Retail Sales report, five days out.
