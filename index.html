Geopolitics and a Walmart Miss Sour the Overnight Mood

Futures point lower across the board as an Iran threat lifts oil and volatility, while Treasury yields tick up despite the Treasury's own efforts to hold them down.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Overnight futures are broadly weaker heading into Thursday's session, with S&P 500 futures (ES=F) off 0.91% to 7,668.50, Nasdaq futures (NQ=F) down 0.98% to 29,322.50, Dow futures (YM=F) down 1.28% to 52,870, and small-cap Russell futures (RTY=F) leading the decline at -1.43% to 3,002.60. The VIX jumped 7.52% to 16.01, and the 10-year yield (^TNX) rose 0.92% to 4.70%, both moving in the direction consistent with President Trump's Truth Social threat of "economic warfare" against Iran. That same headline is doing double duty on the commodities side: WTI crude (CL=F) climbed 2.15% to $86.31, crossing the threshold for today's chart, while gold (GC=F) added 0.69% to $4,587.30 as investors leaned into traditional safe havens. Bitcoin (BTC-USD) bucked the risk-off tone with a 5.73% jump to $73,271, and the dollar index (DX-Y.NYB) was essentially flat at 98.84.

Oil and volatility moved together overnight, both reacting to the same geopolitical headline out of Washington.

Overseas, Asian markets closed higher before the U.S. headlines hit in force: the Nikkei 225 gained 1.2% to 66,216.79 and the Hang Seng added 0.8% to 25,698.49. Europe was more mixed, with Germany's DAX down 0.42% to 25,983.04 and London's FTSE essentially flat, up 0.04% to 10,748.16. Separately, a running story out of Washington continues in the background: Treasury Secretary Bessent's efforts to talk down long-term yields haven't yet worked, according to CNBC, and that tension is now colliding with a parallel question about Fed independence as Bessent's approach bumps up against the central bank's own turf under Fed Chair Warsh -- a dynamic worth watching alongside tonight's headline-driven risk-off move, though it isn't the proximate cause of this morning's futures weakness.

Company Briefs

Walmart Slides on Soft Outlook Ahead of Earnings

A disappointing guidance signal from the retail bellwether stands out against an otherwise quiet single-stock morning.
Markets Desk
Walmart (WMT) --

Shares tumbled 9% after the retailer's outlook disappointed Wall Street ahead of its fiscal second-quarter report, per CNBC. The report is being watched as a read on both the broader consumer and the so-called K-shaped economy, where higher- and lower-income spending patterns have been diverging -- a theme that gives this single-name move some macro relevance beyond Walmart itself.

Hyundai --

CEO José Muñoz told CNBC the automaker is considering increasing U.S. production capacity at its new Georgia metaplant, part of a previously announced $26 billion U.S. investment plan through 2028. No specific figures on the potential capacity increase were disclosed.
