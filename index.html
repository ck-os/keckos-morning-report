Yields Hit 2007 Highs, Oil Firms, as Markets Brace for a Hawkish Fed

A broad, cross-asset pullback lines up with a bond market pricing in more hikes, not fewer.
Markets Desk · Premarket

The single clearest story in the tape this morning is the bond market: CNBC reports the 10-year Treasury yield has risen to its highest level since 2007, now at 4.96%, as investors increasingly price in a rate hike at Wednesday's FOMC decision. That repricing isn't a one-day flinch -- a fresh CNBC survey out this morning finds Fed watchers now expect at least two hikes over the coming year, with roughly three-quarters of respondents describing the inflation problem as broader than just energy prices, even as oil itself trades higher (+0.99% to $102.39). CNBC separately flags that oil and the 10-year yield are moving in their tightest lockstep since 2019 -- a correlation it explicitly calls bad news for stocks, since rising energy costs and rising financing costs are now reinforcing each other rather than offsetting. Equity futures are lower across the board (S&P futures -0.16%, Nasdaq futures -0.10%, Dow futures -0.27%, Russell futures -0.22%), and gold's -0.63% slide alongside falling equities and a firmer dollar (+0.13%) is a notable break from the usual flight-to-safety pattern -- consistent with a market repricing higher rates broadly rather than fleeing to traditional havens.

Oil and Treasury yields are moving in their tightest lockstep since 2019 -- and CNBC calls that combination bad news for stocks, not a wash.

Bitcoin is the sharpest mover in the Glance basket this morning, down 1.63% to $76,888, consistent with a broader flight from higher-duration and rate-sensitive assets as yields climb toward 2007 levels -- the same dynamic showing up in gold's decline despite equities also falling, an unusual pairing that typically signals a rates-driven repricing rather than a classic safety bid. There's no scheduled US economic release today (Retail Sales and the FOMC decision itself both land tomorrow, September 16), so today's premarket move looks driven entirely by yesterday's yield move and this morning's CNBC hike survey rather than a fresh data print -- worth watching whether that yield climb extends into tomorrow's Fed decision itself.
