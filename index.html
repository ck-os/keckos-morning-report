Yields hit a 2025 high as oil extends its Iran-driven surge

Markets Desk · Premarket

The catalyst is unchanged from overnight but has intensified: CNBC reports Iran struck two U.S. bases in Jordan and Trump has vowed to hit Iran "hard," while Tehran is now pushing to revive the June nuclear deal -- and that standoff is directly cited by CNBC as the reason the 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 4.79% this morning, its highest level since January 2025. Oil is the clearest expression of the story, up 2.55% to $87.95 as traders price in supply risk around the Strait of Hormuz corridor, while the VIX has jumped 6.23% to 15.85 -- a sizable move that confirms the market is treating this as a genuine risk event rather than routine noise. Equity futures are lower across the board, but Nasdaq futures are down the most at -1.26% versus roughly -0.65% for the S&P, Dow, and Russell futures; that gap lines up with the KPI data showing tech (XLK) as the single worst-hit sector today at -1.12%, so the heavier rate-sensitivity of mega-cap tech inside the Nasdaq is doing real work in that underperformance.

The 10-year yield's push to a January-2025 high, oil's 2.55% jump, and a 6% VIX spike are all pointing the same direction: a real risk event, not routine noise.

Within the Glance basket, the clearest mover tied to a real story is oil: WTI's climb to $87.95 is the direct product of the Iran-Jordan escalation and Trump's retaliation vow, and it's now feeding through into the bond market, where CNBC links the fresh 10-year high explicitly to oil-driven inflation worries. Utilities (XLU, -2.2%) and industrials (XLI, -2.05%) are the session's weakest sectors per the live sector data, consistent with a market bracing for both higher input costs and higher-for-longer rates rather than a single-sector story. There's no scheduled U.S. economic data today -- the next tracked release is Friday's jobs report -- so with no data print to react to, today's price action into the cash open is squarely a geopolitical and oil story rather than a calendar-driven one.
