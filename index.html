Strait of Hormuz Clash Sends Oil Toward $100, Futures Lower Across the Board

A denied-but-disputed naval incident near Iran is showing up as a textbook risk-off pattern -- oil and gold higher, equity futures and the dollar softer, volatility waking up.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Crude oil is the number doing the talking this morning: WTI is up 2.75% to $95.59, and CNBC reports Brent touched $100 a barrel overnight after the U.S. denied Iran's claim that it struck two American vessels in retaliation for earlier strikes, with the two sides trading attacks near the Strait of Hormuz. That's a real, dated geopolitical catalyst, not a vague market mood, and it's landing squarely on energy prices while equity futures broadly retreat -- S&P futures -0.37%, Nasdaq futures -0.53%, Dow futures -0.65%, Russell futures -0.55%. The VIX's 4.13% jump to 16.37 confirms this is being read as a genuine risk event rather than routine premarket chop, and the fact that Dow futures are underperforming S&P futures by nearly 30 basis points fits a story about energy-driven input-cost anxiety rather than a tech-specific selloff.

Oil's move toward $100 a barrel is the actual catalyst this morning -- everything else in the tape is reacting to it, not the other way around.

The one name bucking the defensive tape is Meta (META, $643.50, +4.89% premarket), which is up sharply even as its mega-cap tech peers slip -- Alphabet (GOOGL, $330.75, -2.25%) and Amazon (AMZN, $253.30, -1.43%) are both down more than the broader futures decline, with no single headline in today's pulled news naming a specific driver for Meta's strength. Gold's 1.28% rise to $4,450 and the 10-year yield's move up to 4.81% are both consistent with the same Strait of Hormuz story -- gold as a safe-haven bid, yields firmer on inflationary oil-shock concern -- so the reaction is showing up broadly across assets, not confined to one corner of the market. Nothing on today's economic calendar lands before Thursday's PPI, so between now and the cash open the tape is likely to keep taking its cues from how the Iran situation develops rather than from scheduled data.
