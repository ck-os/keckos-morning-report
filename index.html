Crude Jumps on Iran Rhetoric, Crypto Rallies, Equities Tread Water

Geopolitical risk lifts oil while easing yields and crypto-policy optimism pull in opposite directions from a soft equity futures tape.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Futures point to a modestly lower open, with S&P 500 futures (ES=F) off 0.16% to 7,726.50, Nasdaq futures (NQ=F) down 0.25% to 29,538.25, and small-caps lagging most with Russell futures (RTY=F) down 0.29%. The VIX is up 2.22% to 15.22, still a low absolute level but a notch more cautious than yesterday's close. The clearer story is in commodities and crypto: WTI crude (CL=F) jumped 2.38% to $86.50 after President Trump threatened an 'Economic D-Day' for Iran, while bitcoin surged 4.14% to $72,167 on Trump's public push for Congress to pass the crypto Clarity Act. The 10-year Treasury yield eased 1.13% to 4.65% as traders digested a Treasury debt buyback plan, even as separate reporting flagged U.S. government debt crossing $40 trillion for the first time.

Oil's Iran-driven jump and bitcoin's policy-driven surge are pulling the tape in opposite directions from a merely soft equity futures session.

Overseas, Asia outperformed Europe overnight: the Nikkei 225 gained 1.2% to 66,216.79 and Hong Kong's Hang Seng added 0.8% to 25,698.49, while Germany's DAX slipped 0.54% to 25,986.59 and London's FTSE 100 eased 0.22% to 10,724.00. Gold (GC=F) is fractionally softer, down 0.28% to $4,543.00, and the dollar index (DX-Y.NYB) is down 0.21% to 98.60 -- a mild dollar pullback that sits alongside, but doesn't obviously explain, the sharper moves in oil and bitcoin. None of this is settled premarket positioning; volume is thin before the cash open and today's macro headlines (Iran rhetoric, debt-load commentary, Fed criticism from the White House) are qualitative overhangs rather than sized, dated events.
