Risk-Off Holds Through the Open as Small Caps Lag and Bitcoin Bucks the Trend

Thirty minutes into the cash session, the premarket selloff has largely held, with small caps underperforming and oil extending its climb.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Thirty minutes into the cash session, equity futures-implied losses from earlier this morning have largely carried through: S&P futures are down 0.57%, Nasdaq futures off 1.11%, the Dow down 0.86%, and the Russell 2000 the session's clear laggard at -1.42%. The VIX has climbed to 15.85, up 6.45% on the day, while the 10-year Treasury yield sits at 4.71%, up 1.18%. Oil is the standout cross-asset mover, with WTI crude up 2.69% to $86.76 as Iran-related tensions persist, and gold is modestly firmer at $4,572.30 (+0.36%). The dollar index is roughly flat at 98.85.

Small caps are absorbing the brunt of the selloff while bitcoin, up 4.45% to $72,385, is moving in the opposite direction entirely.

Overseas, the picture was mixed heading into the U.S. session: the Nikkei 225 closed up 1.2% to 66,216.79 and Hong Kong's Hang Seng added 0.8%, while Europe was softer, with Germany's DAX down 0.55% and London's FTSE essentially flat at +0.01%. Bitcoin's 4.45% jump to $72,385 stands out against the broader risk-off tape, tied to a reported $3.3 billion short-squeeze that has also lifted crypto-adjacent equities. Treasury Secretary Bessent's comments this morning -- both on Iran policy and a potential $4 billion-plus buyback operation targeting longer-dated securities -- are circulating alongside the yield move, though the 10-year's rise to 4.71% is modest in scale.

Walmart Slumps on Sales Miss as Peers Hold Firmer

Walmart (WMT) --

Walmart shares fell roughly 8% after the company reported its slowest same-store sales growth since 2020, even as it raised its full-year outlook and disclosed a large tariff refund.
