Crude Spikes on Iran Rhetoric, Futures Drift Lower

Oil leads the tape higher as geopolitical tension resurfaces, while equity futures pull back modestly and yields ease.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Premarket futures are pointing to a softer open across the board, with S&P 500 futures (ES=F) down 0.30% to 7,715.25, Nasdaq futures (NQ=F) off 0.63% to 29,427.50, Dow futures (YM=F) down 0.42%, and small-cap Russell futures (RTY=F) down 0.50%. The standout move is in crude oil, with WTI (CL=F) up 3.10% to $87.11 as President Trump escalated rhetoric on Iran, threatening an "Economic D-Day" and further consequences for Tehran's backers -- a fresh geopolitical risk premium building into the barrel. Meanwhile the 10-year Treasury yield (^TNX) eased 1.13% to 4.65%, a partial reversal after CNBC reported yields edging higher overnight as traders digest a new Treasury debt buyback plan. The VIX ticked up 3.02% to 15.34, still a low absolute level but a signal that some hedging demand is creeping back in.

Crude's 3% jump on Iran rhetoric is the one cross-asset move that stands out against an otherwise routine, modestly risk-off premarket tape.

Overseas, Asian equities outperformed -- the Nikkei 225 gained 1.2% to 66,216.79 and Hong Kong's Hang Seng added 0.8% to 25,698.49 -- while European bourses lagged, with Germany's DAX down 0.59% and London's FTSE 100 off 0.25%. Gold (GC=F) slipped 0.32% to $4,541.00 even as the dollar index (DX-Y.NYB) was roughly flat, down 0.11% to 98.70. The more notable divergence sits in crypto: bitcoin (BTC-USD) surged 4.04% to $72,100 after Trump urged Congress to pass the crypto Clarity Act, a rally that stands apart from the otherwise cautious tone in equity futures. Separately, CNBC reported U.S. government debt has now passed $40 trillion, more than doubling over the past decade -- a structural backdrop worth noting even though it isn't moving today's tape on its own.

Company Briefs

Walmart raises its outlook while Alibaba slides on AI-driven margin pressure.
Markets Desk
Walmart (WMT) --

Walmart raised its full-year outlook ahead of its fiscal second-quarter earnings report Thursday, saying it will use a large tariff refund to help keep prices low for consumers. The report is being closely watched as a read on the health of the consumer and the broader K-shaped economy narrative that has dominated retail coverage this year.

Alibaba (BABA) --

Alibaba's U.S.-listed shares fell roughly 3% in premarket trading after the company reported a 75% drop in net income for the June quarter, which it attributed to heavy AI-related spending. The move underscores the market's growing scrutiny of AI capex outpacing near-term earnings, a theme flagged elsewhere in the tape's cautious tone toward high-spending tech names.
