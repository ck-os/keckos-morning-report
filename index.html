Calm Futures, Restless Rates: Gold and Bitcoin Do the Talking

Equity futures point to a modestly higher open while gold, bitcoin, and long-dated Treasury yields all move more than the index tape suggests.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Premarket futures are quietly green across the board this Friday morning: S&P futures (ES=F) up 0.24% to 7,687.50, Nasdaq futures (NQ=F) up 0.41% to 29,480.50, Dow futures (YM=F) up 0.29% to 53,027, and small-cap Russell futures (RTY=F) up 0.31% to 3,017.90. The VIX is down 1.94% to 15.70, ordinarily a signal of a placid session ahead -- but that calm sits oddly next to the rest of the board. Gold (GC=F) has jumped 1.25% to $4,648.10, bitcoin is up a sharp 6.57% to $77,791, and the 10-year Treasury yield (^TNX) has ticked up 0.92% to 4.70%, with CNBC reporting that the Treasury's bond buyback rally is 'fizzling out' and yields drifting higher again on renewed debt-market unease. WTI crude is up a more modest 0.59% to $86.97, and the dollar index is essentially flat, down 0.12% to 98.63.

Equity futures read calm; gold, bitcoin, and long-dated yields are all telling a noisier story underneath.

Overseas, the picture is mixed rather than uniformly risk-on: Hong Kong's Hang Seng is up a strong 1.21%, Germany's DAX is flat (+0.02%), while Japan's Nikkei 225 is down 0.22% and London's FTSE is off a slight 0.05%. CNBC also flags bitcoin's move as part of a broader weekly run -- on track for roughly a 20% weekly gain -- and separately reports that hedge funds suffered their worst month of underperformance versus the S&P 500 in more than 20 years of Goldman's data, a possible clue that de-grossing and forced repositioning, not just fresh conviction, is part of what's moving flows this week. Treasury-market strain is a running thread: JPMorgan's James Sullivan compared federal bond-market intervention to 'paying your mortgage with your credit card,' and a separate CNBC piece frames a brewing tension between Treasury Secretary Bessent and Fed's Warsh over how far the central bank should coordinate on bonds and the balance sheet.
