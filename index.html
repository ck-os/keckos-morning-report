Bond Selloff Fears Send Gold Sharply Higher, Equities Barely Move

Rates and inflation worries are showing up in gold and the dollar this morning, not in stocks.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Gold (GC=F, $4,474.50, +2.48%) is the loudest signal on the board this morning, and CNBC's own reporting spells out why: a global bond selloff is underway, driven by a mix of heavy government debt issuance, an oil-price shock that's reignited inflation concerns, and expectations that rates are settling into a structurally higher regime. The 10-year Treasury yield is holding at 4.80%, essentially unchanged on the day but still elevated, while the 3-month/2-year/10-year curve remains upward-sloping (10s2y at 0.40, 10s3m at 0.87) -- no inversion, but a curve consistent with a market pricing persistent inflation risk rather than an imminent Fed cut. Oil's own 0.77% gain to $91.71 is a live piece of that inflation-shock narrative rather than a bystander. None of this is showing up as equity stress yet: S&P futures are up just 0.04% and Nasdaq futures are down 0.05%, a genuinely flat premarket that contrasts with gold's sharp move -- the story this morning is concentrated in rates-sensitive assets, not broad risk appetite.

Gold's 2.48% jump reads as a hedge against a global bond selloff, not a flight from stocks -- equity futures are essentially flat.

Within the Glance basket, gold's move dwarfs everything else, and the dollar's 0.35% slide (DX-Y.NYB, 99.21) fits the same story -- a weaker dollar and stronger gold both point to investors positioning against currency and rate risk rather than equity risk specifically. There's no scheduled US economic release today; the next major print is Friday's jobs report (nonfarm payrolls), which will be the first real test of whether the labor market backs up or complicates this higher-rate narrative. Among individual names, Nvidia (NVDA, $224.41, +3.21% premarket) and Meta (META, $592.85, +2.47% premarket) are the biggest movers on the notable-companies list this morning, though the provided headlines don't tie either move to a specific new catalyst today -- Broadcom's (AVGO, $367.24, -0.66% premarket) fiscal 2028 guidance was reportedly well-received per CNBC even as its near-term revenue outlook disappointed, but that isn't showing up as a premarket gain for the stock itself.
