Iran De-Escalation Sinks Crude, AI Rally Broadens Beyond Chips

Energy sells off on easing geopolitical risk while the AI trade rotates into software and communications, leaving broad equity futures roughly flat.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Crude oil is doing the heavy lifting this morning, down 4.3% to $90.53 as signals of de-escalation around Iran take a real risk premium out of the barrel. Iran's government told CNBC that Trump's threats reflect 'strategic desperation' while floating conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz, and Ukraine's Zelenskyy separately called for an 'energy ceasefire' with Russia -- together, two distinct geopolitical flashpoints that had been supporting oil prices are both cooling at once. That's translating directly into the sector data: Energy (XLE) is down 2.3% on the day, the worst-performing sector in the basket, dragging names like Chevron (CVX, $203.61, +0.59% premarket) and Exxon Mobil (XOM, $160.08, +0.88% premarket) to relatively muted premarket moves given the scale of the crude decline. At the same time, a second story is running in parallel: Meta's (META, $738.43, +0.25% premarket) newly launched Muse AI model is, per CNBC, 'reignit[ing] AI disruption fears' strong enough to pressure Charles Schwab as a brokerage casualty -- and that AI-disruption narrative is showing up as a rotation beyond just chipmakers into software and communications, with Communication Services (XLC, +3.9%) and Technology (XLK, +2.89%) the two standout sectors today.

Two geopolitical flashpoints -- Iran and Ukraine -- are cooling at the same time, and crude is pricing that out fast; equities, meanwhile, are absorbing a second, unrelated story as the AI trade broadens beyond chips into software and communications.

The broader tape is barely moving in comparison -- S&P futures are down just 0.11%, Nasdaq futures -0.27%, and Dow futures -0.29% -- because the two big stories today are pulling different sectors in different directions rather than moving the index as a whole: oil's slide is a drag on energy while the AI-driven rotation into tech and communications is offsetting it elsewhere. The one clear laggard is small caps, with Russell futures down 0.56%, likely reflecting Boston Fed President Collins' warning this morning that inflation could stay 'notably' above the Fed's 2% target after she backed a rate hike -- comments keeping the 10-year yield elevated near 4.96% and disproportionately weighing on rate-sensitive small-cap names. There's no scheduled US economic data release today; the next data of note isn't until PCE and GDP on September 30, so today's tape is being driven almost entirely by the geopolitical and Fed-commentary headlines above rather than a print.

JPMorgan Lands $20 Billion QIA Mandate as Dealmaking Hits Records

Record global dealmaking and a fresh sovereign-wealth partnership put JPMorgan in focus, while Home Depot flags a squeezed consumer.
Markets Desk
JPMorgan Chase (JPM, $338.01, -0.62% premarket) —

JPMorgan Chase has landed a $20 billion multi-market investment mandate from the Qatar Investment Authority, a partnership led by J.P. Morgan Asset Management spanning public equities and private credit across several regions. Bank executives also pointed to record global dealmaking and resilient M&A and IPO activity in recent commentary, even against a backdrop of macro headwinds -- a sign the bank's capital-markets franchise is holding up well despite the choppier rate environment.

Home Depot (HD, $304.00, -0.46% premarket) —

Home Depot issued a fresh warning that squeezed American shoppers are scaling back big-ticket renovation projects in favor of fixing only what's broken, as elevated mortgage rates and inflation weigh on discretionary home spending. Separately, the retailer is among several major chains, alongside Walmart and Lowe's, receiving billions of dollars in tariff refunds -- though research cited in today's coverage suggests shoppers have already absorbed part of that tariff cost themselves.

Meta (META, $738.43, +0.25% premarket) —

Meta's newly launched Muse AI model is being described as a serious step forward for the company's consumer AI ambitions, with commentary framing it as Meta catching up to OpenAI and Anthropic. The product's momentum is significant enough that traders are reportedly targeting Charles Schwab as a potential disruption casualty tied to the broader AI-driven shakeup in financial services, and the news is feeding into a broader debate over AI-industry concentration and valuation risk highlighted elsewhere in today's coverage.

Amazon (AMZN, $254.70, -0.11% premarket) —

Amazon is showing up in today's coverage mainly through the lens of AI-concentration risk rather than its own news -- commentary from Gabelli Funds and Wells Fargo Advisors warns of a 'concentration trap' catching retail investors exposed to mega-cap tech, while a separate piece covers the intensifying Anthropic-OpenAI model race (new Claude Opus 5.5 versus GPT-6 Sol and Luna) that bears on Amazon's own AI infrastructure bets.

Boeing (BA, $197.92, +0.10% premarket) —

Boeing is in focus ahead of the Trump-Xi summit, with Jefferies flagging tariff cuts as the 'single most possible deliverable' from the talks while AI chips and rare-earths relief look unlikely -- a mixed signal for Boeing's China exposure. Separately, rival Embraer is drawing attention for a technical breakout while Boeing and Airbus remain stuck below key moving averages, a relative-strength gap worth watching in the aerospace sector.
