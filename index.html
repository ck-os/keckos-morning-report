Yields at 2007 Highs, Oil Climbing on Pipeline Risk Ahead of Wednesday's Fed Decision

A hawkish rate-hike repricing and a Middle East supply scare are pulling bonds, oil, equities, and crypto in the same direction at once.
Markets Desk · Premarket

The 10-year Treasury yield has climbed to its highest level since 2007, CNBC reports, as markets increasingly price in a rate hike at Wednesday's FOMC decision -- a fresh CNBC survey out this morning finds respondents now expecting at least two more hikes over the coming year, with roughly three-quarters citing an inflation problem broader than energy prices alone. The 10-year itself sits at 5.01%, up 1.03% on the session, while crude oil is climbing in the same direction rather than falling with equities: WTI is up 1.59% to $103.00, which CNBC attributes to Saudi Arabia racing to restart a key pipeline before an estimated five-to-seven-day inventory cushion runs dry. That combination -- a hawkish Fed repricing pushing yields up while a separate supply shock pushes oil up too -- is the double-barreled story behind today's softness in risk assets, since higher-for-longer rates and higher energy costs both squeeze the same equity multiples at once.

Rising yields on a hawkish Fed repricing and rising oil on a Saudi pipeline scare are squeezing risk assets from two directions at once.

S&P futures are down a modest 0.13% and Nasdaq futures off 0.11%, but the mover with real breadth this morning is cross-asset rather than confined to one index: gold is down 0.81% to $4,316.50 and bitcoin has dropped 1.61% to $76,901, both falling alongside equities rather than acting as the usual safe-haven offset -- a signature more consistent with real rates rising broadly than with a classic flight-to-safety rotation. The Fed's own rate decision lands tomorrow, Wednesday, September 16, alongside Retail Sales the same morning, so today's premarket move reads as positioning ahead of that pair of releases rather than a reaction to news already in hand; a hawkish surprise or a soft retail print could swing the tape meaningfully into and through the announcement.
