Markets Desk · Premarket

Half an hour into the cash session, the pattern set in the premarket has held rather than reversed: S&P futures are up 0.17%, the 10-year yield has eased further to 4.66% (-0.94% on the day), and the VIX has ticked down to 15.75 -- equities firmer, yields lower, and volatility calm all at once, a combination that typically signals genuine risk appetite rather than a stock market drifting higher on its own. Crude is the one instrument moving on a scale that actually stands apart, down 3.34% to $82.58 after CNBC reported the U.S. is shifting toward economic pressure rather than military escalation against Iran, easing fears of a wider regional war that had been supporting the oil price. Gold, meanwhile, is only fractionally softer (-0.19%) rather than confirming a classic flight-to-safety unwind, which suggests today's move is less about investors de-risking broadly and more about one specific geopolitical premium coming out of crude specifically.

Equities firmer, yields lower, volatility calm, and oil sliding on an easing war premium -- four signals pointing the same low-stress direction, but for two distinct reasons.
