Oil Shock Deepens, AI Selloff Concentrates the Damage in Tech

Two distinct shocks are hitting different corners of the tape rather than one broad selloff.
Markets Desk · Premarket

The dominant story this morning is energy, not equities: Saudi Arabia's shutdown of its East-West pipeline -- the kingdom's key alternative route around the Strait of Hormuz -- combined with Iran's claim to have downed a U.S. drone over the strait, per CNBC, has pushed Brent above $109 and WTI crude (CL=F) up 4.16% to $104.21. CNBC also reports U.S. diesel prices have crossed $6 a gallon for the first time, underscoring that this is a real supply-side shock working through the economy, not a one-day futures spike. Layered on top of that is a second, separate story: Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and OpenAI's Sam Altman have both publicly called for the AI industry to slow down, a warning that CNBC and Stocktwits both tie directly to premarket weakness in AMD ($486.01, -5.84%), Broadcom, Micron, and Nvidia (NVDA, $212.03, -2.87%) alongside AI-adjacent names like Oracle and Tesla -- Stocktwits explicitly named MU, AMD, NVDA, ORCL and TSLA together as the stocks in focus for the Nasdaq's premarket slide. That sector concentration is exactly why Nasdaq futures (-1.71%) are falling roughly four times as hard as Dow futures (+0.41%): the AI-chip and mega-cap tech names carry outsized weight in the Nasdaq and essentially none in the Dow, so a story that's really about one sector's valuation reset is showing up almost entirely in one index.

The VIX is the clearest confirmation that today's move is broad enough to matter -- up 11.74% to 17.70, its second straight double-digit jump, while gold is falling 1.13% rather than rising, a combination that breaks the classic flight-to-safety pattern (falling equities usually pair with rising, not falling, gold). That split -- fear gauge up, safe-haven metal down -- reads less like systemic panic and more like a rotation: money moving out of AI-adjacent tech and into energy-linked and value names, consistent with Russell futures (+0.20%) and Dow futures (+0.41%) holding up while Nasdaq futures lag. There's no major scheduled U.S. data release today to compete for attention -- the next big prints are Wednesday's FOMC decision and retail sales report -- so today's tape is being driven almost entirely by the two live news threads above rather than a data surprise.
