Iran Rhetoric Lifts Crude, Sours Futures

A fresh round of Trump-Iran tension is doing more to move markets this morning than any scheduled data.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Equity futures are pointed lower across the board this morning, with S&P 500 futures (ES=F) off 0.47%, Nasdaq futures (NQ=F) down 0.88%, Dow futures (YM=F) down 0.65%, and Russell 2000 futures (RTY=F) down 0.76%. The VIX is up 3.90% to 15.47, a modest but notable pickup in hedging demand. The clearest driver in the tape is oil: WTI crude (CL=F) is up 3.23% to $87.22 after President Trump sharpened his rhetoric against Iran amid a stalled negotiations track, reviving supply-disruption concerns around the Strait of Hormuz corridor. Curiously, gold (GC=F) isn't playing along as a safe haven this morning, down 0.80% to $4,519.10, and the dollar index (DX-Y.NYB) is essentially flat at 98.78.

Oil is doing the talking this morning -- up over 3% on Iran rhetoric while equity futures retreat and gold, oddly, doesn't follow the safe-haven script.

The 10-year Treasury yield (^TNX) eased 1.13% to 4.65%, a partial retracement after Wednesday's bond rally reportedly took 30-year yields down sharply on Treasury support headlines circulating in the wires. Overseas, Asian equities are firmer -- the Nikkei 225 is up 1.2% to 66,216.79 and the Hang Seng is up 0.8% to 25,698.49 -- while Europe is softer, with Germany's DAX down 0.55% and London's FTSE 100 down 0.32%, a split that looks more regional-rotation than uniformly risk-off. Bitcoin (BTC-USD) is the outlier of the morning, up 3.45% to $71,691 on renewed momentum around U.S. crypto legislation, moving in the opposite direction of the broader futures tape.

Company Briefs

Walmart Lifts Guidance, Alibaba Slides on AI Spend

One retail giant raises its outlook while one Chinese tech giant absorbs the cost of its AI buildout.
Markets Desk
Walmart (WMT) --

Walmart raised its full-year outlook ahead of its fiscal second-quarter earnings report Thursday, saying it will use a large tariff refund to keep prices low for consumers. The report is being closely watched as a read on the health of the consumer and the broader K-shaped economy -- whether lower- and higher-income shoppers are experiencing meaningfully different spending trends.

Alibaba (BABA) --

Alibaba's U.S.-listed shares fell roughly 3% in premarket trading after the company reported a 75% drop in net income for the June quarter, driven by heavy AI infrastructure spending. The results echo a broader theme playing out across the sector: several large tech and cloud players are spending aggressively on AI capacity well ahead of realized returns, and markets are beginning to selectively punish that gap rather than reward the spending outright.
