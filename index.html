Small Caps Lead a Broad Futures Pullback as Yields Climb

Volatility jumps, oil pushes higher, and crypto rallies against the grain of a risk-off equity tape.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Equity futures are pointing lower across the board this morning, with Russell 2000 futures off 1.59% to 2,997.60 leading the decline, followed by Nasdaq futures down 1.07% to 29,294.75, Dow futures down 0.99% to 53,025, and S&P futures down 0.69% to 7,685.25. The VIX is up 6.11% to 15.80, and the 10-year Treasury yield has climbed 1.05% to 4.70%, a combination that points to a genuine, if modest, risk-off tilt rather than a single-stock story. Crude oil stands out to the upside, up 2.38% to $86.50 a barrel, while gold is essentially flat at $4,567.90 (+0.27%) and the dollar index is little changed at 98.91.

Russell 2000 futures are down 1.59%, the weakest of the four major index futures, even as bitcoin surges nearly 5% and oil climbs more than 2%.

Overseas, the picture is mixed rather than uniformly risk-off: Japan's Nikkei 225 closed up 1.2% to 66,216.79 and Hong Kong's Hang Seng added 0.8% to 25,698.49, while Germany's DAX slipped 0.42% to 25,983.04 and London's FTSE 100 was essentially flat, up 0.04% to 10,748.16. Bitcoin's near-5% jump to roughly $72,739 continues a two-day, 12% surge tied to a last-ditch legislative push for the Clarity Act, an unusual pairing with a domestic equity futures market that's broadly lower and a bond market where long-end yields keep climbing -- a reminder that today's tape isn't reducible to one single driver.

Walmart Slips Despite Raised Outlook and Tariff Refund

A retail bellwether's mixed reaction offers an early read on the consumer heading into the back half of the year.
Markets Desk
Walmart (WMT) --

Walmart raised its full-year outlook and disclosed a large tariff refund alongside fiscal second-quarter results, but shares are reported lower on sales concerns -- a reminder that guidance beats don't automatically translate into a positive stock reaction when the underlying sales trend disappoints. As one of the first major retailers to report this cycle, the release is being watched as an early read on the health of the consumer and the broader "K-shaped economy" narrative that's been a recurring theme in recent coverage.
