Yields Set the Tone Ahead of the CPI Print

A rates-led selloff pauses for breath, but the underlying pressure -- climbing yields and a Middle East-driven oil story -- hasn't actually eased.
Markets Desk · Premarket

The market's real preoccupation this morning is the bond market, not the futures board. The 10-year yield has pushed to 4.94%, up 2.21% on the day, and CNBC's Jim Cramer said Thursday the 30-year Treasury yield -- climbing toward roughly 5.3% -- is "the key force driving stocks right now." That's not just a talking point: valuation expert Aswath Damodaran, cited separately overnight, found the S&P 500 has historically moved about 0.5% whenever the 10-year shifts by more than three basis points, which is exactly the kind of move underway today. That backdrop is why today's CPI print, due this morning, carries outsized weight -- a hot reading would extend the yield climb that's already driven a four-day equity slide; a cooler one could validate the bounce futures are currently pricing.

The 10-year yield's push to 4.94% is doing more to explain today's tape than any single equity headline.

Futures are broadly higher this morning -- S&P, Nasdaq, Dow, and Russell futures all up roughly half a percent to six-tenths -- a tentative bounce after the four-day slide Stocktwits described overnight, which it tied to oil-driven rate-hike odds. Confirming that the mood has genuinely eased somewhat rather than just drifted, the VIX has dropped -3.53% to 17.21, even as crude fell -2.57% to $99.85 -- an unusual pairing given fresh Iran-related sanctions headlines this morning, where Treasury Secretary Bessent said a "large bank" will be sanctioned Monday as part of the administration's Iran strategy. Gold's modest +0.47% gain and the dollar's flat read suggest no broad flight-to-safety yet; the more direct read is that today's 8:30 CPI print, not the overnight geopolitical headlines, is what the tape is actually positioned around.
