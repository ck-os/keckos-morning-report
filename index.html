Oil's Break Above $100 -- Not Today's PPI Print -- Is Setting the Rate Path

A tame inflation report is taking a back seat to crude's surge on Iran-war fears, and the yield reaction is hitting growth stocks harder than the broader tape.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Producer prices rose 0.4% in August, landing exactly on the Dow Jones consensus -- ordinarily a non-event for markets. But CNBC's own reporting this morning makes clear that oil, not inflation, is what's actually moving Treasurys: WTI crude has broken above $100 per barrel, up 4.13% to $100.02, as traders brace for what CNBC describes as a prolonged Iran war disrupting supply through the region. That combination pushed the 10-year yield to 4.88% (+0.99%), a multi-year high, with CNBC directly crediting surging oil for outweighing the tame CPI-adjacent data. The European Central Bank added another log to the fire, hiking its own rate to 2.5% overnight on the same inflation-and-Iran-risk logic, reinforcing that this is a global, not just domestic, repricing of rate risk around energy supply.

Oil breaking $100 on Iran-war fears is outweighing a tame PPI print -- and pushing the 10-year to a multi-year high in the process.

Nasdaq futures are the weakest of the four major contracts this morning, down 0.92% versus a comparatively modest 0.13% decline in Dow futures -- a gap that lines up with the yield story above, since the Nasdaq's heavier concentration in long-duration, richly-valued tech names makes it more sensitive to a rising discount rate than the more value-tilted Dow. That reaction is showing up broadly rather than in isolation: the VIX is up 3.89% to 17.10 and gold is down 0.48% even as yields rise, a combination consistent with a rates-led repricing rather than a pure flight-to-safety trade. Today's PPI print has already landed and come in as expected, so the next real test for this tape is tomorrow's CPI release, followed by the Fed's rate decision and Retail Sales both due September 16 -- with oil's trajectory now arguably the bigger swing factor for how those numbers get read.
