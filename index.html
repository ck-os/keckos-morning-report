Yields Lead, Tech Lags Ahead of Wholesale Inflation Data

Rising rates and oil are pulling in the same direction against equities this morning, with the pressure concentrated in the most rate-sensitive corner of the tape.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Treasury yields are the story of the morning: CNBC reports the 10-year note hit multi-year highs overnight as traders position ahead of today's Producer Price Index release, due later this morning as the first of two inflation prints this week (CPI follows Friday). The 10-year sits at 4.84%, up 0.65% on the session, and that move is showing up unevenly across equities -- Nasdaq futures are down 0.50%, notably worse than the Dow's +0.15%, as the same higher-rate backdrop is feeding a live valuation debate across the largest tech names: this morning's coverage separately questions whether Nvidia (NVDA, $221.99, -0.75% premarket), Microsoft (MSFT, $491.00, -0.13% premarket), and Apple's near-term reliance on the iPhone already price in too much good news. Because those three carry outsized weight in the Nasdaq relative to the Dow, the index gap is a fairly direct read-through of where the rate pressure is landing hardest.

The 10-year's push to 4.84% is landing hardest where it always does first -- the most richly-valued corner of the tape.

Oil is the other number doing real work today: WTI crude is up 1.52% to $97.51, and CNBC's own reporting ties that climb directly to the same rising-yield story, framing crude above the psychologically important $100 level as adding to the case for continued rate pressure. The VIX is up 1.34% to 16.68 -- a modest but confirming move alongside softer equities and higher yields, consistent with a cautious rather than panicked tone into today's data. With PPI due later this morning and CPI following Friday, the next several hours carry real potential to move the tape further depending on how wholesale inflation prints relative to expectations.
