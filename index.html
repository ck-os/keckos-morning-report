Oil and Gold Surge Together as a Bond Selloff Narrative Builds

Commodities and yields are moving in the same direction -- a sign the story is inflation and rates, not a single stock or a flight to safety.
Markets Desk · Premarket

The load-bearing story this morning is CNBC's report that global bond markets are in the midst of a broadening selloff, driven by a mix of heavy government debt issuance, an oil-price shock, and rising expectations for higher rates -- and this morning's data backs that read directly. Crude is up 2.11% to $92.93 following fresh escalation in the Middle East, where Iran struck Kuwait overnight and Trump said the renewed hostilities "will not last too long." Gold is up an even sharper 2.47% to $4,474, and the Dutch central bank's move to repatriate gold bars from the U.S. and Canada, cited explicitly as "crisis preparedness," underscores that this is being read as a hedging move, not just a commodities story. The 10-year yield is holding at 4.80%, unmoved on the day but already elevated -- exactly the level CNBC's "higher-rate era" piece frames as the backdrop putting pressure on borrowers globally.

Gold and oil rising together, alongside a yield backdrop already near 4.80%, is inflation-and-rate anxiety showing up broadly across assets, not a single market drifting on its own.

Broadcom (AVGO, $356.39, -2.95% premarket) is today's single biggest mover in the notable-companies watchlist, and it's weighing more on the Nasdaq than the Dow: Nasdaq futures are down 0.21% versus Dow futures up 0.05%, consistent with a chipmaker's guidance miss hitting the tech-heavy index harder. CNBC's coverage frames the move as a disconnect between a disappointing near-term revenue forecast and otherwise strong fiscal 2028 earnings guidance tied to AI-lab demand -- the market is reacting to the guide, not the long-term growth story. Separately, Alphabet is showing renewed strength after what CNBC calls its longest monthly losing streak in over a decade, helped by new AI model releases; no scheduled US economic data lands today, with the jobs report not due until tomorrow, September 4.
