Warsh's Fed Framework Keeps Hikes on the Table, Yields Spike

A hawkish tilt at the Fed is pushing Treasury yields higher even as equity futures and gold both climb, a combination that reads as investors hedging inflation risk rather than betting on a clean risk-on tape.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh's push to overhaul the central bank's policy framework is the story driving the tape this morning: CNBC reports his emerging approach leaves the door open to further rate hikes, not just holds, and that's showing up directly in the bond market -- the 10-year yield has climbed to 5.11% (+0.15 points) and the 2-year to 4.85% (+0.14), continuing a march higher that a separate overnight wire already flagged as pressuring futures. Equity futures are, for now, taking it in stride -- S&P futures +0.36%, Nasdaq futures leading at +0.65%, Dow futures +0.34% -- but the fact that gold is also up 0.92% to $4,337.40 while the dollar slips 0.30% is the tell that this isn't a simple growth-optimism rally; it looks more like markets pricing both higher stock prices and a real inflation/rate-path hedge at the same time, a combination that doesn't fit neatly into either a clean risk-on or risk-off box.

Gold climbing alongside equity futures while yields spike on Warsh's hawkish framework is a hedge, not a clean risk-on signal.

Costco (COST) topped quarterly earnings and revenue estimates this morning, though comparable-sales growth lagged expectations -- shares are down 0.58% premarket, a reminder that even a headline beat isn't moving the needle broadly when the market's attention is on the rate path instead. There's no major scheduled US economic release today; the next data catalysts are PCE and GDP on September 30 and the jobs report on October 2, so the tape between now and the close is likely to keep taking its cues from Fed commentary and the bond market rather than a fresh data print. VIX easing to 15.05 -- within a point of its 6-month low of 14.25 -- alongside a vol curve still in contango (VIX3M at 18.43) suggests options markets aren't yet pricing much near-term concern from the yield move, even as the 10-year sits at its highest levels in months.
