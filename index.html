ADP's Soft August Print Unwinds the Overnight Bond Scare

A weaker-than-expected labor market reading is pulling yields and volatility back down together, but the relief isn't reaching tech evenly.
Markets Desk · Premarket

The overnight story was a bond sell-off tied to inflation fears and Iran-linked geopolitical risk that pushed the 10-year Treasury yield to its highest level since November 2023. That story has partially reversed this morning on the back of ADP's private payrolls report, which showed just 38,000 jobs added in August -- the smallest gain since January and short of expectations, per CNBC. A softer labor market reads as disinflationary and rate-cut-supportive, and the market is treating it that way: the 10-year yield has eased 0.42% to 4.78% and the VIX has dropped 1.47% to 16.10, both retreating from their overnight highs. That's the classic signature of a growth-scare-turned-relief-rally setup -- falling yields and falling volatility moving together -- and it's showing up broadly enough to lift S&P, Dow and Russell futures into positive territory, evidence the reaction isn't confined to one corner of the market.

A softer labor market is reading as rate-cut fuel, not fresh risk -- yields and volatility are both retreating together.

The one holdout is the Nasdaq, with futures down 0.11% even as the other three major benchmarks gain -- and the headlines point to why. Berkshire Hathaway CEO Greg Abel told CNBC there's "a lot more pushback" nationwide on data-center construction, a direct challenge to the AI-infrastructure buildout that's been carrying tech's outperformance, and CNBC separately reports Apple shares have become inversely correlated with their tech peers for the first time in 20 years -- together pointing to a tech sector pulling in different directions internally even as the broader tape catches a bid on the ADP-driven yield relief. There's no scheduled US economic release today (the next is Friday's jobs report), so the ADP print -- a private-sector proxy for that report -- is likely to keep setting the tone into the close, alongside any further reaction to Abel's data-center comments.

Berkshire's Abel Talks AI, Data Centers and Alphabet -- Apple Breaks From the Pack

A round of Berkshire CEO commentary and an unusual Apple correlation pattern are the morning's single-name stories.
Markets Desk
Berkshire Hathaway (BRK.A/BRK.B) --

CEO Greg Abel gave CNBC a wide-ranging set of comments this morning: he said there's "a lot more pushback" nationwide on data-center construction, a real headwind for the AI-infrastructure buildout theme; separately called Alphabet a "significant player" in AI after Berkshire grew its stake in the second quarter; and said Japanese bond yields aren't currently a challenge for trading houses. The data-center remark is the one with the clearest market tie -- it lines up with Nasdaq futures being the lone laggard in an otherwise green futures session.

Apple (AAPL) --

CNBC reports Apple shares are now moving inversely to their tech peers, something the stock hasn't done in 20 years. The divergence adds color to why the Nasdaq isn't moving as one block this morning even as the broader market gets relief from falling yields.

Chevron (CVX) --

Chevron announced a $7 billion investment to more than double production in Venezuela, part of a broader US push to expand oil production there. The news lands alongside a pullback in crude ($89.64, -0.64%) and separate Energy Department comments on Hormuz shipping volumes, but the headlines don't tie a specific price move to this story.
