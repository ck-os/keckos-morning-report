Yield Spike Puts Fed's Warsh on the Spot as Mortgage Rates Hit Multi-Year High

CNBC reports the Treasury selloff is a genuine new headache for Fed policy, not just a bond-market blip, and it's already showing up in the mortgage market.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Treasury yields keep grinding higher this morning, with the 10-year at 5.16% (+0.94%) and the 2-year at 4.85% -- and CNBC reports the move is posing what it calls "a brand new problem" for Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, with markets pricing in a firmer central-bank response on inflation even though, as the piece notes, that's not a simple call to make. This isn't an abstract bond-market story: the same yield surge already pushed the 30-year fixed mortgage rate to 7.45% on Thursday, its highest since April 2024, a direct read-through into the real economy. Equity futures are barely reacting on the surface -- S&P futures -0.04%, Dow futures -0.01%, Nasdaq futures actually +0.08% -- but the VIX's 3.23% jump to 15.67 and a firmer gold price ($4,305.90, +0.18%) both point to more unease underneath than the flat index futures alone suggest, a sign the market is watching the rate move closely even before it fully shows up in equity prices.

The 10-year at 5.16% is now a real problem for the Fed, not just a bond-market curiosity -- it's already pushed the 30-year mortgage to 7.45%.

With no scheduled US economic release landing today -- the calendar's next entries are PCE and GDP on September 30 and the jobs report on October 2 -- today's tape is being driven entirely by the ongoing yield story rather than a fresh data print. Oil is the notable outlier against that backdrop, down 1.63% to $93.07, a divergence worth flagging since rising yields on inflation concern would typically travel with firmer, not softer, crude. Elsewhere, the Trump-Xi summit and state dinner are dominating the geopolitical wires but haven't produced a clear market reaction in the data pulled this morning -- worth watching for spillover into trade- or tech-policy-sensitive names given Xi's on-record push for U.S.-China cooperation on AI, but nothing sized yet.
