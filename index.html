Yields, VIX, and Oil All Retreat Into a Fed Decision Traders Still Expect to Be a Hike

A calmer cross-asset tape heading into this afternoon's announcement, even as the underlying rate-hike setup remains unusual and unresolved.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Today's Fed decision is the story, and it's an unusual one: CNBC reported traders see a 92% chance of a rate hike, not a cut, a setup that drove the S&P, Nasdaq, and Dow all lower into yesterday's close as the market priced it in. That tension has eased somewhat this morning -- the 10-year yield has slipped to 4.98%, VIX has dropped 2.33% to 16.80, and futures have pushed further into positive territory since the overnight edition, with Nasdaq futures (+0.59%) outpacing the Dow (+0.26%) and Russell (+0.22%). Crude has also pulled back 2.02% to $103.69 on reports of a surprise rise in U.S. inventories, a rare bit of relief after oil's Iran-conflict-driven run -- though gold's continued climb, up 1.11% to $4,380.90, shows the underlying geopolitical risk premium hasn't fully unwound, it's just being expressed differently across assets this morning.

Yields, VIX, and oil have all eased together into the Fed decision -- a broad, not narrow, pullback in risk pricing, even as gold keeps climbing on the same Iran-conflict backdrop.

Retail Sales prints today alongside the Fed's decision, giving the market a second data point to react to before the close -- a weak or strong read could easily reinforce or complicate whatever the Fed does this afternoon. Away from the macro story, chip-sector M&A chatter is also moving individual names: CNBC reports Intel and SK Hynix shares jumped on a report the two are discussing U.S. memory-chip manufacturing, a headline sitting alongside separate reporting that Micron's blowout quarter and $100 billion in committed revenue contracts left its stock sliding anyway -- a reminder that even strong AI-memory results are being read skeptically this week.
