Crude's 4.6% Slide Headlines a Quiet Premarket as Fed's Collins Flags Inflation Risk

Oil's sharpest drop of the week sits alongside hawkish Fed commentary and a barely-moving equity tape.
Markets Desk · Premarket

The clearest single move on the board this morning is oil: WTI crude (CL=F) is down 4.59% to $90.25, easily the largest swing across the entire Glance basket, described in early wire copy as part of a broader premarket mood of "cooling crude oil prices" following President Trump's UN address on Iran. That drop is landing the same morning Boston Fed President Susan Collins told reporters there's an "increased likelihood" inflation stays "notably" above the Fed's 2% target after she backed a rate hike -- an unusual pairing, since falling energy prices would typically ease rather than raise inflation concern. The 10-year yield is up modestly to 4.97% (+0.10%) and gold is down 0.63%, a combination that doesn't line up cleanly in either a classic risk-off (yields down, gold up) or risk-on (yields up, gold down, equities up) direction -- consistent with a market digesting a big commodity move and a hawkish Fed voice at the same time rather than trading one dominant narrative.

Oil's 4.6% slide is the loudest number on the board, but it's landing the same morning a sitting Fed president warned inflation risk is still skewed higher -- an unusual pairing, not a confirming one.

Equity futures themselves are essentially flat: S&P futures +0.04%, Nasdaq futures -0.05%, Dow futures -0.01%, and Russell 2000 futures the laggard at -0.31%, while the VIX sits calm at 14.15. The one pocket of real strength in the sector data is technology and communication services -- XLK is +2.89% and XLC +3.9% over the past day, with Meta (META, $741.31, +0.64% premarket) cited in headlines for a new Muse AI-commerce tie-up with Shopify that's drawing bullish coverage across the AI-adjacent trade; energy (XLE) is the clear sector laggard at -2.3%, consistent with crude's slide. There's no scheduled US economic data release today -- the next tracked print is PCE and GDP on September 30 -- so today's tape is being driven by the overnight oil move and Fed commentary rather than a data catalyst.

JPMorgan Lands $20B Mandate, Meta's AI-Commerce Push Draws Bullish Coverage

Two names stand out on real dealmaking and product news rather than broad market beta.
Markets Desk
JPMorgan Chase (JPM, $341.26, +0.33% premarket) —

JPMorgan Chase and the Qatar Investment Authority agreed to a $20 billion multi-market investment partnership spanning public equities and private credit, led by J.P. Morgan Asset Management and allocating capital across regions and asset classes for QIA. JPMorgan executives also pointed to record global dealmaking, with resilient M&A and IPO activity despite macro headwinds. Separately, JPMorgan Payments announced expanded cross-border payment capabilities with Thunes, extending faster payouts to bank accounts and mobile wallets across more than 100 corridors.

Meta (META, $741.31, +0.64% premarket) —

Meta's new AI shopping agent, Muse, is teaming up with Shopify to let consumers complete purchases directly through the AI tool, a tie-up Yahoo Finance coverage credited with lifting the stock. Coverage framed Meta's approach as differentiated from OpenAI and Anthropic's focus on raw model performance -- Meta's edge, per that reporting, is first-party purchase data letting it act as the marketplace itself rather than just the model layer.

Home Depot (HD, $305.28, -0.02% premarket) —

Home Depot issued a cautious outlook, saying inflation and elevated mortgage rates are pushing homeowners to fix only what's broken rather than fund larger renovation projects, a shift the company described as squeezing its core project-driven business. Separately, reporting noted Home Depot is among retailers receiving billions of dollars in tariff refunds, though research cited in that coverage found shoppers have already absorbed part of the original tariff cost.

Boeing (BA, $198.30, +0.29% premarket) —

Boeing's drone subsidiary Insitu and aviation Wi-Fi provider Gogo successfully tested a military drone using satellite communications, integrating a modified Gogo Galileo HDX antenna onto Insitu's Integrator drone -- part of a broader push by defense firms and governments to reduce reliance on Elon Musk's Starlink for military connectivity. Boeing was also mentioned in coverage of the upcoming Trump-Xi summit as investors watch for progress on tariffs and trade that could affect aerospace demand.

Amazon (AMZN, $255.90, +0.36% premarket) —

Amazon was named alongside Meta and Shopify in Yahoo Finance's roundup of stocks on the move this week, tied to the same AI-commerce storyline lifting Meta -- specific coverage of Amazon's own driver wasn't included in today's pulled headlines beyond that shared mention.
