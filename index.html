Nikkei Drops 2.5% as Bond Yields Push to Multi-Decade Highs

An overnight strike near the Strait of Hormuz turned a standing threat into a real escalation, rattling Asia and pushing global government bond yields higher; U.S. futures are down more modestly, and oil is actually easing despite the tension.
Markets Desk · Premarket

The U.S.-Iran standoff near the Strait of Hormuz turned from a standing threat into an actual incident overnight: a vessel sustained engine-room damage and a crew casualty near Oman, with the Omani Coast Guard assisting survivors. Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 2.5% on the news — the sharpest single-day move on the index in a month — while global government bond yields pushed to multi-decade highs as investors priced in the risk of a prolonged disruption. The 10-year Treasury yield ticked up to 4.72%, closing in on its 52-week high of 4.75%, and the VIX jumped a real 4.7% to 15.91. Hong Kong's Hang Seng was little changed (+0.07%), while Germany's DAX (-0.41%) and London's FTSE 100 (-0.04%) both eased alongside the risk-off tone.

A standing threat became an actual strike overnight — and bond markets, not just equities, are the ones pricing in the risk.

U.S. futures are pulling back more modestly than Asia did: S&P futures -0.16%, Nasdaq futures -0.55%, Dow futures actually higher (+0.12%), Russell 2000 futures -0.10%. Oil is worth a second look here — one wire report framed crude as rising on Hormuz disruption risk, but WTI is actually down 1.33% to $84.12 as of this pull, either a fade from an earlier spike or a genuine divergence from that narrative; taking the live number over the headline framing. Gold and the dollar are both little changed, and bitcoin is down a modest 0.55%. A separate, supportive thread worth noting against the risk-off headline: South Korean retail investors are reportedly rotating out of their own market and into U.S. stocks in size, a capital-flow story distinct from this morning's geopolitical news. Treat every figure above as thin premarket positioning — it can move again well before the 9:30 open.

Company Briefs

Home Depot Beats, L3Harris Ousts Its CEO

Two distinct company stories this morning: an earnings beat with reaffirmed guidance, and a governance shake-up with no severance paid.
Markets Desk
Home Depot (HD) —

Q2 results beat expectations on both top and bottom lines, with U.S. comparable-store sales up 1.3% as homeowners took on smaller, spring-maintenance-style projects rather than large renovations amid "frozen housing market conditions." Home Depot reaffirmed its full-year outlook and announced a nationwide launch of express delivery. Management described broad-based demand across the business despite continuing economic uncertainty.

L3Harris Technologies (LHX) —

The board removed Chairman and CEO Christopher Kubasik after an investigation found conduct inconsistent with the company's code of conduct — terminated without severance, forfeiting roughly $45 million in outstanding equity, a notably firm governance stance. Longtime insider Sam Mehta was named the new CEO, and the company reaffirmed its full-year 2026 guidance despite the leadership shake-up. The stock dropped about 4% on the news, and coverage has focused as much on the company's unusually tight-lipped handling of the ouster as on the succession itself.
