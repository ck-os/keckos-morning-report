Payrolls Beat Triples Expectations -- and the Bond Market Shrugs, Not Sells

A jobs surprise big enough to complicate the Fed's rate-hold case is landing as a cross-asset split, not a clean directional move.
Markets Desk · Premarket

August nonfarm payrolls rose 162,000, roughly three times the 53,000 economists had penciled in, with the unemployment rate holding steady at 4.1%, CNBC reported this morning citing Dow Jones consensus data. That's a genuinely large surprise, and it lands one day after Fed Governor Christopher Waller argued for holding rates steady provided inflation keeps cooling -- a beat this size is the kind of data point that could give the Fed less room to ease than that comment implied. Yet the reaction across the Glance basket is notably restrained rather than a violent repricing: the 10-year yield actually eased slightly to 4.75%, and equity futures split by index rather than moving as one -- Nasdaq futures inched up 0.14% while S&P (-0.20%), Dow (-0.26%), and Russell 2000 (-0.44%) futures all slipped. That the small-cap Russell is the weakest of the four is consistent with a stronger-than-expected jobs number reading as marginally less rate-cut-friendly, a dynamic small caps are typically most sensitive to given their financing costs.

Payrolls tripled expectations, but the 10-year yield eased anyway -- a beat this size landing without a clean directional move across the tape.

The clearest single-stock story this morning has nothing to do with jobs: Lululemon posted another quarter of disappointing results and a sales slowdown, and CNBC reports the stock is plunging roughly 20% premarket as the company continues to struggle to turn its business around. Elsewhere, gold ($4,449.20, -0.95%) and oil ($90.33, -1.06%) are both falling alongside a firmer dollar (99.37, +0.37%), a combination that reads less like fear leaving the market and more like a reassessment of how much easing room the jobs beat actually leaves the Fed -- reinforced by Mohamed El-Erian telling CNBC this morning that the global bond sell-off "likely isn't over," even as today's yield move itself was small. With the jobs report already out, the next scheduled catalysts are PPI and CPI next week (September 10 and 11) and the FOMC decision on September 16 -- all of which the market will now read through the lens of today's surprise beat.
