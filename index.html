Yields and Oil Climb Together Into This Morning's PPI Print

A rates-and-energy story, not a risk-off one -- gold's muted response says this reads as inflation concern, not a safety bid.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Treasury yields are pushing higher into this morning's Producer Price Index release, with CNBC reporting traders positioning defensively ahead of the print; the 10-year sits at 4.84%, up 0.65% on the session. Layered on top is crude oil's climb to $97.43 (+1.44%), with overnight coverage from CNBC tying part of that move to escalating U.S.-Iran tension near the Strait of Hormuz after President Trump warned Tehran over activity at a suspected nuclear site. The combination -- rising yields, rising oil, and a VIX up 1.22% to 16.66 -- is showing up broadly across the tape rather than in one corner of it: Nasdaq futures are the weakest of the four major contracts (-0.37%), a reasonable consequence of rate-sensitive growth and mega-cap tech carrying more duration risk than the Dow (+0.18%) or the flat Russell 2000. Notably, gold is up only 0.20% despite the same backdrop -- a muted response for a classic flight-to-safety asset, which supports reading this morning's move as inflation-anxiety-driven rather than a broader risk-off shift.

Gold's muted 0.20% gain alongside rising yields and oil is the tell: this reads as inflation anxiety, not a flight to safety.

Inside the S&P sector basket, energy (XLE) is the day's standout at +0.83%, tracking crude's climb, while industrials (XLI, -1.51%) and consumer discretionary (XLY, -1.34%) are leading the laggards -- a pattern consistent with higher input costs and higher financing rates squeezing cyclical names first. Today's PPI release, due later this morning, is the next scheduled catalyst that could move the tape materially before the close: a hot print would likely reinforce the yield move already underway, while a soft one could unwind some of this morning's defensiveness ahead of tomorrow's CPI data.
