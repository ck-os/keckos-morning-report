Oil-Yield Lockstep Weighs on Futures Ahead of Fed Decision

Crude's spike and a seven-year-tight correlation with Treasury yields are pressuring risk assets broadly, a day ahead of the FOMC.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Crude oil is the load-bearing story this morning: WTI (CL=F) is up 2.16% to $103.58 as Saudi Arabia races to restore a key pipeline before, CNBC reports, an estimated five-to-seven day inventory cushion runs dry -- and analysts warn prices could rally further if the outage persists. What makes this more than an energy-sector story is a second CNBC piece out this morning noting oil and the 10-year Treasury yield are now moving in near lockstep, with their correlation at its strongest since 2019; sure enough, the 10-year yield has pushed to its highest level since 2007 as traders increasingly price in a Fed rate hike -- not a cut -- at Wednesday's decision. That combination is showing up as a broad, if modest, pullback across futures: S&P (-0.35%), Nasdaq (-0.37%), Dow (-0.47%), and Russell (-0.45%) are all lower, with the VIX up 1.75% to 17.40.

Oil and the 10-year yield are moving in their tightest lockstep since 2019 -- and both are working against equities the same morning.

Dow futures are underperforming the other three benchmarks this morning (-0.47% vs. -0.35% for the S&P), consistent with the tape's rate-hike-scare framing hitting the more rate-sensitive, cyclically-tilted index harder. Gold's 0.96% decline to $4,310.30 is the tell that this isn't a classic flight-to-safety move -- a genuine risk-off day typically sends gold higher alongside falling yields, but here yields are rising and gold is falling together, which points to an inflation/rate-repricing scare rather than a growth scare. Nothing is scheduled to print today, but tomorrow's back-to-back Retail Sales report and FOMC rate decision (Wednesday, September 16) sit directly behind this move -- Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is also due to testify before Congress today and may face direct questions on rising energy prices and Fed policy, per CNBC.
