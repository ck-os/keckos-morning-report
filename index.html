Cooler PCE, Firmer Yields: A Market Waiting on Nvidia, Not Celebrating Inflation

The inflation data undershot forecasts, but futures, yields, and the VIX are all reading it as noise ahead of bigger catalysts, not a green light.
Markets Desk · Premarket

The headline this morning is a genuine disinflation surprise: core PCE, the Fed's preferred gauge, rose 3.3% annually in July versus the 3.6% economists expected. In a textbook world that print should have sent futures higher and yields lower. Instead, the 10-year yield ticked up to 4.64% and the VIX rose 1.55% to 15.69, while S&P futures stayed down 0.10% and Nasdaq futures down 0.33% -- a mismatch between a dovish data point and a market that isn't rewarding it. Gold's 0.45% decline breaks the usual flight-to-safety pairing with a rising VIX, and the dollar index is up only marginally (+0.06%), so this isn't a coordinated risk-off move either. The more coherent read: with GDP data also due today and Nvidia's earnings looming, positioning is dominated by what's still ahead of the tape rather than what just landed.

A cooler-than-expected inflation print that didn't move futures higher is itself the story -- this is a market waiting on GDP and Nvidia, not one reacting to PCE.

The VIX's 1.55% rise to 15.69 is the standout mover in the Glance basket this morning, nudging above its recent range even as the inflation news should, in isolation, have calmed nerves -- a sign the market is bracing for today's GDP release and Nvidia's earnings rather than the PCE data itself. That GDP figure lands later this morning per the standing economic calendar, and given it's arriving the same day as the inflation read, it's reasonable to expect it -- alongside Nvidia's results after the bell -- to carry more weight for the rest of the session than this morning's premarket drift.

Boston Scientific Slides on Cyberattack; Amazon Retires a 20-Year-Old Crowdsourcing Tool

One name is dealing with an operational disruption; the rest of today's company news is more structural than market-moving.
Markets Desk
Boston Scientific (BSX) --

Shares are falling premarket after the medical device maker confirmed a cyberattack that has disrupted its ability to ship products, with no clear timeline yet for when services will be restored. It's a genuine operational story -- a named disruption to shipping and fulfillment, not just a headline -- and one to watch for follow-through once the extent of the outage and any customer/revenue impact becomes clearer.

Amazon (AMZN) --

The company is shutting down Mechanical Turk, the crowdsourced task-labor platform Jeff Bezos once dubbed 'artificial artificial intelligence' when it launched in 2005. It's a symbolic bookend more than a market-moving event for a company Amazon's size, but it lands amid a broader news cycle about AI infrastructure spending and where automation is actually replacing versus complementing human labor.
