Oil's Slide Sets the Tone as Yields Ease and Growth Stocks Lead

A sharp drop in crude and falling global yields are lifting risk appetite broadly, with the move concentrated more in growth-tilted futures than cyclicals.
Markets Desk · Premarket

West Texas Intermediate crude ($94.11) is down 6.17% this morning, the day's standout cross-asset move, as easing concerns over Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline shutdown and reporting on Big Oil's resilient production levels take some of the geopolitical risk premium out of the barrel. CNBC also reports Treasury yields easing globally this morning "following last week's interest rate bonanza," a combination that -- alongside a still-contained VIX at 14.95 (+0.95%) -- is feeding into a broadly risk-on premarket tape ahead of Thursday's Trump-Xi summit, for which Treasury Secretary Bessent has already called his own pre-summit meeting with China's Vice Premier He Lifeng "successful." Nasdaq futures are leading the four major contracts at +1.14%, well ahead of the Dow's +0.61%, consistent with falling yields disproportionately benefiting the growth- and tech-heavy Nasdaq composition; bitcoin's 4.4% jump to $84,715 suggests speculative risk appetite is running even hotter than the equity complex.

Crude's 6% slide and easing global yields are doing the work today -- and the Nasdaq's lead over the Dow shows exactly where that's landing hardest.

Within the notable-companies watchlist, energy majors Exxon Mobil (XOM, $162.48, -0.43%) and Chevron (CVX, $208.50, -0.43%) are both seeing outsized activity this morning, tracking directly with crude's slide even as both companies used the Gastech conference in Bangkok to publicly double down on long-term LNG demand, per Insider Monkey -- a case of a shared macro headwind (falling oil) outweighing company-specific bullish messaging. No major U.S. economic release lands today; the next scheduled prints are PCE and GDP on September 30 and the jobs report on October 2, so the tape between now and the close is more likely to be driven by continued positioning ahead of Thursday's Trump-Xi summit than by a data surprise.
