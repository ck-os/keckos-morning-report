Oil Jumps on Mideast Strikes, Dragging Futures Lower

A weekend of U.S.-Iran strikes is pushing crude toward $100 and putting a risk-off tilt across equities, yields, and volatility alike.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Crude oil is the story this morning: CNBC reports the U.S. and Iran traded strikes over the weekend, with Saudi energy sites hit and Brent crude nearing $100 as renewed Mideast hostilities escalate. WTI crude (CL=F) is up 2.45% to $93.72, the single largest move anywhere in today's Glance basket, and it's already visible in equities -- Exxon Mobil (XOM, $162.04, +1.62%) and Chevron (CVX, $211.10, +1.23%) sit atop today's notable-company movers, both direct beneficiaries of the energy story. Eighteen maritime authorities separately warned Tuesday that the shadow fleet and geopolitical turmoil threaten to undermine global shipping altogether, reinforcing that this isn't an isolated headline. Dow futures are down 0.75%, more than double the S&P's 0.24% decline, which tracks the Dow's heavier industrial and value-sector weighting versus the Nasdaq's relative insulation (NQ=F is roughly flat, +0.03%) -- the same reason XLE (-0.87% today despite the crude spike, likely on demand-destruction worry) and XLI diverge in sector performance.

Crude's 2.45% jump on weekend U.S.-Iran strikes is dragging the Dow more than the Nasdaq -- exactly what you'd expect given where the energy exposure sits.

The cross-asset pattern is confirming a genuine risk-off tilt rather than an isolated equity dip: the 10-year yield is up to 4.78%, the VIX has risen 1.76% to 15.57, and gold is up a modest 0.39% -- a mixed rather than textbook flight-to-safety signature, since yields rising alongside a firmer VIX is more consistent with an inflation/oil-shock worry than a pure safety bid. Separately, Novartis shares fell 9% on three drug-trial setbacks including its del-desiran readout, a story CNBC frames as raising the stakes for Eli Lilly (LLY, $1,144.42, -0.43%) and Amgen in the Lp(a) cholesterol-drug race -- a genuine sector-wide read-through even though neither of those two names is posting an outsized move yet. No major U.S. economic data lands today; the next scheduled releases are PPI on Thursday and CPI on Friday, both worth watching for whether the oil-driven cost pressure shows up in the inflation prints.
