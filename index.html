Small Caps Lead a Broad Risk-Off Move as Yields and Oil Both Climb

The overnight caution has carried into the afternoon, with volatility elevated and safe-haven and inflation-sensitive assets moving alongside equities.
Markets Desk · Premarket

The risk-off tone that opened the day has not faded. S&P 500 futures (ES=F) are down 0.92% to 7,667.75, but the real story is beneath the headline index: Nasdaq futures (NQ=F) are off 1.00%, Dow futures (YM=F) are down 1.31%, and Russell 2000 futures (RTY=F) are the session's laggard, down 1.55% to 2,998.80. The VIX has jumped 7.52% to 16.01, its highest reading in weeks, while the 10-year Treasury yield (^TNX) has ticked up 0.92% to 4.70% -- a combination that points to genuine repricing of risk rather than a single-stock story. Overseas, the picture is mixed: the Nikkei 225 closed up 1.2% and the Hang Seng gained 0.8%, while Europe is softer, with Germany's DAX down 0.42% and London's FTSE roughly flat at +0.04%.

Small-cap futures are down more than 1.5% while bitcoin is up nearly 5% -- a split tape that resists a single explanation.

Commodities are telling a more complicated story than a simple flight to safety. Crude oil (CL=F) has extended its gain to 2.04%, trading near $86.21, and gold (GC=F) is up a more modest 0.43% to $4,575.10 -- both consistent with inflation-sensitive positioning rather than pure defensiveness. The dollar index (DX-Y.NYB) is essentially flat, up just 0.06%, while bitcoin (BTC-USD) is the day's standout gainer across any asset class, up 4.95% to $72,732 -- a divergence from the equity futures and VIX move that resists tidy explanation. Underneath the market-wide numbers, a Treasury-market storyline is also in view: reporting today describes Treasury Secretary Bessent's efforts to influence long-term yields as so far ineffective, and a separate piece frames a brewing tension between Bessent and Fed Chair Warsh over how far the central bank should coordinate with fiscal policy on bonds and the balance sheet -- a thread worth watching given today's yield move, though not one this report can size as a direct cause.

Walmart Slides on Cautious Outlook Despite the Earnings Headline

Walmart shares have tumbled roughly 9% after the retailer's fiscal second-quarter report failed to reassure Wall Street on its outlook, even as the company was widely watched today as a bellwether read on the broader, so-called K-shaped consumer economy.
