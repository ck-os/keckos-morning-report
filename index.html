VIX Collapses 12.82% as Post-Fed Relief Rally Carries Into Thursday's Premarket

Volatility and yields both retreated sharply overnight, a textbook confirmation of Wednesday's oil-driven relief rally rather than a fresh catalyst of its own.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Wednesday's Fed decision -- a 25 basis point hike, the first since 2023 -- was the actual catalyst behind this week's move, and the market's reaction wasn't fear but relief: Stocktwits reports the S&P 500 and Nasdaq closed at their best levels in six weeks, retaking their 50-day moving averages as falling oil prices eased inflation concerns that might otherwise have accompanied a hike. That relief carried straight into this morning's data: the VIX is down 12.82% to 15.44, and the 10-year yield has slipped 1.18% to 4.95%, both consistent with a market that came away from the Fed meeting more comfortable, not less. WTI crude's 1.25% drop to $101.15 is the specific mechanism CNBC and Stocktwits point to -- lower energy costs taking pressure off the inflation outlook right as the Fed just moved on rates, which is why the vol and yield reaction is so much larger than the move in equity futures themselves (S&P futures -0.09%, Nasdaq futures -0.17%, Dow futures -0.09%). Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang telling CNBC the company expects to sell twice as many AI chips next year adds a bullish undertone specific to the tech-heavy Nasdaq, though it isn't yet showing up as a distinct premarket move in the index futures.

The VIX's 12.82% collapse and a falling 10-year yield are confirming Wednesday's relief rally, not producing a new one.

The standout mover in this morning's Glance basket is unambiguously the VIX, down 12.82% to 15.44 -- a move of that size this far from the open typically reflects a genuine reassessment of risk rather than routine premarket noise, and it lines up with vol_term data showing the curve back in contango (VIX3M at 18.55, a 3.11-point spread) rather than the flattened or inverted curve that usually accompanies real stress. Oil's slide to $101.15 is the second-biggest mover and, per the news above, is the actual mechanism behind Wednesday's rally, easing the inflation math the Fed had to weigh into its hike. There's no scheduled US economic release today -- the next dated items on the calendar are PCE and GDP on September 30 and the jobs report on October 2 -- so today's premarket drift is best read as digestion of Wednesday's Fed decision rather than positioning ahead of a new data point.

Boeing Slides Against the Tape, GE Aerospace and AMD See Elevated Activity

A handful of names are trading against or ahead of the broader market's post-Fed calm, each on its own specific story.
Markets Desk
Boeing (BA, $197.45, +0.23% premarket) —

Boeing shares fell in the prior session even as the broader market rallied, with Zacks noting a roughly 2.5% decline against a backdrop of the S&P and Nasdaq posting their best day in six weeks -- a divergence that stands out given nothing in the day's news pointed to company-specific bad news, making it one of the more notable relative laggards of the session.

AMD (AMD, $545.12, +0.01% premarket) —

AMD was cited alongside SpaceX and Moderna in Investor's Business Daily's rundown of names flashing bullish technical signals as the S&P and Nasdaq reclaimed their 50-day lines following the Fed's rate decision, a sign the chipmaker is being grouped with the market's other high-beta rebound stories this week.

GE Aerospace (GE, $313.60, +0.04% premarket) —

GE Aerospace disclosed an $11.75 billion acquisition of castings maker Consolidated Precision Products, its largest deal since becoming a standalone company in 2024, aimed at securing supply of turbine-blade components that have been a persistent bottleneck across the jet-engine industry -- alongside separate commentary from CFO Rahul Ghai at a Morgan Stanley conference pointing to strong demand and a $170 billion services backlog.
