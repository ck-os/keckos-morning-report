Yields Climb Through a Cooler Inflation Print as Crude Does the Morning's Real Moving

Half an hour into the open, the tape looks less like a reaction to today's data than a market that's already moved past it.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Thirty minutes into the cash session, the pattern that stood out in the premarket edition has essentially held rather than resolved: core PCE printed a genuinely cooler 3.3% annually against a 3.6% forecast, and today's GDP release has also now landed, yet the 10-year yield has continued climbing rather than falling, up another leg to 4.66% (+0.54% on the day). That's the opposite of the textbook reaction to soft inflation data, and it's paired with a firmer dollar (99.15, +0.18%) and gold giving back 0.40% -- none of the classic flight-to-safety or dovish-reprice signatures are showing up together. Equity futures reflect that ambivalence directly: the S&P is barely positive (+0.09%), the Nasdaq is flat, and the Dow is marginally negative, which reads as a market that already priced in the cooler print before the open and is now waiting on something else -- likely Jackson Hole positioning, which CNBC reports has bond and dollar markets already 'on edge' amid pressure on Fed governor Warsh from Treasury Secretary Bessent's recent market intervention.

Yields climbing through a cooler-than-expected inflation print is the tell here -- this tape isn't trading the data, it's trading what comes after it.

The clearest mover in the Glance basket by a wide margin is crude oil, up 1.32% to $81.33, enough on its own to cross this edition's chart-generation threshold; nothing else in the basket is moving with comparable force. Today's calendar carried real weight before the open -- both the PCE inflation gauge and GDP were scheduled for release today, and CNBC's own reporting on the print (core prices +3.3% annually, below the 3.6% consensus) confirms the data has already been digested by the time of this edition; the muted equity reaction alongside still-rising yields suggests the market's attention has largely rotated to Jackson Hole positioning rather than lingering on this morning's numbers.

Kohl's Slides Despite a Beat, Boston Scientific Hit by Cyberattack

Two names are moving on company-specific news this morning, both for reasons that go beyond the headline number.
Markets Desk
Kohl's (KSS) --

Shares are down roughly 6% despite a blowout earnings beat, raised full-year guidance, and a $150 million tariff refund -- a reaction that stands in sharp contrast to peers Ross and TJX, which are little changed. The disconnect points to something buried in the margin detail that's giving investors pause even as the headline numbers look strong, a reminder that a guidance raise alone doesn't guarantee a positive reaction when the underlying quality of earnings is in question.

Boston Scientific (BSX) --

Shares are falling after the medical device maker confirmed it's been hit by a cyberattack that has disrupted its ability to ship products, with no clear timeline yet for restoring service. This is an operational disruption story rather than a demand or guidance issue -- the risk is to near-term shipping and revenue recognition, not the underlying business thesis, pending more clarity on how long the disruption lasts.
