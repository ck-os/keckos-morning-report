Oil's Slide Deepens as Risk Appetite Holds Into Summit Week

A broad, cross-asset risk-on tape -- lower yields, a soft dollar, gains everywhere in equities -- built on top of a sharply lower oil market, even as a structural sentiment read stays cautious underneath it.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Crude oil is the story again this morning, and it's gotten louder since the open: WTI crude ($93.60) is down 6.68%, extending the overnight slide as easing fears over a broader Mideast supply disruption keep a lid on the barrel even against the Saudi East-West pipeline shutdown that CNBC reports is squeezing crude supply for Asian importers, with South Korea the most directly exposed. That's the same dynamic driving the risk-on tone across futures: with oil-driven inflation pressure easing, the 10-year yield has fallen to 4.95% (-0.86%), and lower yields are showing up asymmetrically across the Glance basket -- Nasdaq futures (+1.05%) are outrunning the Dow (+0.85%) and S&P (+0.66%), consistent with tech's greater rate sensitivity and heavier index weight capturing more of the benefit from falling yields than industrial- and value-tilted benchmarks. Bitcoin has extended its rally further since the first edition, now at $85,239 (+5.05%) and its highest level since January, with CNBC noting the market is actively debating whether the 'crypto winter' is over.

Oil's slide is doing the work of easing yields, and easing yields are doing the work of lifting the Nasdaq more than the Dow.

The clearest single mover in the Glance strip remains crude, but on the equity side it's a genuinely broad tape rather than one name: every futures contract and every international index pulled this morning (Nikkei +1.38%, Hang Seng +1.18%, DAX +1.16%, FTSE +1.03%) is higher, and the VIX sitting at just 14.87 (+0.41%) confirms there's no hidden stress underneath the advance. No major US economic data lands today -- the next scheduled release is PCE and GDP on September 30 -- so today's tape is being driven by the oil/summit story and the ongoing bitcoin rally rather than a data catalyst; watch for headlines out of the run-up to Thursday's Trump-Xi summit, which CNBC and Stocktwits both flag as a live focus for sentiment into midweek.
