Iran Rhetoric Lifts Oil, Sends Risk Appetite Lower

Futures soften and volatility ticks up as crude jumps on renewed Iran tensions, while yields and crypto move on separate tracks.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Equity futures are pointing to a lower open, with Nasdaq futures off the most at -1.03% to 29,308.75, S&P futures down 0.6% to 7,692.00, the Dow down 0.76%, and small caps (Russell futures) down 0.86%. The VIX is up 5.57% to 15.72, a level move that lines up with the futures weakness rather than contradicting it. The clearest single catalyst is oil: WTI crude is up 3.25% to $87.24 after President Trump sharpened his rhetoric against Iran, threatening what he called an 'Economic D-Day' amid a stalled talks process, with the market pricing in renewed risk to flows through the Strait of Hormuz.

The VIX's 5.57% jump alongside broadly lower futures is the more telling signal than any single index move.

Elsewhere on the tape, the moves don't all point the same direction, which argues against reading this as one clean risk-off morning. The 10-year yield is down 1.13% to 4.65%, consistent with a bond-market rally reported overnight, and bitcoin is up 3.49% to $71,718 on Trump's public push for Congress to pass crypto-regulation legislation (the Clarity Act) -- a gain that runs counter to the equity-futures weakness. Gold, meanwhile, is down 0.99% to $4,510.60, an unusual pairing with rising geopolitical tension that's worth watching rather than explaining away. Overseas, Asia outperformed Europe: the Nikkei closed up 1.2% and the Hang Seng up 0.8%, while the DAX (-0.73%) and FTSE (-0.51%) are both lower, a split that keeps this from reading as a single global narrative.
