Oil Spikes on Iran Tensions While Yields Cool and Futures Slip

A geopolitical jolt in crude is setting the tone, even as bond yields ease and risk appetite stays selectively split.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Crude oil is the standout mover of the morning, up 3.21% to $87.20 a barrel after President Trump escalated his rhetoric toward Iran, threatening what he called an 'Economic D-Day' for the country and its backers. That geopolitical edge is weighing modestly on equity futures, with S&P 500 futures down 0.38%, Nasdaq futures off 0.75%, Dow futures down 0.50%, and small-cap Russell futures down 0.59%. The VIX is up 3.29% to 15.38, still a low absolute level but a notable pop that fits a morning where oil-driven uncertainty is the dominant theme. Not everything is playing defense, though: the 10-year Treasury yield eased 1.13% to 4.65%, bitcoin is up 3.70% to $71,865 on fresh White House support for crypto legislation, and the dollar index is essentially flat at 98.79.

Overseas, Asia outperformed Europe: the Nikkei 225 gained 1.2% to 66,216.79 and Hong Kong's Hang Seng added 0.8% to 25,698.49, while Germany's DAX slipped 0.55% to 25,984.62 and London's FTSE 100 eased 0.27% to 10,718.81. Gold, often a geopolitical hedge, is actually down 0.65% to $4,525.90, a reminder that this morning's oil move looks more supply/sanctions-driven than a broad flight-to-safety trade. Bond-market commentary is also circulating around a Treasury debt buyback plan and news that U.S. government debt has crossed $40 trillion, both worth watching as background context even though neither is moving prices as directly as the oil headlines this morning.

Company Briefs

Walmart Lifts Guidance, Alibaba Slides on AI Spending

Two large-cap earnings reactions are the clearest single-name stories of the morning.
Markets Desk
Walmart (WMT) --

Walmart raised its full-year outlook ahead of its fiscal second-quarter report Thursday, saying it will use a large tariff refund to keep prices low for shoppers. The report is being watched closely as a read on the broader, so-called K-shaped consumer economy -- whether higher- and lower-income shoppers are diverging in spending behavior -- rather than purely a company-specific print.

Alibaba (BABA) --

Alibaba's U.S.-listed shares fell roughly 3% premarket after the company reported a 75% drop in net income for its June quarter, which it attributed to heavy AI infrastructure spending. The move is part of a broader pattern flagged elsewhere this morning (see Barchart's bubble commentary in The Wire) of investors starting to scrutinize the gap between AI capital spending and near-term earnings across the sector.
