Oil and bitcoin surge as stock futures ease back

A firmer risk tone in commodities and crypto contrasts with modestly softer equity futures ahead of Walmart's earnings.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Stock futures are pointing to a quieter, slightly lower open, with S&P 500 futures (ES=F) off 0.21% to 7,722.50, Nasdaq futures (NQ=F) down 0.47% to 29,473.00, Dow futures (YM=F) down 0.26% to 53,416, and Russell 2000 futures (RTY=F) down 0.32% to 3,036.30. The VIX has ticked up 1.95% to 15.18, still a low absolute level, while the 10-year Treasury yield (^TNX) eased 1.13% to 4.65% as bond markets extend Wednesday's rally even as traders weigh a new Treasury debt buyback plan. The standout move is in commodities: WTI crude (CL=F) is up 2.83% to $86.88 on the back of Trump's threatened 'Economic D-Day' pressure campaign against Iran, while gold (GC=F) is down 0.34% to $4,540.10 and the dollar index (DX-Y.NYB) is roughly flat at 98.73. Bitcoin (BTC-USD) has jumped 3.74% to $71,890 after Trump urged Congress to pass the crypto Clarity Act.

Oil's 2.83% jump on Iran pressure and bitcoin's 3.74% surge on crypto-legislation news stand out against a broadly softer, quieter equity futures tape.

Overseas, Asian markets outperformed European ones overnight: Japan's Nikkei 225 rose 1.2% to 66,216.79 and Hong Kong's Hang Seng added 0.8% to 25,698.49, while Germany's DAX slipped 0.47% to 26,004.40 and London's FTSE 100 eased 0.23% to 10,722.24. The split suggests the pressure on European equities and the modest pullback in U.S. futures are more about digesting recent gains and today's Walmart earnings than any single overnight catalyst, while Asia continued to build on its own momentum.
