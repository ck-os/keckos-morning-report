Calm Index Futures Mask a Sharper Split Between Oil and Everything Else

Equities, yields, and volatility are all still leaning the same easy direction, but oil's steepening slide and a flat gold tape complicate the read.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Futures, yields, and the VIX are still telling a consistent story into the 8:45 window: Nasdaq futures up 0.57%, the 10-year yield down another leg to 4.67% (-0.77%), and the VIX barely moved at 15.82 -- equities, rates, and volatility all leaning the same easy-risk direction rather than fighting each other. The one piece that doesn't fit is crude, now down 3.63% to $82.33 after CNBC reported the U.S. plans to return evacuated diplomats to the Middle East, a de-escalation signal that's pulling energy lower even as everything else drifts higher. Gold's near-flat print (+0.03%) is the tell that this isn't a clean flight-to-safety or flight-to-risk morning in either direction -- if it were pure risk-on, gold would typically soften with falling yields losing their safe-haven pull; if it were fear, gold would be firmer alongside falling stocks. Instead it's sitting still while stocks, yields, and oil all move, which reads more like a single geopolitical de-escalation story doing the work on oil specifically than a broad cross-asset repricing.

Gold's flat print alongside easier yields and firmer futures is the tell that this isn't a clean risk-on or flight-to-safety morning -- something narrower is doing the work.

Beneath the calm futures strip, the standout mover in the Glance basket is unambiguously crude, extending its overnight decline to -3.63% on the diplomat-return headline -- CNBC's own reporting is the direct attribution here, not just a coincidence of timing. There's no major US economic release scheduled today; PCE and GDP are the next data points due, both landing Wednesday, August 26, so today's tape is trading on the oil headline and individual earnings reactions (Dick's Sporting Goods, Intel) rather than a scheduled macro catalyst.

Dick's Sporting Goods Slides on a Miss, Intel's Skid Draws a Contrarian Call

One clean earnings-driven mover and one stock still absorbing a rough week, from opposite directions.
Markets Desk
Dick's Sporting Goods (DKS) --

Shares fell 15% after second-quarter results missed Wall Street expectations, with the retailer citing a "challenging" footwear market. It's a clean, earnings-driven move rather than a broad sector story -- CNBC's reporting ties the decline directly to the guidance and the miss, not to any wider consumer-discretionary weakness showing up elsewhere in today's tape.

Intel (INTC) --

Intel's stock has shed roughly 15% over the past week, and HSBC used the pullback to issue a price target implying about 130% upside from here, according to 24/7 Wall St. -- a call the piece itself frames as resting on Intel's foundry business, which has yet to turn a profit. It's an outlier bullish view against a stock still working through a rough stretch, not a signal the broader chip complex has turned.
