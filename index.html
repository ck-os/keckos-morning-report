Iran's Hormuz Diplomacy Signal Sinks Oil, Energy Stocks Follow

A reported Iranian offer to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within a week is unwinding the war-risk premium in crude, while yesterday's AI-led rally takes a breather.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Crude oil is the story this morning, down 5.58% to $90.44 after Iran reportedly told the UN General Assembly it could reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days if the U.S. eases military pressure, and after Tuesday's fresh sanctions on Iranian airlines gave way overnight to renewed optimism about U.S.-Iran diplomacy. That's a sharp reversal of the geopolitical premium that had been supporting crude, and it's dragging energy stocks down with it -- Exxon Mobil (XOM, $156.58, -1.15%) and Chevron (CVX, $201.80, -0.97%) are both among the watchlist's worst performers, and the sector ETF XLE is off 2.3% on the day, the steepest sector decline in the Market KPI data. Layered on top is a pause in yesterday's AI trade: the Nasdaq closed nearly 3% higher Monday as AMD crossed a $1 trillion market cap, but AMD (AMD, $608.64, -1.12%) is giving some of that back this morning, and Nasdaq futures are flat (-0.01%) versus a firmer Russell 2000 (+0.40%) and Dow (+0.18%), suggesting yesterday's move was concentrated in a handful of AI names rather than broad-based.

The clearest mover in the Glance basket remains oil's 5.58% drop, and it lines up with a broader risk-easing signature: gold is down 0.81% and the 10-year yield has eased to 4.96%, while the VIX sits low at 14.78 -- none of the classic flight-to-safety combination (falling yields, rising gold) is present here, which is consistent with a story about a specific geopolitical premium unwinding rather than a broader risk-off move. There's no major U.S. economic release scheduled today -- the calendar's next data points are PCE and GDP on September 30 and the jobs report on October 2 -- so today's tape is being driven by the Iran headlines and the digestion of Monday's AI rally rather than a scheduled data catalyst.
