Oil Slides on Energy Ceasefire Talk as AI Names Drive Tech Higher

A cooling geopolitical risk premium in crude and a continued AI-sector bid are pulling markets in different directions this morning.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Crude oil is the standout move of the morning, down 4.49% to $90.34, as Ukraine's Zelenskyy called for an 'energy ceasefire' with Russia and Iran floated conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz -- both easing the geopolitical risk premium that's been embedded in energy prices in recent weeks. That de-escalation is showing up as a genuine relief signal rather than a demand-side worry: gold is also softer, down 0.73% to $4,344.30, and the dollar index is roughly flat, a combination more consistent with falling risk premium than falling growth expectations. Layered on top is a distinct AI-sector story: Meta (META, $737.23, +0.09% premarket) unveiled its new Muse model and headlines are highlighting intensifying competition between Anthropic and OpenAI, and that enthusiasm is concentrated enough to show up clearly in sector data -- XLK up 2.89% and XLC up 3.9% on the day -- even though the broader equity futures basket (S&P futures -0.04%, Nasdaq futures -0.21%) isn't yet reflecting it heavily in the premarket tape.

Oil's 4.5% slide reads as a geopolitical relief signal, not a growth scare -- gold is softer too, not spiking, alongside it.

The cross-current holding equity futures in check is the Fed: Boston Fed President Susan Collins warned this morning that there's an 'increased likelihood' inflation stays 'notably' above the 2% target, a notably hawkish tone that's keeping the 10-year yield near 4.97% (+0.10 points) even as oil eases. That's a genuine tension for the tape -- lower energy prices would normally ease inflation pressure, but a Fed official flagging persistent inflation risk anyway is keeping bond yields firm and small caps (Russell futures -0.42%) under more pressure than the large-cap indices. No major scheduled US economic release lands today -- the next data points on the calendar are GDP and the Fed's preferred PCE gauge on September 30, followed by the jobs report on October 2 -- so today's tape is being driven by the Iran/Ukraine de-escalation story and Fed commentary rather than a data print.

JPMorgan Lands $20B Mandate, Home Depot Flags Squeezed Shoppers

A handful of names are seeing real activity behind today's otherwise muted premarket moves.
Markets Desk
JPMorgan Chase (JPM, $340.65, +0.15% premarket) —

JPMorgan Chase and the Qatar Investment Authority agreed to a $20 billion multi-market investment partnership spanning public equities and private credit, led by J.P. Morgan Asset Management. Bank executives pointed to record global dealmaking and resilient M&A and IPO activity despite macro headwinds, and the firm separately expanded its cross-border payments capabilities with Thunes to more than 100 corridors.

Boeing (BA, $198.00, +0.14% premarket) —

Boeing remains stuck below key moving averages, with rival Embraer highlighted as breaking out while Boeing and Airbus lag. Separately, Jefferies analysts flagged that this week's Trump-Xi talks are unlikely to deliver breakthroughs on AI chips or rare earths, though a potential Board of Trade overseeing tariff reductions was called the most plausible deliverable -- one that could benefit Boeing if realized.

Home Depot (HD, $305.00, -0.13% premarket) —

Home Depot warned that squeezed American shoppers are cutting back on big home-improvement projects and sticking to only what's broken, citing inflation and elevated mortgage rates. The retailer is also among several -- alongside Walmart and Lowe's -- receiving billions in tariff refunds, though reporting suggests shoppers have already absorbed part of that cost.

Meta (META, $737.23, +0.09% premarket) —

Meta unveiled its new Muse AI model, drawing attention as a serious contender against OpenAI and Anthropic in the AI race, discussed prominently at Qualcomm's Snapdragon Summit. Separately, commentary is circulating warning about an AI 'concentration trap' in tech-heavy portfolios amid broader debate over whether the AI buildout risks becoming a bubble.

Amazon (AMZN, $255.80, +0.32% premarket) —

Amazon is being referenced in broader coverage of the intensifying AI model race, with Anthropic's new Claude Opus 5.5 and OpenAI's GPT-6 Sol and Luna models raising the competitive stakes across the sector. Separate commentary is warning retail investors about tech-concentration risk in portfolios amid the ongoing AI enthusiasm.
