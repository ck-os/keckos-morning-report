Chip Stocks Drag Asia Lower Overnight; U.S. Futures Barely Move

A heavy, AI-related selloff in chipmakers spread from Tokyo and Seoul into Europe overnight, but U.S. futures are little changed this morning as Treasury yields pull back from yesterday's highs and lingering Iran tension simmers in the background.
Markets Desk · Premarket

An overnight, AI-stock-led selloff pulled Asian and European markets sharply lower ahead of the U.S. open. Japan's Nikkei 225 sank 3.2% to its lowest close in two weeks, and the damage was even more concentrated in chipmakers: Samsung Electronics fell 7.8% and SK Hynix tumbled 9.8% in Seoul, spilling into European chip names as the session progressed. "Rising bond yields, higher oil prices, and renewed geopolitical concerns are weighing on investor appetite," StoneX's Matt Simpson told Barron's — a fair summary of a session with more than one thing going wrong at once.

Rising bond yields, higher oil prices, and renewed geopolitical concerns are weighing on investor appetite.

The tech selloff itself isn't new — it started yesterday, when a jump in Treasury yields pushed the SOXX semiconductor ETF to its worst day since July and dragged Micron, Sandisk, and Credo lower again in premarket trading today. What's different this morning is the U.S. reaction: the 10-year Treasury yield has actually eased back to 4.71%, down about four basis points overnight, and both S&P and Nasdaq futures are essentially flat. Whether that's genuine resilience or just Asia and Europe absorbing yesterday's damage a day late is one of the more interesting open questions for today's cash session.

Every one of the ten Markets-at-a-Glance metrics below is inside a normal overnight range — Dow and Russell futures are modestly higher, gold and oil are both up a little over a quarter of a percent, and the dollar index is softer. The VIX, at 15.87, is sitting near the low end of its past-year range, which reads as genuine calm rather than nervous quiet ahead of today's Fed minutes. Two other threads are running in the background without moving any single number enough to headline on their own: Iran-related tension continues to simmer (the UAE suspended trade with Tehran overnight, and Iran is reportedly weighing retaliatory targets in Europe), and Washington paused a scheduled 50% tariff escalation on Canada for three more days — a modest de-escalation that futures markets don't seem to be pricing as particularly meaningful either way. As always with premarket levels: thin volume, not fully settled, and can move again well before the 9:30 open.

Neither of those is a dated, calendared event — they're developing stories, a different kind of thing than a release with a fixed time on the clock. Company Briefs

Target Raises Its Outlook; Lowe's Turns Cautious

Two different mornings for the two big-box retailers: a clearer turnaround signal at Target, continued softness flagged at Lowe's.
Markets Desk
Target (TGT) —

The retailer raised its full-year outlook this morning; CNBC reports a rebound in comparable sales and a "huge tariff refund" boosting the bottom line. It's the clearest sign yet of traction in Target's multi-quarter turnaround — Investor's Business Daily notes the stock was already up 56% year-to-date heading into the print, which raises the bar for how the cash session ultimately judges today's number.

Lowe's (LOW) —

A more cautious morning for the other big home-improvement name: Lowe's gave a muted outlook and said it continues to see "pressure" in home-improvement spending, consistent with the softer DIY demand analysts had flagged heading into the print. The contrast with Target's morning is a useful reminder that "retail earnings" isn't one story this week — the same macro backdrop is landing differently depending on the business.
