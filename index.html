Small Caps Lead a Broad Futures Pullback as Yields, Oil Climb

Risk-off in equity futures, risk-on in crude and crypto -- a tape pulling in more than one direction at once.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Equity futures are lower across the board this morning, with the Russell 2000 (RTY=F) down 1.42% to 3,002.80, the Nasdaq (NQ=F) off 0.88% to 29,351.25, the Dow (YM=F) down 0.89% to 53,079, and the S&P (ES=F) down a comparatively milder 0.53% to 7,697.50. The VIX has jumped 6.85% to 15.91, and the 10-year Treasury yield (^TNX) is up 1.01% to 4.70%, a combination that points to markets pricing in a bit more near-term unease even from still-low absolute volatility levels. Treasury Secretary Bessent said the department's buyback operation in longer-dated securities, where yields have been rising, could total more than $4 billion -- a detail worth watching against that yield move.

Small caps are bearing the brunt of today's pullback, down 1.42% against a milder 0.53% S&P futures decline.

Commodities and crypto are telling a different story than equities. WTI crude (CL=F) is up 2.57% to $86.66 -- large enough to cross this report's chart threshold -- while gold (GC=F) is up a more modest 0.25% to $4,567 and the dollar index (DX-Y.NYB) is essentially flat, up 0.07% to 98.88. Bitcoin (BTC-USD) has jumped 4.51% to $72,422, part of a roughly 12% two-day surge tied to a Trump-backed push for the Clarity Act. Overseas, Asian markets are firmer -- the Nikkei 225 up 1.2% to 66,216.79 and the Hang Seng up 0.8% to 25,698.49 -- while Europe is mixed, with Germany's DAX down 0.42% and London's FTSE roughly flat at +0.04%.

Walmart Slides on Weakest Comp Sales Since 2020 Despite Raised Guidance

A tariff refund and a full-year outlook hike weren't enough to offset a soft same-store sales read.
Markets Desk
Walmart (WMT) --

Walmart shares are down roughly 8% this morning after reporting its slowest same-store sales growth since 2020, even as the company raised its full-year outlook and disclosed a large tariff refund. Reporting alongside it in the same earnings window, Target reportedly held steady and Costco eased only modestly -- a split that suggests today's pressure point is specific to Walmart's own quarter rather than a broad read-through on the consumer.
