Oil's Iran-Driven Spike Deepens, and the Cross-Asset Tape Confirms It's Broad

A geopolitical risk premium, not a single equity story, is showing up consistently across oil, volatility, gold, and the futures complex this morning.
Markets Desk · Premarket

The Strait of Hormuz clash reported overnight -- CNBC noted the U.S. denied Iran's claim it struck two American vessels near the strait -- is still the dominant story on the tape this morning, and if anything it has intensified since the first pull: WTI crude has extended its gain to +3.07%, now at $95.89, with Brent reportedly having touched $100 a barrel overnight. That kind of move in crude typically shows up as an inflation and risk worry rather than a pure energy-sector story, and the rest of the basket is confirming it: the VIX has climbed further to 16.51 (+5.03%), gold has pushed to $4,437.40 (+0.99%), and the 10-year yield has ticked up slightly to 4.81%. Gold rising alongside a firmer VIX and weaker equity futures is a textbook flight-to-safety signature -- not just stocks drifting on their own -- and it's showing up broadly: S&P futures are down 0.43%, Nasdaq futures down 0.63%, Dow futures down 0.74%, and Russell futures down 0.68%, a fairly even spread across the four rather than one index absorbing the whole move.

Inside the cash market, the sector tape shows exactly where the pressure is landing: XLV (healthcare) is down 2.52% today, XLF (financials) down 1.38%, and broader breadth confirms the weakness is unusually narrow-based -- the equal-weight S&P proxy (RSP) is down 1.04% against the cap-weighted SPY's 0.55% decline, meaning the mega-caps are cushioning the index-level damage while the average stock is faring notably worse. The one clear exception is Meta (META, $644.00, +4.97% premarket), still holding a standalone gain even as its megacap software peers Alphabet (GOOGL, $331.49, -2.03%) and Amazon (AMZN, $252.91, -1.58%) trade lower alongside the broader tape. No major U.S. economic release lands today -- PPI is scheduled for tomorrow and CPI for Friday -- so today's session is left to trade primarily on the oil/geopolitical headline flow rather than a scheduled data catalyst.
