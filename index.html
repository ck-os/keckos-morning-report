Strait of Hormuz Tensions Send Oil Past $100, Equities Into Broad Risk-Off Mode

A real geopolitical shock is showing up as a textbook cross-asset flight-to-safety pattern, not just an energy-sector move.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Brent crude crossed $100 a barrel for the first time since July on Wednesday as the U.S. and Iran traded strikes near the Strait of Hormuz, with Washington denying Tehran's claim that it struck two American vessels in retaliation for earlier attacks, according to CNBC. WTI crude (CL=F) is up 2.49% to $95.35 in the premarket reaction, and the knock-on effect is visible well beyond the energy complex: the VIX has jumped 4.45% to 16.42, gold is up 1.20% to $4,446.50, and all four major U.S. equity futures are lower, with S&P futures (ES=F) down 0.38%. The Dow (YM=F, -0.65%) is underperforming the Nasdaq (NQ=F, -0.51%) here -- a reversal of the usual tech-led weighting pattern -- consistent with a supply-shock story hitting industrial, transport, and financial-linked names harder than a typical risk-off session led by growth-stock derating.

Falling risk appetite, rising gold, and a jump in the VIX moving together on a real geopolitical headline is a textbook flight-to-safety signature, not stocks drifting on their own.

Within the Glance basket, the standout move is the VIX's 4.45% jump to 16.42 -- a genuine volatility repricing tied directly to the Strait of Hormuz escalation rather than a technical drift, and it lines up with today's sector data: XLE is the best-performing S&P sector on the day (+1.11%) while XLV (-2.52%) and XLF (-1.38%) lag, a rotation consistent with an oil-driven scare rather than a broad growth selloff. There's no major U.S. economic release scheduled today -- PPI lands tomorrow (Thursday, September 10) and CPI on Friday, both worth watching for whether inflation data compounds or offsets today's oil-driven price pressure, but for now the tape is trading almost entirely off the geopolitical headline rather than any data print.
