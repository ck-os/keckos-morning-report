Iran's Hormuz De-Escalation Deepens, Oil and VIX Both Extend Their Slide

The overnight risk-off premium is unwinding further, not reversing, as Iran's offer to restart nuclear talks holds.
Markets Desk · Premarket

The story moving markets this morning is the same one that set the tone overnight: Iran's offer to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days and restart nuclear talks, which per CNBC came alongside a new wave of Houthi missile and drone attacks on Saudi targets -- a reminder the region remains volatile even as the specific chokepoint risk eases. That de-escalation is showing up most clearly in crude, with WTI down 1.90% to $92.81, extending its overnight decline rather than stabilizing, and in the vol curve: VIX has fallen to 15.12, within a point of its 6-month low of 14.25, with the VIX3M spread still in contango at 3.31 points -- a curve shape consistent with markets pricing calm ahead rather than gathering stress. Equity futures are broadly higher but only modestly so (S&P +0.29%, Nasdaq +0.49%, Dow +0.29%, Russell +0.26%), a proportionate rather than euphoric reaction given the size of the move in oil and vol.

Oil's slide and VIX's approach to a 6-month low both confirm the same Iran de-escalation story -- but gold's continued gain says the safe-haven bid hasn't fully unwound.

Gold is the one instrument resisting the risk-on read, still up 0.96% to $4,339 even as stocks and oil both settle into a calmer posture -- a pattern that lines up with CNBC's reporting this morning that Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's push for a more hawkish policy framework, including the possibility of further rate hikes, is meeting internal resistance rather than being resolved. The 10-year yield ticking up to 5.11% (+0.15) alongside that story is consistent with markets still pricing some chance of a firmer Fed stance. There's no US economic data release scheduled today -- the next major prints (PCE and GDP) don't land until Wednesday, September 30 -- so the Iran story and the Fed leadership dynamic are likely to keep driving the tape into the weekend rather than a scheduled number.
