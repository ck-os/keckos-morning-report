Oil Spikes on Gulf Attacks, Yields Hit 19-Year High as Fed Hike Bets Build

A geopolitical shock in oil and a rate-hike repricing in bonds are running side by side without dragging equity futures down with them -- yet.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Crude oil is the morning's clearest mover, up 2.71% to $104.14, after CNBC reported fresh Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia and Iranian attacks on shipping in the Gulf overnight -- a direct geopolitical supply-risk premium, not a demand story. Layered on top of that, CNBC also reports the 10-year Treasury yield has risen to its highest level since 2007, with the sell-off in government debt deepening as investors increasingly price in a Fed rate hike rather than a cut at Wednesday's FOMC decision -- a striking reversal of the easing narrative that's dominated recent months, and one Stocktwits' own premarket sentiment read frames as leaving retail positioning on SPY and QQQ "extremely bearish." Equity futures haven't caught that fear yet: S&P and Nasdaq futures are both modestly higher. But the cross-asset confirmation is showing up elsewhere -- VIX is up 3.45% to 17.69 and gold is down 0.94%, an unusual pairing (equities up, gold down, volatility up) that suggests the market is still digesting which of the two stories, oil or rates, matters more, rather than confirming either one cleanly.

Oil is spiking on a real geopolitical shock while Treasury yields hit their highest since 2007 on rate-hike bets -- and equity futures haven't decided which story to believe yet.

Alphabet (GOOGL, $345.74, -1.04%) and Microsoft (MSFT, $500.32, -1.01%) are among the watchlist's bigger premarket decliners, continuing the AI-led tech pullback that Yahoo Finance's Ines Ferre and DA Davidson's Gil Luria described yesterday as investors respond to renewed calls to slow AI development, a theme Elon Musk also weighed in on publicly. That pullback lines up with today's sector KPI data showing XLK down 1.81% on the day, the weakest major sector, consistent with tech-specific rather than broad-market pressure. Nothing on today's date is scheduled on the economic calendar -- the next major catalysts are Wednesday's FOMC decision and retail sales report -- so between now and the open, the oil and yield moves described above, plus Scott Bessent's testimony before Congress today (per CNBC, on energy prices, Fed policy, and government debt), are the more immediate things capable of moving the tape.
