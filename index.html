Crude Climbs on Iran Rhetoric as Futures Sit Slightly Lower

A quiet premarket for equities masks a sharper move in oil and a cooling in yields.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Equity futures are pointing to a soft open, with S&P 500 futures (ES=F) off 0.12% to 7,729.25, Nasdaq futures (NQ=F) down 0.19% to 29,555.75, and small-caps lagging most with Russell futures (RTY=F) down 0.30% to 3,037.10. The more notable move is in crude: WTI (CL=F) is up 2.60% to $86.69 a barrel, tracking President Trump's escalating rhetoric threatening an "Economic D-Day" for Iran. The 10-year yield (^TNX) eased 1.13% to 4.65%, continuing a pullback from Wednesday's session as traders digest news that U.S. government debt has passed $40 trillion alongside a Treasury debt buyback plan. The VIX ticked up 1.75% to 15.15, still a low absolute level.

Oil's 2.60% jump on Iran rhetoric stands out against an otherwise muted, slightly risk-off tape in equity futures.

Overseas, Asian equities outperformed Europe overnight: the Nikkei 225 gained 1.2% to 66,216.79 and Hong Kong's Hang Seng added 0.8% to 25,698.49, while Germany's DAX slipped 0.62% to 25,966.59 and London's FTSE 100 fell 0.38% to 10,705.99. Gold (GC=F) is down 0.21% to $4,546.00 and the dollar index (DX-Y.NYB) is off 0.14% to 98.67, a mixed cross-asset picture that doesn't point to a single clean narrative. Bitcoin is the session's biggest mover by percentage, up 4.02% to $72,087, following President Trump's public push for Congress to pass the crypto-focused Clarity Act.
