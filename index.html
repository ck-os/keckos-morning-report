CPI Lands In Line, Core Runs a Touch Hot -- Volatility Unwinds Anyway

A relief-driven cross-asset move: falling volatility and falling oil alongside firmer equities and yields, not a single clean directional signal.
Markets Desk · Premarket

August CPI rose 0.4% month-over-month, matching the Dow Jones consensus, though core inflation (ex-food and energy) came in a bit higher than economists had penciled in, according to CNBC. The market's reaction has been one of relief rather than alarm: the VIX, which had already eased overnight, extended its drop to -4.82% and now sits at 16.98, its sharpest move on the board this morning, while equity futures firmed across the board (S&P futures +0.43%, Nasdaq futures +0.44%, Dow futures +0.48%, Russell futures +0.36%). Oracle (up 6% on a 30% revenue jump and 121% growth in cloud infrastructure revenue tied to AI cloud demand, per CNBC) is one of the few single-company stories with real market reach today, easing some of the market's recent worry about AI-spending concentration risk that had weighed on tech sentiment. The 10-year yield, however, isn't cooperating with the all-clear read -- it ticked up again to 4.95%, meaning the bond market hasn't fully priced out the hotter core print even as options-market fear has unwound.

The VIX's sharpest drop on the board and oil's steepest slide are telling two different relief stories at once -- one about inflation fear unwinding, the other about a supply rally running out of steam.

The two biggest movers in today's Glance basket are the VIX (-4.82% to 16.98) and crude oil (-3.16% to $99.24), and both trace to real, separate stories rather than one shared catalyst. The VIX's slide lines up with this morning's CPI print landing close enough to consensus to unwind pre-data hedging, even with core inflation running a touch hot. Oil's drop snaps a multi-day winning streak that had pushed both Brent and WTI above $100 amid Ukraine- and Iran-related supply disruption -- CNBC reports diesel prices hit a record above $6 a gallon on the same disruption, so today's pullback reads as a breather in that rally rather than a reversal of the underlying supply story. With CPI now behind the tape for this cycle, the next scheduled catalyst is the September 16 FOMC decision and Retail Sales report, five days out.
