U.S. equity futures are pointing modestly lower this morning, with S&P 500 futures (ES=F) off 0.25% to 7,719.75, Nasdaq futures (NQ=F) down 0.53% to 29,456.75, Dow futures (YM=F) down 0.33%, and small-cap Russell futures (RTY=F) down 0.41%. The standout move is crude oil, with WTI (CL=F) up 2.92% to $86.96, while the 10-year Treasury yield (^TNX) eased 1.13% to 4.65% -- a combination worth watching since rising oil alongside falling yields doesn't fit a single clean narrative. The VIX ticked up 2.22% to 15.22, still a low absolute level but a sign of slightly more hedging demand overnight. Gold (GC=F) is little changed, down 0.22% to $4,545.90, and the dollar index (DX-Y.NYB) is off a modest 0.12% to 98.69.

Crude oil's near-3% jump alongside a pullback in Treasury yields doesn't fit a single clean narrative this morning.

Overseas, Asian markets closed broadly higher -- the Nikkei 225 gained 1.2% to 66,216.79 and the Hang Seng added 0.8% to 25,698.49 -- while European bourses are softer, with Germany's DAX down 0.51% and London's FTSE 100 off 0.22%. Bitcoin (BTC-USD) is the session's biggest mover by percentage, up 4.06% to $72,113 after President Trump urged Congress to pass the crypto Clarity Act, a regulatory-framework bill for digital assets. Separately, bond-market commentary points to yields firming slightly as traders digest a Treasury debt buyback plan, a day after U.S. government debt was reported to have crossed $40 trillion for the first time.

Company Briefs

Walmart raises its outlook while Alibaba's AI spending squeezes profit.
Markets Desk
Walmart (WMT) --

Walmart hiked its full-year outlook ahead of its fiscal second-quarter earnings report due Thursday, saying it will use a large tariff refund to help keep prices low for consumers. The report is being watched as a read on both the health of the U.S. consumer and the so-called K-shaped economy, where higher- and lower-income spending patterns are diverging.

Alibaba (BABA) --

Alibaba's U.S.-listed shares fell roughly 3% and were volatile in premarket trading after the company posted a 75% drop in net income for the June quarter, which it attributed to heavy AI spending. The results add to a broader market question about whether current AI infrastructure investment is outrunning near-term earnings payoff across the sector.
