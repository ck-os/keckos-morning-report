Strait of Hormuz Strikes Keep the Risk-Off Trade Intact

Oil, yields, and the VIX are all moving the same direction, a broad confirmation rather than an isolated commodity story.
Markets Desk · Premarket

The U.S. and Iran exchanged strikes near the Strait of Hormuz over the weekend for the first time since July, and CNBC reports President Trump has threatened the region's strategic oil hub -- the immediate market reaction is WTI crude up 3.51% to $86.33, extending the same overnight jump that showed up in the earlier edition and, if anything, widening slightly since. That's a real supply-risk premium being priced into the world's most important chokepoint for seaborne oil, not a routine commodity wobble. The reaction has spread well beyond the oil desk: the VIX is up 6.17% to 15.32 and the 10-year yield has ticked up again to 4.73%, both moving in a direction consistent with genuine risk aversion rather than an isolated single-asset move. Equity futures are softer across the board but only modestly -- S&P futures down 0.32%, Nasdaq futures down 0.25%, Dow futures down 0.32%, Russell futures down 0.23% -- a fairly even spread across large-cap, tech, and small-cap exposure that suggests the market is treating this as a macro/energy-supply story rather than one that hits any single sector harder than another.

Oil, yields, and the VIX are all moving the same direction -- a broad risk-off signature, not a single-asset story.

The clearest mover in the Glance basket this morning is crude, up 3.51% on the fresh Strait of Hormuz strikes, with the VIX's 6.17% jump the next most notable figure -- both tied to the same geopolitical catalyst rather than separate stories. There's no scheduled US economic release today; the calendar's next entries are the September 4 jobs report and PPI/CPI in mid-September, so between now and the close the tape is left to trade on the Iran headlines and any follow-through in oil rather than a data catalyst. Gold's smaller 0.46% gain suggests the safe-haven bid is present but measured so far, more consistent with a market pricing supply risk into energy specifically than with a broader flight from risk assets.
