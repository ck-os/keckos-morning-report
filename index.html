Gold Leads, Nasdaq Lags as Traders Wait on Warsh

A cautious, event-driven tape ahead of a Fed speech, not a reaction to a surprise.
Markets Desk · Premarket

New Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh delivers his first Jackson Hole speech today, and CNBC previewed it last night as a moment prior chairs have used to lay out broad policy frameworks -- the kind of event that gets positioning ahead of it, not just a reaction after it. That positioning shows up clearest in gold, up 1.06% to $4,658.70, alongside a firmer 10-year yield at 4.67%, an unusual same-direction pairing since gold and yields more often move opposite each other; here it reads less like a classic flight-to-safety trade and more like traders hedging against whatever Warsh says today while still bracing for a hawkish surprise on rates. Equity futures are mixed rather than uniformly weak: Dow futures are actually up 0.11% and Russell futures are flat, while S&P futures are down a modest 0.05% and Nasdaq futures are the laggard at -0.29%, with Nvidia, Micron, Marvell, IREN, PayPal, and Rocket Lab all named this morning as stocks in focus for the tech-led softness. That Nasdaq underperformance versus the Dow makes sense given the index's heavier concentration in the semiconductor and AI-adjacent names driving today's premarket weakness.

Gold rising alongside yields, rather than against them, is the tell that this is hedging ahead of a known event -- not a fear trade.

The most notable single mover in the Glance basket this morning is gold's push to $4,658.70, and unlike a typical safety bid it's happening while yields also firm to 4.67% -- both moves plausibly tied to the same catalyst, Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole speech today, rather than pulling in opposite directions as they normally would. There's no scheduled US economic data release today per the calendar -- the next tracked print is the September 4 jobs report -- so Warsh's remarks are effectively the only scheduled catalyst standing between now and the close, and any policy signal in the speech could move both gold and yields further in either direction. Bitcoin's 1.18% decline to $79,308 is a smaller, less clearly connected move worth flagging alongside it, though the news flow doesn't establish a direct link between crypto weakness and today's Fed-speech setup.
