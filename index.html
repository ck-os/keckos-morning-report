Oil Spikes on Hormuz Strikes, But Equities Shrug So Far

A real geopolitical shock is showing up cleanly in oil and gold, while equities register only a modest, contained pullback.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Iran said overnight it struck American vessels and oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz in retaliation for earlier U.S. strikes, and CNBC reports President Trump insists prices will fall once the conflict ends -- for now, the market is pricing the opposite. WTI crude jumped 2.16% to $95.04, and wire coverage from Benzinga flagged Brent pushing toward $100 a barrel amid the escalating exchanges. Gold, the classic geopolitical hedge, is up 1.05% to $4,439.90, and the 10-year yield has ticked up to 4.81% (+0.46%) -- a combination that reads less like a growth scare and more like a supply-shock repricing, since oil-driven inflation risk pushes yields up even as risk assets wobble. The VIX is up 1.72% to 15.99, confirming some real nervousness, but equity futures are only modestly lower -- S&P futures -0.14%, Nasdaq -0.23%, Dow -0.30%, Russell -0.35% -- suggesting markets are treating this, for now, as an energy-and-rates story rather than a broad equity risk-off event.

Oil and gold are telling a supply-shock story that equities, so far, are only partially buying into.

The clearest mover in the Glance basket this morning is oil, not equities -- WTI's 2.16% jump is the single largest percentage move on the board, directly tied to Iran's reported strikes on U.S. vessels and tankers in the Strait of Hormuz. Inside equities, small caps are underperforming (Russell futures -0.35% versus S&P futures -0.14%), consistent with a market treating higher energy costs and firmer yields as a bigger drag on smaller, more rate-sensitive names than on mega-cap indices. There's no scheduled U.S. economic release today -- the next data points on the calendar are PPI tomorrow and CPI Friday -- so between now and the close, the tape is likely to keep taking its cue from headlines out of the Gulf rather than from domestic data.
