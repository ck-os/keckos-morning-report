Crude Jumps on Iran Threat as Futures Drift Lower

Oil leads the tape higher on renewed geopolitical pressure while equity futures ease and yields cool.
Markets Desk · Premarket

U.S. equity futures are pointing modestly lower this morning, with S&P 500 futures off 0.23% to 7,721.25, Nasdaq futures down 0.51% to 29,462.75, and Dow futures down 0.30% to 53,397. The VIX is up 2.01% to 15.19, still a low absolute level but a sign of some caution creeping in. The clearest mover on the board is crude oil, up 3.03% to $87.05 a barrel after President Trump threatened an "Economic D-Day" for Iran and "tremendous" consequences for its backers, extending a pressure campaign the administration has run since April. The 10-year Treasury yield eased 1.13% to 4.65%, following a sharp pullback in bond yields Wednesday as traders digest a new Treasury debt buyback plan -- notable against the backdrop of U.S. government debt crossing $40 trillion for the first time, a figure that has more than doubled in a decade.

Crude oil is up 3.03% to $87.05 as Trump threatens an 'Economic D-Day' for Iran -- the clearest single-asset move on the board this morning.

Overseas, the picture is mixed rather than uniformly risk-off or risk-on: Japan's Nikkei 225 is up 1.2% and Hong Kong's Hang Seng is up 0.8%, while Germany's DAX is down 0.5% and London's FTSE 100 is off 0.22%. Bitcoin stands out on the upside, up 3.73% to $71,885 after Trump urged Congress to pass the crypto Clarity Act, a regulatory framework bill for the sector -- ether reportedly moved in sympathy. Gold is down a modest 0.34% to $4,540, and the dollar index is little changed, down 0.10% to 98.71. Taken together, this is a morning defined less by a single macro narrative than by two separate stories running in parallel -- a geopolitical risk premium building in oil, and a policy-driven rally in crypto -- with the broader equity tape treading water in between.

Walmart Lifts Guidance, Alibaba Slides on AI Spend

Two large-cap earnings stories are setting the tone in Company Briefs this morning.
Markets Desk
Walmart (WMT) --

Walmart hiked its full-year outlook ahead of its fiscal second-quarter earnings report Thursday, saying it will use a large tariff refund to keep prices low for consumers. The report is being watched closely as a read on both the health of the U.S. consumer and the broader K-shaped economy, where higher- and lower-income spending patterns have been diverging.

Alibaba (BABA) --

Alibaba's U.S.-listed shares fell roughly 3% in premarket trading after the company reported a 75% drop in net income for the June quarter, which it attributed to heavy AI spending. The move is a reminder that the market has grown more discerning about AI-related capital outlays, penalizing names where spending is running well ahead of near-term earnings.
