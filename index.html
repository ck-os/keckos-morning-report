Oil's 6% Slide on Saudi, Iran Signals Offsets a Cautious Equity Open

A real de-escalation story in the oil market is doing more work this morning than anything in equities, which are consolidating Monday's AI-led gains rather than extending them.
Markets Desk · Premarket

The clearest catalyst on the tape this morning is in crude, not stocks: WTI is down 5.94% to $90.09 after a report that Saudi Arabia aims to restart a critical pipeline this week, layered on top of Iran reportedly signaling it could reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days if U.S. military pressure eases, per CNBC. That's a real unwind of the war-risk premium that had been supporting oil, and it's happening alongside, not instead of, a broader risk-friendly tone: the 10-year yield has eased to 4.96% (-0.70%) and the VIX has slipped to 14.73 (-0.94%), both consistent with markets treating the Iran/Saudi headlines as good news rather than a source of new uncertainty. Equity futures are barely moved by comparison -- S&P futures +0.06%, Nasdaq futures +0.03% -- which reads as consolidation after Monday's session, when tech-sector strength (XLK +2.89% on the day, XLC +3.90%) drove what wire coverage is already calling an "AI-led rally." Meta (META, $735.43, -0.78% premarket) is pulling back slightly even after its Muse AI agent topped Apple's App Store rankings and drew a Wells Fargo price-target increase from $640 to $796 yesterday -- a case of a stock digesting a big prior move rather than reversing the story behind it.
