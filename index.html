Yields Hit 19-Year High, Williams Floats Another Hike, Stocks Extend Slide

The bond rout is the story again this morning, not a fresh shock -- and it's pulling equities down broadly rather than in one corner of the market.
Markets Desk · Premarket

The 30-year Treasury yield pushed to its highest level since 2004 this morning, CNBC reports, extending a bond-market rout that's now run at least two sessions, and New York Fed President John Williams added fuel by telling a London audience it's "reasonable" to expect another rate hike by year-end. That combination -- rising long-end yields plus a Fed official not ruling out more tightening -- is the clearest identifiable driver of this morning's equity weakness: Nasdaq futures are down 0.98%, the steepest of the four major futures contracts, while S&P futures (ES=F) are off 0.52%, Dow futures (YM=F) down a more modest 0.27%, and Russell 2000 futures (RTY=F) down 0.36%. Nasdaq's underperformance lines up with the story -- higher long-term yields disproportionately pressure the rate-sensitive, longer-duration growth and mega-cap tech names that dominate that index's weighting, and several of those names are visible in the pain this morning: Nvidia (NVDA, $223.10, -1.07%) and Broadcom (AVGO, $349.38, -1.58%) among the chip-heavy laggards, alongside Meta (META, $730.37, -1.85%) and Amazon (AMZN, $246.62, -1.06%).

Higher long-term yields disproportionately pressure the rate-sensitive, longer-duration growth names that dominate the Nasdaq's weighting.

The cross-asset picture largely confirms this is a rates story rather than a broad flight-to-safety: the VIX is up 5.20% to 15.97, a real but orderly increase in hedging demand, while gold is actually down 0.33% alongside stocks rather than climbing as a haven asset would in a genuine risk-off episode -- a pattern that reads as investors repricing the rate path, not fleeing risk outright. Oil stands out as the true outlier, up 1.74% to $93.76, a move that doesn't fit the yields narrative cleanly and is worth flagging as unusual against an otherwise coherent tape. There's no scheduled US economic data release today -- the next major prints (PCE and GDP) aren't due until September 30 -- so between now and the close, the tape's direction likely hinges on further Fed commentary and headlines out of the Trump-Xi summit underway in Washington, where China has already confirmed AI-related talks have taken place and hinted at extending the trade truce.

Boeing's Aftermarket Push, Caterpillar's Rebound Story Draw Fresh Attention

Two names are drawing real news flow this morning even as the broader tape drifts lower on yields.
Markets Desk
Boeing (BA, $199.17, -0.30% premarket) —

Boeing is expanding its 737 MAX Landing Gear Exchange Program to single-aisle jets, completing its first exchange with American Airlines -- a step the company is framing as growing its aftermarket revenue opportunity. Separately, Boeing's engineering union recommended members accept the company's latest contract offer on September 22, removing a strike risk that CEO Kelly Ortberg had called a top priority, though the company has said it still needs roughly $10 billion in cash flow to complete its turnaround and flagged that 737 production at 47 planes a month remains "not yet stable" due to a wing-shop flow issue.

Caterpillar (CAT, $796.90, -1.91% premarket) —

Caterpillar is back in focus after reporting strong results, raising its 2026 outlook, and expanding autonomous hauling technology with quarry operator Luck Stone in Virginia. The stock has pulled back sharply over the past quarter -- down roughly 23% over 90 days -- but remains up about 36% year-to-date, and the recent operational and guidance news appears to be renewing investor attention despite the near-term drawdown.
