Futures Slide as Yields and Oil Climb Together

Small caps lead a broad premarket decline while volatility, crude, and bitcoin all move higher -- not the usual single-driver story.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Equity futures are pointed lower across the board this morning, with S&P 500 futures (ES=F) down 0.66% to 7,687.75, Nasdaq futures (NQ=F) off 1.04% to 29,306.00, Dow futures (YM=F) down 0.96% to 53,041, and Russell 2000 futures (RTY=F) the weakest of the group at -1.58% to 2,997.90. The VIX has jumped 5.44% to 15.70, still a low absolute level historically but a notable one-day move higher, while the 10-year Treasury yield (^TNX) is up 0.92% to 4.70% -- a rise Treasury Secretary Bessent addressed directly this morning, telling CNBC his department's buyback operation in longer-dated securities, where yields have been surging, could total more than $4 billion. Crude oil (CL=F) is up 2.13% to $86.29, gold (GC=F) is modestly higher at $4,567.40 (+0.26%), the dollar index (DX-Y.NYB) is little changed at 98.90 (+0.10%), and bitcoin (BTC-USD) stands out with a 5.00% gain to $72,763, extending a two-day, roughly 12% surge tied to a last-minute push by President Trump and crypto executives for the Clarity Act.

Small caps and volatility moving together points to a broad risk-off tone rather than a single sector's problem.

Overseas, the picture is more mixed than the U.S. futures tape suggests: Japan's Nikkei 225 is up 1.2% to 66,216.79 and Hong Kong's Hang Seng is up 0.8% to 25,698.49, while Germany's DAX is down 0.42% to 25,983.04 and London's FTSE is roughly flat, up just 0.04% to 10,748.16. That divergence -- Asia firm, Europe soft, U.S. futures down -- combined with rising yields, rising oil, and a rising VIX all at once, argues against pinning this morning's tone on any one catalyst; it reads more like a rates-and-oil-led repricing than a single headline-driven selloff. As always, these are premarket levels on thin volume and can shift meaningfully once the cash session opens.
