Iran's Hormuz Offer Eases Oil and Volatility Premarket

A de-escalation signal out of Tehran is doing more to move the tape than any scheduled data this morning.
Markets Desk · Premarket

Iran's offer to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days and restart nuclear talks -- reported by CNBC this morning even as Houthi forces launched a new wave of missile and drone attacks on Saudi targets -- is the identifiable catalyst behind this morning's calmer tape. WTI crude is down 1.86% to $92.85 as the geopolitical risk premium that had been baked into oil prices unwinds, and that relief is showing up elsewhere too: VIX has fallen 4.02% to 15.04, within a point of its 6-month low of 14.25, while the vol curve remains in contango (VIX3M at 18.43) rather than flipping into the kind of backwardation that would signal near-term stress. Equity futures are broadly higher in response -- S&P futures +0.33%, Nasdaq futures +0.59%, Dow futures +0.29%, Russell futures +0.30% -- a fairly even move across the board rather than one index dramatically outrunning the others, consistent with this being a macro relief trade rather than a sector-specific story.

Iran's Hormuz offer is unwinding the geopolitical risk premium baked into oil, and equity futures and VIX are both confirming the relief.

Not every risk gauge is playing along, though: gold is still up 0.92% to $4,337 even as oil slides and VIX cools, a combination that doesn't fit a clean single-direction risk-on move. Part of that likely reflects overnight coverage of Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's more hawkish policy framework, which CNBC reports is pushing ahead but meeting internal resistance, and a fresh Danish intelligence warning that Russia could attack a NATO country within months -- both keeping a bid under gold even as the Iran news eases the oil-driven risk trade. There's no scheduled US economic release today; the next data of note is next Wednesday's PCE and GDP prints on September 30, so today's price action is being driven almost entirely by the geopolitical and Fed-leadership headlines rather than incoming data.

Home Depot shares are drawing scrutiny after a Simply Wall St. valuation piece argued the stock could be up to 7% overvalued given questions over gig-delivery costs, even after a 25.5% pullback over the past year that's put the spotlight on whether current cash flows justify the price.

Costco (COST, $891.90, -0.51% premarket) —

Costco topped quarterly earnings and revenue estimates, though comparable sales growth lagged expectations. The company also unveiled a $7.5 billion expansion plan, stepping up spending in fiscal 2027 to add more warehouses and expand digital delivery, backed by what it describes as resilient member spending.

Boeing (BA, $197.92, +0.58% premarket) —

Boeing shares are attracting attention amid comparisons with Lockheed Martin, with Boeing trading at a much richer 49.7x forward P/E versus Lockheed's 17.3x -- a bet on turnaround upside rather than the stability Lockheed offers. Separately, Boeing closed the prior session slightly softer than the broader market.

Meta (META, $771.68, -0.76% premarket) —

Meta's stock continues its rally, now being framed by analysts as the AI laggard-turned-leader story of the year, with KeyBanc highlighting upcoming product releases as a potential next leg of growth. Yahoo Finance notes the broader 'Magnificent Seven' cohort recently suffered its steepest one-day decline since the April 2025 tariff selloff, making Meta's resilience stand out from its mega-cap peers.

Walmart (WMT, $107.40, -0.18% premarket) —

Walmart is being mentioned alongside Costco's earnings beat in today's coverage, with commentary framing it as one of the more durably profitable large-cap retailers even as some peers face margin pressure from intensifying competition.
